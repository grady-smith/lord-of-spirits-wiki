### The Concept of an Age in Scripture

**[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-06-22.mp3#t=06:31) And so before we can get to that particular age, we have to understand the concept of an age in general.

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-06-22.mp3#t=07:02) When you see "day" with the definite article, *hayom* or "the day," that is in most cases a reference to the Day of the Lord, the Day of [[Yahweh]], when [[God]] shows up and puts everything right.

**[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-06-22.mp3#t=08:26) There's that weird phrase that gets translated in all kinds of weird, contradictory ways in English, is like "the heat of the day," right? [[God]] comes walking in "the heat of the day" or "the wind of the day" or "the cool of the afternoon." What it literally says there is *ruach hayom*, the spirit of the day. And [[God]] comes walking after Adam and Eve have transgressed, and he comes walking in the spirit of the day. So this is deliberately in the Hebrew evoking the idea of judgment.

**[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-06-22.mp3#t=10:32) You also get "the days of" fill-in-the-blank. So, "the days of Noah" is a very common one in scripture, to refer back to the days of Noah, meaning the period when Noah was alive.

**[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-06-22.mp3#t=17:17) And *aion* is usually literally translated "age." The word *aion* includes more than just the concept of a division of time. Ultimately, it has the resonance of a particular order, a particular state of things that prevails for a period of time.

**[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-06-22.mp3#t=21:02) St. Augustine really develops the way the word *saeculum* is used in his *City of [[God]]*. He uses the sort of Platonic schema of being and becoming and applies it to the city of [[God]]... and then on the other hand, on the earth, there is this constant sort of churning and turning of one *saeculum*, one order, one world, into another.

**[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-06-22.mp3#t=23:05) In some of the early modern English translations becomes "world without end."

### Kiliasm / Chiliasm

**[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-06-22.mp3#t:01:35:35) Kiliasm, as we're going to explain, is not exactly premillennialism, but Kiliasm is a category of views in early Christianity, second, third century, named from *kilioi* which means a thousand, that believed that that Messianic age was in the future.

**[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-06-22.mp3#t:01:38:48) They see affirming Kiliasm, affirming this future material Messianic age in this world, as being the key to rejecting [[Gnosticism]], the key to establishing the justice of [[God]], the truth of [[God]]'s promises in the same kind of way that the [[Pharisees]] did.
