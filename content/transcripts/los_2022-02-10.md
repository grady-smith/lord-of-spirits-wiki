## Episode Summary

[[Fr. Andrew Stephen Damick]] and [[Fr. Stephen De Young]] explore the biblical origins and nature of the [[Priesthood]], beginning with [[Adam]] as the archetypal [[Priest]]-king in the [[Garden of Eden]]. They trace how this integrated role was fractured after the [[FALL]], separated into distinct offices of king and [[Priest]] with [[Moses]] and Aaron, and further corrupted over time, culminating in the [[Hasmonean]] and Sadducean abuses. The episode demonstrates that [[Christ]], as the new [[Adam]], perfectly reunites these roles, establishing a new [[Priesthood]] in His Church, structured in concentric circles of holiness from the [[Bishop]] to the laity, whose collective priestly task is to offer the world to [[God]] and [[God]] to the world.

### [[Adam]] as the Archetypal [[Priest]]-King (02:50)

[Listen from 02:50](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2020-12-03.mp3#t=170)

Stephen]] begins by reviewing the concept of [[Paradise]] as a [[Temple]], drawing parallels between the creation account in [[Genesis]] 1-2 and Ancient Near Eastern [[Temple]] dedication texts. In those texts, creation often involves a deity slaying a chaos monster and building a [[Temple]], culminating in placing an image of the deity within it for the spirit to inhabit.

[Listen from 05:48](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2020-12-03.mp3#t=348)

This active role involved expanding the [[Garden of Eden]] by putting the rest of creation in order and filling it with life, thereby integrating it into [[Paradise]]. This act of expansion was [[Adam]]'s priestly offering.

[Listen from 08:31](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2020-12-03.mp3#t=511)

**[[Fr. Stephen]]:** That expansion was [[Adam]]'s offering. [[Priesthood]] is presented not as a [[Job]] or an office, but as a fundamental aspect of human nature—a "directedness of being" (telos) toward [[God]].

### Gender, [[Priesthood]], and the [[FALL]] (17:10)

[Listen from 17:10](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2020-12-03.mp3#t=1030)

[[Fr. Stephen]] explains that with the division of humanity into male and female, there was a corresponding specialization of the priestly task. Man "majors" in the task of ordering and subduing creation, while woman "majors" in the task of filling creation with life. Ideally, these roles are cooperative. The [[FALL]], as described in [[Genesis 3]], corrupted this cooperation into a dynamic of domination and conflict.

[Listen from 20:34](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2020-12-03.mp3#t=1234)

**[[Fr. Stephen]]:** It's usually... translated into English, "your desire will be for your husband but he will reign over you." And that's what that's saying is desire being sort of on the chaos and bringing life into the world side and reigning or ruling or dominating being on the order side and both of them now rather than cooperating together to create in the world, are now opposed to each other and committed to dominating each other. It's now a power dynamic.

The discussion links [[Priesthood]] directly to fatherhood. In the patriarchal era, the [[Father]] of the extended family (the clan or household) functioned as the [[Priest]], making offerings and representing his people to [[God]]. This role is not about a profession but a fundamental way of relating to [[God]] and the world. This is why, [[Fr. Stephen]] argues, the [[Priesthood]] is masculine: it is the proper, holy way to channel the masculine "energy" of ordering, preventing it from becoming tyranny and domination. Spiritual motherhood, likewise, channels the feminine energy of life-giving, preventing it from becoming chaos.

### The [[Priesthood]] Fractured: [[Moses]], Aaron, and Korah (51:28)

[Listen from 51:28](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2020-12-03.mp3#t=3088)

The episode explains how the unified role of the [[Priest]]-king was fractured. This begins with [[Moses]]. In [[Exodus 4]], after resisting [[God]]'s call, [[Moses]] fails in his priestly duty to circumcise his son. This failure leads to the [[Priesthood]] being formally separated from him and given to his brother, Aaron. From this point, the roles of leader (king) and [[High priest]] are distinct offices.

[Listen from 59:00](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2020-12-03.mp3#t=3540)

**[[Fr. Stephen]]:** What's happening here is that the [[Priesthood]] is being taken away from [[Moses]]. He still has that kind of kingly leadership role, but the [[Priesthood]] has been taken away from him and isolated with Aaron and his sons as opposed to [[Moses]] and his sons. Because his specific failing was as a [[Father]]. He failed to be a [[Priest]] for his own family, and if you can't be a [[Priest]] for your own family, then you can't be a [[Priest]] for the broader community.

This established a new structure of concentric circles of holiness:
1.  **The [[High priest]]** (from Aaron's line, later narrowed by Phinehas and Zadok)
2.  **The [[Priests]]** (from other levitical clans)
3.  **The [[Levites]]** (the rest of the tribe, serving in support roles)
4.  **The People of [[Israel]]** (the other 11 tribes)
5.  **The Nations** (the rest of the world)

In this model, [[Israel]] as a whole becomes a "kingdom of [[Priests]]" ([[Exodus 19:5-6]]) for the 70 nations of the world, interceding for them, as symbolized by the 70 bulls offered during the [[Feast of Tabernacles]]. The rebellion of Korah in [[Numbers 16]] is presented as a direct challenge to this divinely ordered structure, with Korah arguing that all [[Israel]] is holy and thus he should be able to seize the [[Priesthood]]. His destruction confirms that [[Priesthood]] is not seized but is a received function of faithfulness.

### The [[Priesthood]] Corrupted: [[Hasmoneans]] and [[Sadducees]] (1:11:06)

[Listen from 1:11:06](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2020-12-03.mp3#t=4266)

The discussion moves to the [[Second Temple period]]. The [[Hasmonean]] dynasty, after the Maccabean revolt, wrongfully usurped both the kingship and the high [[Priesthood]], reuniting them improperly. Their successors, the [[Sadducees]], were a thoroughly corrupt priestly class by the time of [[Christ]].

[Listen from 1:15:30](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2020-12-03.mp3#t=4530)

**[[Fr. Stephen]]:** They had turned the [[Temple]] into essentially a business venture. It was sort of [[Jerusalem]] [[Temple]], Inc. They did a deal with the [[Romans]] where they were allowed to collect the [[Temple]] tax... They would demand extortionate tax rates, and when people couldn't pay them, they would confiscate people's land to pay the debt. And so by the time of [[Christ]], about 70% of Judean land was owned by the [[Sadducees]], by the high priestly family.

This corruption, where the [[Priests]] were oppressing rather than serving the people, forms the immediate context for [[Christ]]'s ministry and his cleansing of the [[Temple]].

### The [[Priesthood]] Restored and Fulfilled in [[Christ]] (1:20:15)

[Listen from 1:20:15](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2020-12-03.mp3#t=4815)

[[Christ]] is the ultimate [[High priest]] and the "second [[Adam]]" who perfectly fulfills and restores the [[Priesthood]]. Because He is both fully [[God]] and fully man, He is the perfect conduit, representing [[God]] to humanity and humanity to [[God]].

[Listen from 1:22:24](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2020-12-03.mp3#t=4944)

**[[Fr. Stephen]]:** [[Christ]] is the ultimate fulfillment of, and in the sense of filling to overflowing, the role of [[Priesthood]]. He represents [[God]] to man in that He is the express image of the [[Father]]. He is [[God]]. And He presents man to [[God]] because He is truly and fully human, and our human nature is united to [[God]] in His person.

The New [[Covenant]] [[Priesthood]] in the Church mirrors and fulfills the Old [[Covenant]] structure. The [[Bishop]] is the [[Icon]] of [[Christ]] the [[High priest]], and the terms for *presbyter* (elder) and *hierus* ([[Priest]]) are reunited in the [[Christian]] [[Priest]]. The deacons and other minor orders function as the new [[Levites]]. Crucially, the entire people of [[God]]—the laity—are now the "royal [[Priesthood]]" and "holy nation" ([[1 [[Peter]] 2:9]]), serving as [[Priests]] to the world, tasked with integrating it into the life of the Church.

This [[Priesthood]] of all believers is not in opposition to the ordained [[Priesthood]] but is part of the same concentric structure, with each level serving the others and the world. The episode concludes by reframing [[Priesthood]] away from modern ideas of power or career and back to the biblical model of being a transparent conduit for [[God]]'s life, love, and grace into the world.

[Listen from 1:58:31](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2020-12-03.mp3#t=7111)

He goes into Faërie... a [[Priest]] is one who crosses into that other realm and brings something back or brings someone into it. This is the priestly function. It's to be that conduit, that person who stands in between, who's able to mediate between these two realms, between the seen and the unseen... And ultimately this is not about fairy tales... but it's about the reality that is being described in Scripture and the reality that we actually encounter in the divine services, especially the [[Divine Liturgy]] itself, the [[Eucharist]].---
