## Is Marriage Merely a Human Institution?

[04:11](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=04:11) [[Fr. Andrew]] asks if the episode is about how marriage is just a thing any two people who love each other can do. [[Fr. Stephen]] replies with a definitive "No."

[05:11](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=05:11) [[Fr. Stephen]] quotes *The Princess Bride*: "Mawwiage is what bwings us togethew today."

[05:54](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=05:54) The discussion begins with the creation of humanity. [[Fr. Stephen]] explains that humanity is created to bear the image of [[God]], which is a verbal idea—a capacity to be exercised in the world.

[07:18](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=07:18) [[Fr. Stephen]] points out that the idea in [[Genesis]] 1—that both male and female humans bear the image of [[God]] and are even the same species—was revolutionary for the late Bronze Age. He notes that even Aristotle, centuries later, considered it a bold innovation to argue that men and women are the same species, though he viewed females as "defective male humans." The Hippocratic Corpus states they are not the same species at all. This biblical text presents them as having equal value as human beings.

## Adam's Aloneness and the Creation of Woman

[12:00](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=12:00) The discussion moves to [[Genesis]] 2 and Adam's "aloneness." This is not about psychological loneliness but about the fact that there is only one of him. He cannot fulfill the commandment to "fill the earth and subdue it" by himself. Adam's naming of the animals demonstrates his authority to establish order, but he cannot fulfill the command to fill the earth with life.

[14:46](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=14:46) [[God]] says Adam needs an *Ezer Kenegdo*, a Hebrew term which [[Fr. Andrew]] says will be the name of his next band. This is traditionally translated as "help meet" or "helper suitable for him."

[16:10](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=16:10) [[Fr. Stephen]] explains the term *Kenegdo*. It has a literal sense of fitting together, like two puzzle pieces that dovetail, and also refers to the biological compatibility of male and female organisms. He clarifies that at this point, Adam is not an androgynous being but one of two necessary parts.

[18:08](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=18:08) It is commonly taught that Eve was made from Adam's "rib," but [[Fr. Stephen]] states this is not what the Hebrew or Greek text says.

<center>Father Andrew's Etymology Corner</center>

[19:51](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=19:51) [[Fr. Andrew]] explains that while English translations from as early as the 8th century have used the word "rib," the original Hebrew and the subsequent Latin Vulgate (`costam`) refer to a "side." The word is often used architecturally to describe one half of a symmetrical building. The idea is that Adam is pulled apart into two halves, which is why he goes into a death-like sleep. These two halves are then built up to be man and woman.

[24:18](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=24:18) When Adam awakens and sees the woman, he exclaims, "This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh." He means this literally. This is why a man is to leave his father and mother and "cleave" to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh. The word "cleave" itself is an interesting English word that means both to join together and to split apart.

## Marriage in the Patriarchal Narratives

[27:26](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=27:26) [[Fr. Stephen]] notes that the [[Orthodox]] wedding service references the marriages of the [[patriarchs]]: [[Abraham]] and Sarah, [[Isaac]] and Rebecca, and [[Jacob]] with his wives.

[31:25](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=31:25) The marriage of [[Isaac]] and Rebecca is presented in [[Genesis]] as the ideal. It is significant that after their arranged marriage, [[Genesis 24:67]] states that [[Isaac]] "loved Rebecca." [[Fr. Stephen]] explains that in the ancient world, people did not marry for love; the idea of romantic love was seen as a weakness or a sickness. [[Christian]] love, which involves concrete actions and self-sacrifice, is different. [[Isaac]]'s love for Rebecca demonstrates that their marriage is not just a legal arrangement but involves this active, [[Christian]]-style love.

[34:27](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=34:27) [[Isaac]] is the only [[patriarch]] who has only one wife, which is held up as the ideal. [[Abraham]]'s struggles with faithfulness to [[God]] are mirrored in his relationship with Sarah, particularly with Hagar. His story arc involves learning to be faithful to [[God]] by treating Sarah with the dignity and respect she is due.

[46:21](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=46:21) The story of [[Jacob]] and his wives (and their handmaidens) illustrates all the problems associated with [[Holy Matrimony - Polygamy|polygamy]]: rivalry between wives (Leah and Rachel), rivalry between their sons, and even incest with Reuben.

## The Mystery of Marriage in the Bible

[56:11](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=56:11) Wisdom literature and the [[prophets]] emphasize the theme of finding joy with "the wife of your youth" ([[Proverbs 5:18]]). This refers to the person one was married to at a young age in an arranged marriage, and being faithful to that person rather than seeking another later in life.

[62:06](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=62:06) [[Malachi]] chapter 2 directly addresses the men of Israel who are being unfaithful to the wives of their youth. The key verse is [[Malachi 2:15]], where [[God]] asks, "Did he not make them one, with a portion of the spirit in their union?" This verse reveals that marriage is not just a human contract but a divine action where [[God]] makes the two one and breathes a portion of His Spirit into their union. The purpose of this is to produce "godly offspring."

[1:08:47](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=1:08:47) The idea of one man and one woman is reinforced in the law. [[Deuteronomy 17:14-20]] explicitly forbids the king from multiplying wives. Lamech, a descendant of Cain in [[Genesis 4]], is the first recorded polygamist, representing a departure from [[God]]'s original intent.

[1:11:07](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=1:11:07) In [[Matthew 19:3-12]], [[Christ]] addresses the issue of divorce by pointing back to the beginning, to [[Genesis]]. He affirms that [[God]] made them male and female and that the two become one flesh. What [[God]] has joined, man cannot separate. He explains that [[Moses]] permitted divorce only because of the "hardness of their hearts," but it was not so from the beginning.

[1:19:57](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=1:19:57) [[Fr. Stephen]] discusses the disciples' reaction: "If such is the case... it is better not to marry." [[Christ]]'s response about eunuchs is not an endorsement of celibacy as superior, but a statement that if one cannot commit to the indissolubility of marriage, then it is better for them not to marry at all.

[1:40:28](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=1:40:28) In [[Ephesians 5]], [[St. Paul]] calls marriage a "great mystery," stating that it refers to [[Christ]] and the Church. This reveals the ultimate purpose of marriage: it is an image and a means of participating in [[Theosis]], the union between [[God]] and humanity.

[1:48:21](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=1:48:21) This idea of [[God]]'s relationship with His people as a marriage is a consistent theme throughout the Old Testament, particularly in [[prophets]] like [[Hosea]], [[Ezekiel]], and [[Jeremiah]], and allegorically in the [[Song of Solomon]].

[1:55:41](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=1:55:41) In the Gospels, [[Christ]] is repeatedly referred to as the bridegroom ([[Matthew 9:15]], [[Mark 2:19-20]], [[Luke 5:34-35]]). This imagery culminates in [[Revelation 19]] with the "wedding feast of the Lamb."

## Conclusion: Living the Mystery

[2:00:26](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=2:00:26) [[Fr. Stephen]] emphasizes that the nuclear family as the sole unit of society is a modern and ultimately destructive idea. The true [[Christian]] understanding of family is the extended household of the Church. Marriage is the bringing of a person into a household. This is why when people convert to Christianity, they are, in a sense, having a wedding with [[Christ]] and being brought into the household of [[God]].

[2:04:15](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=2:04:15) [[Fr. Stephen]] concludes by noting that our understanding of love has shifted over time, from an active, sacrificial concept in Christianity to a more romantic, feeling-based idea in the Renaissance, and finally to a desire-based, consumptive model in the modern era. [[Christian]] love within marriage is about choosing to do what demonstrates love, regardless of feelings.

[2:08:40](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=2:08:40) [[Fr. Andrew]] concludes the episode by reiterating the core message: marriage is not an end in itself but a means of salvation, a path to [[Theosis]]. It is animated by the [[Holy Spirit]]. The cure for a diseased marriage, and the nourishment for any marriage, is to seek out the [[Holy Spirit]] and live a life of faithfulness in [[Christ]]. By doing so, spouses serve and sacrifice for one another, participating in the life of [[God]] and shining with an angelic light.
