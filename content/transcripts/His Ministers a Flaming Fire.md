## His Ministers a Flaming Fire

### Introduction

[[Fr. Andrew Stephen Damick]] introduces the episode by quoting J.R.R. Tolkien on the nature of heaven and the Christian hope for eternal life. He contrasts this rich vision with the often flat, uninspiring modern conceptions of the afterlife, which can range from a perpetual tropical vacation to an ethereal, boring existence.

**[[Fr. Andrew]]:** [In a June 1941 letter to his son Michael, *Lord of the Rings* author and Anglo-Saxon professor J.R.R. Tolkien included this passage. He wrote this: "There is a place called heaven where the good here unfinished is completed, and where the stories unwritten and the hopes unfulfilled are continued."](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-11-13.mp3#t=01:39)

Tonight, the discussion will focus on the purpose of the Christian life and what it means to be saved, moving beyond simplistic ideas of "going to the good place." The episode will explore the active role of the saints and angels in the age to come, what they do, and how believers on earth participate in that reality now.

### Twofold Ministry of the [[Saints]] and [[Angels]]

[[Fr. Stephen De Young]] begins by building upon previous episodes that discussed the [[Divine Council - Unseen Realm]]. He explains that the angelic beings and glorified human saints who form this council have a twofold ministry: a royal function of ruling and governing creation, and a priestly function of intercession.

**[[Fr. Stephen]]:** [So there's another major element to the ministry that the saints in glory have in this age and that we hope to share in the age to come in God's kingdom. And so it's that sort of that second role that we're going to be talking about and expanding on tonight, and how those two things fit together as sort of this twofold ministry of the sons of God.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-11-13.mp3#t=05:15)

The first part of this ministry involves governing and administration, where glorified humans fill the roles left vacant by the fallen angels. The second, and the focus of this episode, is their priestly ministry.

This priestly role is primarily one of intercession, where the saints and angels bring the prayers of those on earth before [[God]]. [[Fr. Stephen]] provides several examples from Scripture and ancient Jewish literature to illustrate this concept.

1.  **[[Job]] 5:1**: When [[Job]] is suffering, his friend Eliphaz challenges him, saying, [“To which of the holy ones will you address your case?”](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-11-13.mp3#t=17:16). This implies that seeking the intercession of the "holy ones" (angels) was a known practice. The [[Septuagint]] (Greek [[Old Testament]]) translation of this verse makes it even more explicit, reading, ["To which of the holy angels will you address your case?"](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-11-13.mp3#t=18:19).

2.  **[[1 Enoch]] 15:2**: In this text, [[God]] rebukes the fallen angels ([[Watchers]]) for seeking [[Enoch]]'s intercession. [[God]] tells them that their proper role was the reverse: [“You were to intercede for men, not men for you.”](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-11-13.mp3#t=20:26). This establishes the idea that intercession for humanity is a primary angelic duty.

3.  **The Life of Adam and Eve ([[Apocalypse]] of Moses)**: This first-century Jewish text describes that as Adam and Eve are repenting, ["the sun and the moon and the stars and the angels are said to be praying and offering incense to God on their behalf."](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-11-13.mp3#t=23:09). This shows the entire created order, led by the angels, participating in intercession for humanity.

4.  **[[Revelation]] 20:4-6**: St. [[John]] sees the souls of the martyrs participating in the "first resurrection." They are seated on thrones, judging and reigning with [[Christ]], and it is explicitly stated that ["they will be priests of God and of Christ, and they will reign with him a thousand years."](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-11-13.mp3#t=33:00). Their role is both royal and priestly.

The saints in glory, therefore, are not passive. They are actively engaged in ruling creation and offering prayers for us, functioning as both kings and priests alongside [[Christ]].

### Worship vs. Veneration and Necromancy

A significant portion of the discussion addresses the common Protestant objection that asking saints to pray is a form of worship that belongs only to [[God]]. [[Fr. Andrew]] and [[Fr. Stephen]] clarify the crucial distinction between [[Worship vs Veneration]].

**[[Fr. Stephen]]:** [In the ancient world... the way you have communion with a spiritual being is through sacrifice.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-11-13.mp3#t=41:14)

- **Worship (Latria)** is defined by **sacrifice**. In the ancient world, offering a sacrifice to a deity was the primary act of worship and the means of entering into communion with that being. This is why [[St. Paul]], in [[1 Corinthians 10:16-21]], warns Christians not to eat meat sacrificed to idols, because doing so means entering into communion with demons. The ultimate act of Christian worship is the [[Eucharist]], the one sacrifice of [[Christ]], through which believers commune with [[God]].

- **Veneration (Dulia)** is the act of showing **honor**. Scripture commands us to honor parents, civil authorities, and all people ([[1 Peter 2:17]]). Honoring saints and angels by asking for their prayers, bowing before their icons, or singing hymns to them is an extension of this. It does not conflict with the worship due to [[God]] because it does not involve offering a sacrifice to them.

**[[Fr. Andrew]]:** [We don't offer the Eucharist to angels. We don't offer incense to saints. We offer those things to God alone. And so for us, it's like, well, you know, you say, well, we're worshipping saints, we're like, 'Well, no, we didn't offer them the Eucharist.' It becomes really obvious when you understand that *that's* what worship is.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-11-13.mp3#t=50:24)

This distinction is then used to explain why communicating with the saints is not necromancy.

**[[Fr. Stephen]]:** [Necromancy is not just talking to the dead. Necromancy, the way it was practiced in the ancient world... it involves sacrificial meals... It's about having communion with them.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-11-13.mp3#t=41:00)

Necromancy, or "death magic," was an attempt to control or commune with the dead through sacrificial offerings, as exemplified by Odysseus in Homer's *Odyssey*, who pours out the blood of sacrificed goats to summon the shades from [[Hades]]. Christian prayer to saints is fundamentally different: it is asking living members of [[Christ]]'s Body to pray *with* us and *for* us to the one true [[God]].

**[[Fr. Stephen]]:** [So we don't offer the Eucharist, we don't offer sacrifices to the saints. But by offering the Eucharist for ourselves and for the saints, we are in communion with each other because all of us are in communion with Christ.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-11-13.mp3#t=41:35)

### Can a Baptized Orthodox Christian be Possessed?

During the call-in segment, a listener asks if a baptized and chrismated Orthodox Christian can be demonically possessed.

**Sherry:** [Do you feel that a baptized Orthodox Christian can be possessed by demons? Not just oppressed, but actually possessed.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-11-13.mp3#t=52:26)

[[Fr. Stephen]] explains that possession does not happen accidentally. It requires an invitation, whether explicit (as in occult rituals) or implicit (through a prolonged life of sin and turning away from [[God]]).

**[[Fr. Stephen]]:** [People who get possessed... have invited it... That doesn't happen accidentally. Now, that can't happen to an Orthodox Christian, obviously, accidentally. My my qualm with just saying, 'No, that can't happen to a baptized Orthodox Christian' is, unfortunately, some of our folks think that, 'Well, if I was baptized in an Orthodox church, I'm an Orthodox Christian even if I've never seen one since,' right?](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-11-13.mp3#t=55:05)

While baptism places a seal upon a person, a life of apostasy or willful separation from the life of the Church can reopen the door to demonic influence. He compares it to "going back to Egypt," returning to the slavery from which [[Christ]] freed them. However, even in such a case, there is hope for return and reconciliation through the Church's prayers and services for receiving those who have lapsed.

### Final Thoughts

[[Fr. Andrew]] and [[Fr. Stephen]] conclude by emphasizing the communal nature of the Christian life. The saints and angels are not distant figures but active members of a single family, praying with and for believers on earth.

**[[Fr. Stephen]]:** [When I've got an icon of Christ and a candle and I light it and I pray, I'm not alone. I'm surrounded by saints from thousands of years of righteous people who have loved God as I do and loved Christ as I do... and are now there praying with me and for me. And the angels are there worshiping with me, and Christ is there with me. And so, as Christians, we're never alone.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-11-13.mp3#t=1:06:16)

This reality of being "with" the saints is the core of the practice of veneration and intercession. It is not an attempt to circumvent [[God]] but to join in the chorus of praise and prayer that fills the heavens and surrounds the throne of [[God]]. We are never alone in our struggle or our worship.
