### Can God Forgive Sins? Critiquing [[Penal Substitution]]

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-01-12.mp3#t=06:39): Part of what occasioned this phrasing was the exerting from a YouTube conversation I had by a person who I believe is a Muslim apologist of a less than a minute-long clip of me saying that God can forgive people. And this was posted as me making—me as a Christian priest making a startling admission.

This critique stems from a particular view of [[Atonement - Sacrifice]], common in some Protestant circles, which posits that God *must* punish every sin to remain just. [[Fr. Stephen]] and [[Fr. Andrew]] argue this view presents several theological problems:

1.  **It Subjects God to an External Law**: [Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-01-12.mp3#t=09:35), meaning there is something (justice) that God must be obedient to, which contradicts His sovereignty.
2.  **It Renders God Merciless**: [Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-01-12.mp3#t=13:03) If God is required to punish every sin fully, He cannot be merciful.
3.  **It Leads to Injustice**: The solution proposed by this view—that [[Christ]] is punished in our place—is neither just (an innocent person is punished) nor merciful (the punishment is still fully exacted).
4.  **It Creates a Double Standard**: [Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-01-12.mp3#t=17:44).

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-01-12.mp3#t=18:13): As shocking as it is, apparently, to at least one Muslim apologist, God can and does and always has just forgiven people their sins.

### The Purpose of the [[Torah]]

The discussion transitions to the purpose of the [[Torah]], critiquing the idea that it was given as a means to "earn salvation." [[Fr. Stephen]] and [[Fr. Andrew]] clarify that the [[Torah]] was never understood this way, either by Jewish people or in the Scriptures themselves.

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-01-12.mp3#t=25:51). It provides:
1.  **Commandments of life**: Instructions on how to live righteously and avoid sin.
2.  **A way to deal with sin**: A system of repentance and sacrifice for when sin inevitably occurs.

Therefore, "keeping the [[Torah]]" includes the act of repentance. Several figures in Scripture are described as having kept the law blamelessly, not because they never sinned, but because they followed the prescribed path of repentance when they did sin.

*   **King David**: [Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-01-12.mp3#t=29:13). This is despite David's very intentional sins of adultery and murder, because he repented, thereby fulfilling the [[Torah]]'s requirements.
*   **Gentiles**: [[St. Paul]] writes that some Gentiles kept the law without knowing it, being "a law to themselves" ([[Romans 2:14]]).
*   **[[St. Paul]]**: He describes his former life as a Pharisee as being "blameless" in terms of the law ([[Philippians 3:6]]), yet he still needed [[Christ]] for salvation.

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-01-12.mp3#t=27:16): The idea of keeping [[Torah]] is not just about not doing a whole list of bad things.

## Part 2: [[Confession]] and Community

### Forgiveness as Purification

Forgiveness in Scripture is deeply connected to the concept of purification from uncleanness. The Greek word **Agos** illustrates this multifaceted idea.

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-01-12.mp3#t=48:44): The word of the day is *agos*.
*   **Awe, reverence, or fear**, as in the "fear of the Lord."
*   **Impurity or uncleanness.**
*   **An abomination.**
*   **A sin offering or sacrifice** that removes the impurity.

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-01-12.mp3#t=51:27), where the awareness of his own uncleanness ("a man of unclean lips") instills awe and fear before the holy God.

This impurity is a "curse state," a residue of sin that must be cleansed. The sacrifices of the Old Testament were not about earning forgiveness but about this purification, with the blood of the sacrifice serving as a purificatory agent. The actual meal of the sacrifice was a celebration of communion *after* forgiveness and restitution had already taken place.

### The Communal Nature of Sin and Repentance

Sin is never a purely private matter between an individual and God; it always has communal consequences.

*   **David's Sin**: [Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-01-12.mp3#t=53:14).
*   **Zacchaeus**: [Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-01-12.mp3#t=54:48), showing that repentance requires repairing the damage done to the community.

### Binding and Loosing: The Role of the Church

The authority to "bind and loose" sins, given by [[Christ]] to His apostles, establishes the Church's role in the sacrament of forgiveness.

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-01-12.mp3#t=59:54): "Truly I say to y'all, whatever y'all bind on earth shall be bound in heaven, and whatever y'all loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven" ([[Matthew 18:18]]).

This authority is communal and ecclesial. The New Testament pattern for confession is not private prayer alone but confession within the community of the Church.

*   **[[James]] 5:16**: "Therefore, confess your sins to one another and pray for one another, that you may be healed."
*   **[[Acts]] 19:18**: "Also many of those who were now believers came, confessing and divulging their practices."

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-01-12.mp3#t=01:00:23): The biblical pattern is public confession within the church community. There's not a single example of someone confessing their sins to God privately in the New Testament.

The modern practice of confessing to a priest is a continuation of this, with the priest acting as a witness and representative of the entire Church community.

### Excommunication and Anathema as a Call to Repentance

The act of "binding" sins, or excommunication, is not a final condemnation but a severe form of spiritual medicine intended to bring about repentance.

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-01-12.mp3#t=01:07:38): When [[St. Paul]] prescribes putting a man out of the church in Corinth, he says to hand him over to Satan "for the destruction of the flesh, for the salvation of his soul" ([[1 Corinthians 5:5]]). This is being put back into the world, under the influence of the evil one, to be afflicted and hopefully brought to repentance.

Anathemas are similarly not about eternal damnation but are a solemn declaration that a teaching or a person who unrepentantly promotes it is outside the Church. It is a final, urgent call for them to return.

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-01-12.mp3#t=01:10:28): When we anathematize the Arians, if you are an Arian, there's a solution for that: repent of [[Arianism]]. Now you're not an Arian anymore, and you're not accursed.

## Part 3: The Practice and Purpose of [[Confession]]

### The Church as a Hospital, Not a Courtroom

The entire framework for confession, sin, and forgiveness should be understood therapeutically, not juridically. The Church is not a courtroom where punishments are handed out, but a hospital where souls are healed.

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-01-12.mp3#t=01:21:55): As [[St. John Chrysostom]] says, the church is not a courtroom, it's a hospital.

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-01-12.mp3#t=01:23:49). It is part of the "treatment plan"—a set of actions, prayers, or readings designed to help a person make restitution and form new, virtuous habits.

### [[Confession]] as the Cure for Isolation

In a time of increasing social isolation, the ecclesiology of the Church, particularly the sacrament of confession, provides a powerful antidote.

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-01-12.mp3#t=01:28:16): The second one [experience of confession], calls you out of yourself in a way that the first one simply doesn't... [[Confession]] is one of those key ways that we participate [in the Body of [[Christ]]].

Confessing sins within the community breaks the illusion that one is uniquely wicked and alone in their struggles. It restores the individual to the Body of [[Christ]], where healing and forgiveness are found. To refuse to participate in the life of the Church, including confession, is a form of self-excommunication.

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-01-12.mp3#t=01:36:45): Joylessness is not a sign of holiness. [[St. Paul]] says the exact opposite. He says joy is one of the fruits of the Spirit... you should leave confession feeling 50 pounds lighter.
