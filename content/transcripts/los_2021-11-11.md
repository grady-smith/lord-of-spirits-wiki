## The Identity of the [[Angel of the Lord]]

### Linguistic Breakdown

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=02:20)

1.  **"The"**: The Hebrew uses the definite article (*ha*), indicating a specific, singular being. This is not just "an" angel of the Lord but *the* [Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=04:34), but the Hebrew phrase *ha-mal'akh [[Yahweh]]* consistently points to one particular being.

2.  **"Angel"**: The Hebrew word *mal'akh* and the Greek word *angelos* both mean "messenger." It describes a job or a role, not a species. In the ancient world, this term was used for spiritual beings who served pagan gods as well, such as in [Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=05:34)

3.  **"of the Lord"**: This refers to [[Yahweh]], the [[God]] of Israel. The phrasing can be interpreted in two ways:
    *   **Possessive**: "[[Yahweh]]'s angel," meaning an angel who belongs to or serves [[Yahweh]].
    *   **Descriptive/Attributive**: "The [Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=09:27) This second interpretation suggests that the [[Angel of the Lord]] is a divine figure.

## The Narrative Arc of the [[Angel of the Lord]]

[[Fr. Andrew]] and [[Fr. Stephen]] outline a narrative arc for the [[Angel of the Lord]], showing him primarily as a military commander who leads [[God]]'s people, protects them, and executes judgment.

### The Commission ([[Exodus]] 23)

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=50:52) This Angel is unique because [[God]] says, "My name is in him," signifying that the Angel carries [[God]]'s own identity and authority. Israel is commanded to obey him, as he will not pardon their transgression. This mission is explicitly one of conquest—a *gigantomachy*—to blot out the pagan giant clans like the Amorites and Hittites by destroying their religious practices and altars.

### The Confrontation at Jericho ([[Joshua]] 5)

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=1:09:25) [[Joshua]] encounters a man with a drawn sword and asks if he is for Israel or their adversaries. The figure responds, "No; but I am the commander of the army of the Lord. Now I have come."

This encounter contains three key identifiers linking this figure to the [[Angel of the Lord]] and to [[Yahweh]] Himself:
1.  **The Drawn Sword**: This imagery connects him to his appearances to Balaam and [[David]].
2.  **Receiving Worship**: [[Joshua]] falls on his face and worships, and the figure does not correct him, unlike created angels in other parts of Scripture.
3.  **Holy Ground**: The commander tells [[Joshua]], "Take off your sandals from your feet, for the place where you are standing is holy." This is the exact command given by the [[Angel of the Lord]]/[[God]] from the burning bush in [[Exodus 3]].

### The Departure ([[Judges]] 2)

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=1:15:35) The [[Angel of the Lord]] ascends from Gilgal to Bochim and confronts Israel for their disobedience and idolatry. He speaks in the first person, identifying himself as the one who brought them out of [[Egypt]] and made a covenant with them. Because they have broken the covenant, he declares, "I will not drive them out before you, but they shall become thorns in your sides, and their gods shall be a snare to you." With his departure as their active commander, the period of the [[Judges]] begins, characterized by a cycle of sin, oppression, repentance, and deliverance. His departure is an act of mercy, as their continued rebellion in his direct presence would have led to their destruction.

## Conclusion

The figure of the [Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=1:20:45) This is not a contradiction to the humble, suffering [[Christ]] of the Gospels. Rather, it reveals the fullness of His identity. The same one who could command legions of angels and destroy armies with a word chose to offer himself in humility for the salvation of the world. Understanding this eternal reality relativizes our day-to-day struggles and successes, pointing us toward our true identity—who we are becoming in [[Christ]] for eternity.
