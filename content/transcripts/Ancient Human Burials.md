## Part 1: Prehistoric and Ancient Near Eastern Burial

### Neanderthal Burials

[Listen from 09:02](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lord_of_spirits_2024-01-11.mp3#t=542)
[[Fr. Stephen]] begins by discussing three famous Neanderthal burial sites, all dated to roughly 50,000 BC.

1.  **Shanidar Cave (Northern Iraq):** [Listen from 12:24](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lord_of_spirits_2024-01-11.mp3#t=744) This site is notable for the sheer number of burials found within it (at least 13 skeletons), indicating it was a repeatedly used burial location.
2.  **La Chapelle-aux-Saints (France):** [Listen from 13:46](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lord_of_spirits_2024-01-11.mp3#t=826) This site contains a skeleton of an extremely elderly Neanderthal with advanced rheumatoid arthritis, buried in a pit deliberately dug out of limestone. The effort involved suggests this person was an elder held in high esteem.
3.  **Kebara Cave (Palestine):** [Listen from 21:38](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lord_of_spirits_2024-01-11.mp3#t=1298) Here, a headless skeleton was found deliberately buried with its arms folded across its chest. The hyoid bone was intact, which is significant because this delicate bone in the throat is rarely preserved. Analysis of the hyoid bone suggests that [[Neanderthal - Early Humans|Neanderthals likely had very high-pitched, even shrill voices]].

[Listen from 15:16](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lord_of_spirits_2024-01-11.mp3#t=916)
A key feature of these sites is the presence of "grave goods," such as flint tools and worked animal bones. There is academic debate over whether these items were personal possessions buried with the deceased or items intended for use in the afterlife. The latter interpretation implies a belief in an afterlife.

### Early Homo Sapiens Burials

[Listen from 25:57](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lord_of_spirits_2024-01-11.mp3#t=1557)
[[Fr. Stephen]] moves on to early Homo sapiens burials, which are significantly older than the Neanderthal sites discussed.

1.  **Qafzeh Cave (Palestine):** Dated to around 100,000 BC, this cave contains 15 burials of early Homo sapiens. The grave goods found here are more decorative, including shells and small animal bones likely used for jewelry or clothing adornment. A tub of red ochre, a pigment, was also found, suggesting ritual use.
2.  **Panga ya Saidi (Kenya):** [Listen from 29:32](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lord_of_spirits_2024-01-11.mp3#t=1772) Dated to around 84,000 BC, this is the burial of a toddler. The child was laid to rest with a pillow under its head and wrapped in a cloth or shroud. [[Fr. Stephen]] notes how "profoundly human" this act is, showing a tender care for the departed child that transcends time.

[Listen from 30:26](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lord_of_spirits_2024-01-11.mp3#t=1826)
There are commonalities and differences between these groups: Neanderthals were buried on their backs inside caves, while early Homo sapiens were buried in the fetal position on their side, at the entrance to caves. The existence of these shared, specific ritual practices across vast geographical areas points to a common, widespread culture and is one of the pointers toward a belief in early monotheism.

### Later Ancient Near East Burials

*   **Sumeria:** [Listen from 01:00:00](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lord_of_spirits_2024-01-11.mp3#t=3600) Regular people were buried in the floors of their homes or in a family courtyard, wrapped in reed mats. The wealthy were buried in large pits with brick vaults. Their slaves were often poisoned, posed in positions of service, and buried with them, along with elaborate grave goods for the afterlife. Drink offerings (libations) of wine and honey were common.
*   **Egypt:** [Listen from 01:01:20](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lord_of_spirits_2024-01-11.mp3#t=3680) Egyptian religion was heavily focused on funerary rites. Mummification began around 3600 BC and involved removing soft tissues and desiccating the body to preserve it. Pyramids were tombs for Pharaohs, dating to the Old Kingdom and ending around 1550 BC, well before the time of the Hebrew sojourn in Egypt. Human sacrifices also occurred at burials.
*   **Levant/Syria (Nomadic Groups):** [Listen from 01:04:26](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lord_of_spirits_2024-01-11.mp3#t=3866) Nomadic peoples used generational cave tombs, like the Cave of Machpelah purchased by Abraham ([[Genesis 23]]). These sites were revisited for subsequent burials, and the presence of oil lamps indicates they were places of ongoing veneration or ritual.
*   **Assyria:** The first hints of cremation in the Ancient Near East appear in the Assyrian Empire among minority ethnic groups.
*   **Greece:** [Listen from 01:07:15](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lord_of_spirits_2024-01-11.mp3#t=3035) The process involved a viewing of the body at home, a funeral procession with mourners and sacrificial animals, and then burial. Sacrifices were offered at the gravesite, and the deceased were buried with a final portion of the sacrificial meal. This practice shows a shift towards seeing departed ancestors as semi-divine spirits receiving ongoing offerings. This is also the origin of placing a coin (obol) under the tongue to pay Charon the ferryman for passage across the River Styx.
*   **Rome:** [Listen from 01:10:50](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lord_of_spirits_2024-01-11.mp3#t=4250) Roman practices were similar to Greek, but with unique elements. During the funeral procession, mourners wore masks of the family's ancestors, embodying them to welcome the newly deceased. Cremation became the norm, reflecting a greater disconnect between the person's identity and their physical body. Burials took place outside the city, as the burial sites were considered sacred spaces, in contrast to the common (profane) space of the city. This changed with St. Constantine, when [[Romans]] shifted back to burial.

### The Orthodox Funeral and Burial

[Listen from 01:02:04](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lord_of_spirits_2024-01-11.mp3#t=3724)
[[Fr. Stephen]] and [[Fr. Andrew]] describe the traditional Orthodox Christian burial practices, which have their roots in [[Second Temple]] Jewish traditions.

*   **The Vigil:** Traditionally, a vigil is kept with the body of the deceased, during which the Psalter is read. This is an act of prayer, spiritual warfare, and keeping company with the departed.
*   **Preparation of the Body:** The body is washed, clothed in a baptismal robe or other special clothing, and placed in the coffin. A traditional burial shroud may be used. The body is placed in the church facing the altar, as they are still part of the worshipping community. It is an open casket so that the body, as an icon of [[Christ]], can be venerated.
*   **Clergy Burials:** [Listen from 01:12:30](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lord_of_spirits_2024-01-11.mp3#t=4350) Clergy are buried in their vestments. Deacons are buried holding a censer, while priests and bishops are buried holding a Gospel book, and the aer (the large veil covering the chalice and diskos) is placed over their face. A bishop is buried facing the people, as he does when celebrating the [[Liturgy]].
*   **The Funeral Service and Procession:** The funeral service is explicitly about both death and [[Resurrection]]. It confronts the reality of death ("I am a lament and a weeping") while proclaiming the hope of the resurrection. After the service, there is a procession to the cemetery.
*   **The Burial:** The gravesite is blessed, and the body is buried facing east, awaiting the second coming of [[Christ]].
*   **Memorials:** [Listen from 01:14:43](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lord_of_spirits_2024-01-11.mp3#t=4483) Memorial services (Panikhidas) are held on the 3rd, 9th, and 40th days after death, and then annually. These days traditionally correspond to significant points in the soul's journey after death. The hymn "Memory Eternal" is sung, which is a prayer for God to remember the person, not for humans to remember them. A dish called *koliva* (boiled wheat) is prepared, symbolizing the resurrection.

### Final Thoughts

[Listen from 01:16:15](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lord_of_spirits_2024-01-11.mp3#t=4575)
[[Fr. Stephen]] reflects on the continuity seen in human burial practices from the earliest Neanderthals to modern Christians. The loving care shown to the dead, even 84,000 years ago, is a testimony to the fundamental human truth that "love is more powerful than death." This truth is fully revealed in [[Christ]]'s resurrection. Cremation is a rejection of this deep-seated human understanding of the body and its importance.

[Listen from 01:21:40](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lord_of_spirits_2024-01-11.mp3#t=4900)
[[Fr. Andrew]] speaks about the power of parish burial ministries. In a world that is increasingly "excarnated" and virtual, the hands-on, physical act of caring for the dead is a powerful witness. It's an evangelistic act that shows the community's belief in the [[Resurrection]] and their love for the departed. It provides a "haven" for souls ground down by modern disembodied reality.

[[Fr. Stephen]] concludes by returning to the image of the family in Kenya burying their toddler. [Listen from 01:26:35](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lord_of_spirits_2024-01-11.mp3#t=5195)
> When they did that, they testified to the fact that love is more powerful than death. And I don't know how they could have intellectually known how that was true, because it was only really revealed how that was true millennia later when [[Christ]]'s love for us brought Him to the most horrible death humans could devise, and then out the other side in the resurrection. But that truth was in a shadowy way real to them nonetheless.
