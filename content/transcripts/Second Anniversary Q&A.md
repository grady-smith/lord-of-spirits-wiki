## Core Theological Concepts

### [[The Fall of Man]].md

## Second Anniversary Q&A

-   [A caller named Jim asks if it is possible for angels or glorified saints to fall again in the future.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-09-28.mp3#t=09:25) [[Fr. Stephen]] explains that redeemed humans and angels will not fall again, but for different reasons. He clarifies that Adam was created in a state of "innocence and immaturity," not perfection, which made him "mutable" and able to fall. In contrast, the saints are described as "the spirits of the righteous made perfect," having completed their journey and reached their *telos*, a state different from Adam's original condition.
-   [A follow-up caller, Celeste, asks how Adam and Eve could choose to sin if the will to choose evil is a defect resulting from the fall.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-09-28.mp3#t=54:36) [[Fr. Stephen]] distinguishes between the "natural will" which propels towards the good, and the "gnomic will," a second movement of will that emerges after the fall to deal with indecision. He explains, "[Adam and Eve were able to sin is related to that fact that they were innocent and immature... their nous, their ability to perceive what is truly good, was undeveloped and therefore able to be mistaken, able to be fallible.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-09-28.mp3#t=58:00)" [[God]]'s command was a protection for them in their immaturity. After the fall, the human *nous* is even more clouded, "cracked and covered in mud," making correct discernment of the good much more difficult without divine grace.

### [[Divine Council - Unseen Realm]].md

## Second Anniversary Q&A

### [[Biblical Canon - Apocrypha]].md

## Second Anniversary Q&A

-   [A young caller named Ezra asks why Protestant Bibles removed the "extra books" (deuterocanonical books/apocrypha) that are present in Orthodox and Roman Catholic Bibles.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-09-28.mp3#t=42:24) [[Fr. Stephen]] clarifies that the books weren't "taken out" in a simple sense, but that different Christian communities inherited different Old Testament canons based on what local Jewish communities were using.
-   [He explains, "In the early Church, different Christian communities in different parts of the world received an Old Testament that was based on what the Jewish communities were using in that part of the world... In Greek-speaking communities, they received a Greek Old Testament that had books in it that were not necessarily part of the Hebrew Old Testament."](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-09-28.mp3#t=46:07) The debate over which canon to use was primarily a Western European phenomenon, which the Roman Catholic Church officially settled for itself at the Council of Trent in the 16th century, while the Orthodox Churches have continued to use the canons they received from antiquity.
