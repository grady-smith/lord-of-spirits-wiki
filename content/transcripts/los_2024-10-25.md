# Halloween 2021: Demons and the Unseen Realm

## Introduction

This is the annual [Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-10-25.mp3#t=171).

The central thesis of the episode is that in the ancient Christian understanding, demons are not fallen angels themselves, but rather the disembodied spirits of the dead [[Nephilim - Giants]], the offspring of the fallen angels and human women. The opposite of a demon is not an angel, but a saint.

## The Greek and Roman Understanding of [[Demons]]

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-10-25.mp3#t=447). These were seen as spiritual beings who divided up lots, fates, and destinies. In Latin, the related concept is the *genius*, a spirit associated with a person, place, or people group, from the root *genē* (to beget or produce offspring).

According to ancient Greek writers like Hesiod, the *daemones* (demons) were originally the spirits of the dead heroes from the golden age. [Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-10-25.mp3#t=883). These spirits were thought to wander the earth, and shrines were built to them next to temples to placate them and keep them from causing trouble. This idea of placating angry, wandering spirits was a common feature of paganism. Ruined cities were considered haunted and dangerous because the local gods and demons were no longer being worshiped and were therefore angry. [Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-10-25.mp3#t=1300).

These beings occupied a realm between gods and humans. They were primarily "energetic" beings, meaning their influence was experienced rather than being physically seen or directly interacted with in most cases. The original meaning of "demonic possession" was simply to have a demon associated with you, which, as in the case of Socrates's *daimonion*, was not always considered a negative thing. [Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-10-25.mp3#t=1403).

## St. Augustine, Demon Sex, and the Great Shift

In the 5th century, there was a shift in interpretation, most famously represented by St. Augustine. He and others began to interpret the "sons of [[God]]" in [[Genesis]] 6 as the righteous line of Seth, not angels. However, even in rejecting the literal angel-human procreation, St. Augustine did not reject the reality of sexual interaction between demons and humans.

This demonstrates that even the later Fathers who reinterpreted [[Genesis]] 6 still held to a very real and interactive view of demons.

## Conclusion: The Opposite of a Demon is a Saint

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-10-25.mp3#t=4042). A demon is the spirit of a dead human (a [[Nephilim]]) who has given themselves over to evil and is now bound to tormenting others. A saint is a human who has united themselves to [[God]] through [[Christ]] and now lives in eternal glory, interceding for and helping humanity. This reframes the spiritual battle not just as a conflict between humanity and external evil forces, but as the ultimate choice each human soul must make.

The exorcisms performed at baptism are a direct confrontation with this reality. They cast out the demonic spirit that may be yoked to a person's life and replace it with a Guardian Angel, who guides and protects the person on their path to holiness.
