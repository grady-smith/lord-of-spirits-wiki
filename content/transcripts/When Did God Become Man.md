# When Did God Become Man?

## Introduction

[[Fr. Andrew]] begins by quoting 1 [[Enoch]], setting the stage for a discussion on the spiritual realm. The episode's central theme is introduced: understanding the incarnation of [[Christ]] in light of the bodily experiences of God in the [[Old Testament]]. [Listen from 03:13](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-12-12.mp3#t=03:13) They tackle the question of how the [[Son of God]] could have a physical body in Eden or with [[Abraham]] before the Annunciation, and whether this contradicts the doctrine that He became man only 2000 years ago.

## Christological Heresies: Docetism and Adoptionism

[[Fr. Stephen]] begins by explaining that early Christological heresies help define orthodox [[Christology]] by showing what the Church asserted in response.

**Docetism**: [Listen from 16:01](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-12-12.mp3#t=16:01) Derived from the Greek word *dokeo* ("to seem"), this was arguably the first Christological heresy, prominent at the end of the 1st century. It taught that [[Jesus]] of Nazareth only *appeared* to be a human person. This heresy arose not as a developed system but within actual communities, which is why St. [[Ignatius of Antioch]] so strongly refutes them. These groups were often Jewish Christians who kept the [[Torah]] strictly, which has confused modern scholars who associate this physicality-denying heresy with [[Gnosticism]]. [[Fr. Stephen]] proposes a simpler solution: these Jewish-Christian groups understood the appearances of God in the [[Old Testament]] as theophanies and viewed [[Jesus]] as another theophany, rather than a truly incarnate human being.

> **[[Fr. Stephen]]**: "It seems to me a much simpler solution that these Docetists are groups of Jewish Christians who believed that [[Jesus]] of Nazareth was a theophany, like the [[Old Testament]] theophanies, and not an actual human being." [Listen from 41:13](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-12-12.mp3#t=41:13)

**Adoptionism**: [Listen from 11:54](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-12-12.mp3#t=11:54) This is a broader category of heresies teaching that [[Jesus]] was a man who was later divinized or "adopted" as the [[Son of God]] at a certain point, such as his baptism or resurrection. Cerinthianism is an early example, which taught that the divine [[Logos]] attached itself to the man [[Jesus]] at his baptism.

## Scripture's Witness to [[Christ]]'s Flesh

[[Fr. Andrew]] and [[Fr. Stephen]] review numerous [[New Testament]] passages that explicitly emphasize the physicality and flesh of [[Jesus Christ]], often in direct opposition to docetic ideas.

*   **[Luke 24:39](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-12-12.mp3#t=44:44)**: The resurrected [[Christ]] proves His bodily reality by showing his hands and feet, stating, "a spirit does not have flesh and bones as you see that I have."
*   **[John 1:14](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-12-12.mp3#t=46:43)**: "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us."
*   **[Acts 2:31](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-12-12.mp3#t=47:04)**: His flesh did not see corruption.
*   **[Romans 1:3](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-12-12.mp3#t=47:37) and [Romans 9:5](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-12-12.mp3#t=48:04)**: Paul emphasizes [[Christ]]'s descent from [[David]] "according to the flesh."
*   **[1 [[John]] 4:2](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-12-12.mp3#t=50:17) and [2 [[John]] 1:7](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-12-12.mp3#t=50:36)**: St. [[John]] makes confessing that [[Christ]] has come "in the flesh" the very test for discerning true spirits from false ones, directly countering the docetic communities he was dealing with.

The consistent scriptural emphasis on "flesh" is significant because "flesh" often has negative connotations in the [[New Testament]], referring to sinful desires. The apostles' deliberate use of this word underscores the reality of the [[Incarnation]] against those who would deny it.

## The Body of God in the [[Old Testament]]

The discussion then pivots to the [[Old Testament]], where the [[Son of God]] appears in a physical, material form long before the Annunciation.

*   **[Genesis 3:8](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-12-12.mp3#t=75:42)**: God is heard "walking in the garden," which implies a physical presence making sound.
*   **[Genesis 18:1-8](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-12-12.mp3#t=83:31)**: The Lord appears to [[Abraham]] with two angels. They have their feet washed, they rest under a tree, and they eat a meal prepared for them. This is not a mere vision; it is a physical interaction.
*   **[Exodus 24:9-11](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-12-12.mp3#t=76:36)**: [[Moses]], Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and 70 elders "saw the God of [[Israel]]," and there was something "under his feet." They also "beheld God, and ate and drank."
*   **[1 [[Samuel]] (1 Kingdoms) 3:10](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-12-12.mp3#t=81:10)**: "And the Lord came and stood, calling as at other times, '[[Samuel]], [[Samuel]].'"
*   **[Jeremiah 1:9-10](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-12-12.mp3#t=82:38)**: "Then the Lord put out his hand and touched my mouth."

These passages show a consistent portrayal of God (specifically, the Son) interacting with the world in a body with tangible, material properties.

## Reconciling the [[Incarnation]] with Time and Eternity

How can [[Christ]] have a body in the [[Old Testament]] if He only "became man" at a specific point in history? The key is understanding that [[Christ]]'s humanity, once assumed, is not bound by linear time in the same way ours is.

> **[[Fr. Stephen]]**: "Humanity exists in time and space... Therefore, if [[Christ]] takes upon himself our humanity, our human nature, that means de facto, he's going to have a birth... he's going to live... and he died a human death... But humanity also exists outside of time and space." [Listen from 1:16:44](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-12-12.mp3#t=1:16:44)

The resurrected humanity of [[Christ]] is not constrained by the normal limits of space and time. This is why He can appear and disappear, walk through doors, and why St. [[John]] can see himself enthroned as one of the 24 elders in [[Revelation]]. This reality outside of time also applies retrospectively.

> **[[Fr. Stephen]]**: "Those feet can walk in the garden... The Lamb was slain from the foundation of the world. [[Christ]]'s self-offering on the cross... this is the basis for the creation of everything in the universe, and this is the purpose of the creation of everything in the universe." [Listen from 1:31:07](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-12-12.mp3#t=1:31:07)

The [[Incarnation]] is not "plan B" after the Fall. It is the central purpose of all creation. The same body that walked in Eden, ate with [[Abraham]], and was taken from the [[Theotokos]] is the one that was crucified, resurrected, and ascended. This does not mean God changes; the unchangeable divine person of the Son takes on human nature without alteration to His divinity.

## Conclusion: Awe Before the Mystery

The episode concludes not with a simple formula, but with an invitation to awe. Understanding these truths about the [[Incarnation]] should lead us beyond intellectual puzzles to a state of wonder and worship.

> **[[Fr. Stephen]]**: "I think there's also an important place for moving past all that work and all that toil... for awe directed toward God. For awe and admiration directed toward our Lord [[Jesus Christ]] for who He is and what He has done for us... Just being in rapt awe for a while. That's what I hope thinking about some of the things... that we talked about in this episode... that's the effect I hope it will have." [Listen from 1:51:22](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2024-12-12.mp3#t=1:51:22)

The [[Incarnation]] is the reality through which God creates, reveals Himself, and shows His love. It is the center of everything, and our salvation is found in entering into the life of the incarnate [[Christ]].
