Stephen]] explains that pain in childbirth is a direct consequence of [[The Fall of Man]].](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits_thanksgiving_qa.mp3#t=41:47:00) The Theotokos's painless birth signifies that her Son, [[Jesus Christ]], was born outside of this curse. [[Christ]] was not subject to the curse but voluntarily chose to take its effects upon Himself, including suffering and death, for the sake of humanity's salvation. Therefore, the painless birth is not about denigrating the female body, but about affirming [[Christ]]'s unique status as being free from the curse of the Fall from the moment of His conception.

### Worship vs Veneration
## Asynchronous Q&A on Patriotism, Catechesis, and the Lord's Prayer
[A caller named Eric asks about liturgical language that says "we worship Thy Cross" or "we worship Thy Resurrection."](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-11-23.mp3#t=44:40:00) He notes the potential confusion with idolatry and asks why the Cross is treated differently than other objects [[Christ]] touched.

[[Fr. Andrew]] explains that this is an issue of translation and the evolving meaning of the English word "worship." He states that the original Greek term is *proskynesis* (*proskynoumen*), the same word used for veneration. Historically, the English word "worship" simply meant to show worth or honor, as seen in phrases like a judge being called "Your Worship" or the Anglican wedding vow "with my body, I thee worship."

[Fr. Andrew notes that while the Greek makes a technical distinction between *proskynesis* (veneration) and *latria* (worship reserved for God), the English translation can be ambiguous.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-11-23.mp3#t=49:15:00) However, the act of "worshipping" the Cross is not offering sacrifice to it as if it were a god. It is a higher level of veneration given to an object intimately connected to [[Christ]]'s saving work. [[Fr. Stephen]] adds that the terms can also refer to events or divine [[energies]] (like the Passion or [[Resurrection]]), not just physical objects, which are inseparable from the person of [[Christ]].

### The Fall of Man

[Fr. Stephen explains the respective curses on Adam and Eve.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-11-23.mp3#t=42:40:00) Adam's task of ordering creation would become difficult (working by the "sweat of your brow"), and Eve's task of filling the earth with life would become painful. The fact that the Theotokos gave birth without pain is a theological statement that [[Christ]] was not born under the curse of the Fall. This highlights [[Christ]]'s voluntary assumption of suffering and death, rather than being subject to it by necessity as a consequence of fallen human nature.

### [[Priesthood]] and [[Tabernacle]]
## Asynchronous Q&A on Patriotism, Catechesis, and the Lord's Prayer
[Two callers, Christy and Johannes, ask for clarification on a previous episode's discussion about obedience to a spiritual father, referencing a quote from St. Silouan about obeying even if told to sin.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-11-23.mp3#t=05:47:00)

[Fr. Stephen clarifies the structure of authority in the Orthodox Church, stating, "priests have no authority."](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-11-23.mp3#t=14:09:00) All authority a priest has is delegated from his bishop. Therefore, if a priest teaches something questionable, it is "perfectly acceptable" to ask the bishop for clarification. If a bishop commands a priest to do something against his conscience, the priest's only recourse is to request release or laicization, not to rebel. This same principle applies to the relationship between a layperson and their spiritual father: the relationship is based on trust, and if that trust is broken, one finds a new spiritual father rather than second-guessing every piece of guidance. The danger is in refusing to have a spiritual father at all, thereby denying oneself the benefits of spiritual guidance.
