## The Blood that Cries Out

[[Fr. Andrew]] introduces the main topic of the episode: martyrdom. He asks whether it is simply being killed for one's beliefs or something deeper.

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2026-04-27.mp3#t=05:08): "I mean, you know, martyrdom, is it is it just getting killed for what you believe in, or is or is there something deeper going on here? I mean, we all know that when you hear a rhetorical question like that, that the answer is always, yes, of course, there's something deeper going on."

[[Fr. Stephen]] begins the theological discussion with the story of Cain and Abel in [[Genesis]], establishing the theme of innocent blood crying out from the ground.

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2026-04-27.mp3#t=10:56), quoting [[Genesis]] 4:10-11: "What have you done? The voice of your brother's blood is crying to me from the ground. And now you are cursed from the ground, which has opened its mouth to receive your brother's blood from your hand."

[[Fr. Stephen]] explains that this language is not merely poetic but introduces a scriptural theme: the blood of the innocent cries out to [[God]], demanding justice and a reckoning. He then connects this to the prohibition against consuming blood given to Noah after the flood.

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2026-04-27.mp3#t=16:15), quoting [[Genesis]] 9:4-6: "But you shall not eat flesh with its life, that is, its blood. And for your lifeblood, I will require a reckoning. From every beast, I will require it, and from man, from his fellow man, I will require a reckoning for the life of man. Whoever sheds the blood of man, by man shall his blood be shed, for [[God]] made man in his own image."

He explains that in the Hebrew scriptures, blood is directly connected to life and the soul. The shedding of innocent blood disrupts the created order and requires a reckoning to restore justice. This concept is so strong that even an animal that kills a human will face a reckoning. The ultimate reason for this is that humanity is made in the image of [[God]].

## [[Atonement]] and Purification

The discussion then moves to how the sacrificial system in the [[Torah]] addresses this problem of shed blood.

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2026-04-27.mp3#t=27:11), quoting [[Leviticus]] 17:11-12: "For the life of the flesh is in the blood, and I have given it for you on the altar to make atonement for your souls, for it is the blood that makes atonement by the life. Therefore, I have said to the people of Israel, 'No person among you shall eat blood, neither shall any stranger who sojourns among you eat blood.'"

[[Fr. Stephen]] explains that there are two kinds of blood: the innocent blood of victims that cries out for a reckoning, and the sacrificial blood that covers it, silences it, and provides purification. This sacrificial blood, by the command of [[God]], is what makes [[Atonement - Sacrifice]].

The theme of bloodguilt is further explored through the story of Joseph's brothers, who avoid murdering him directly but still feel the weight of their actions.

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2026-04-27.mp3#t=31:16), quoting [[Genesis]] 42:22: "And Reuben answered them, 'Did I not tell you not to sin against the boy? But you did not listen. So now there comes a reckoning for his blood.'"

This leads into a discussion of the "bridegroom of blood" in [[Exodus]] 4, where Zipporah performs a circumcision to avert [[God]]'s wrath, highlighting the idea of blood atoning for a failure to keep the covenant. The law regarding killing a thief at night ([[Exodus]] 22) introduces the legal concept of "bloodguilt," or `damim` in Hebrew, literally "bloods" in the plural. This legal status determines whether a killing constitutes murder requiring a reckoning.

## [[Martyrdom]] as [[Sacrifice]]

[[Fr. Stephen]] transitions the discussion to the pre-Christian Jewish understanding of martyrdom, reading the stories of Eleazar and the seven Maccabean brothers and their mother from [[2 Maccabees]]. These martyrs, facing death for refusing to violate [[God]]'s law, offer their suffering and death as a sacrifice for the sins of their people.

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2026-04-27.mp3#t=1:47:50), quoting the seventh Maccabean brother: "I, like my brothers, give up body and life for the laws of our fathers, appealing to [[God]] to show mercy soon to our nation, and by afflictions and plagues to make you confess that he alone is [[God]], and through me and my brothers to bring to an end the wrath of the Almighty, which has justly fallen on our whole nation."

This demonstrates a developed theology of redemptive suffering where the martyrs' innocent blood is offered to atone for the sins of the nation and put an end to the reckoning—[[God]]'s wrath—that has come upon them.

## The Blood of [[Christ]]

The hosts explain that this understanding of martyrdom is the direct context for the [[New Testament]]'s presentation of [[Christ]]'s sacrifice. The blood of [[Christ]] is the ultimate fulfillment of this pattern.

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2026-04-27.mp3#t=1:00:46), quoting [[Hebrews]] 9:22: "Indeed, under the law, almost everything is purified with blood, and without the shedding of blood, there is no forgiveness of sins."

The blood of [[Christ]] accomplishes what the blood of animals and even the blood of the Maccabean martyrs could not: it purifies, cleanses, and establishes a New Covenant. Unlike the blood of Abel that cried out for vengeance, the blood of [[Christ]] speaks a better word.

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2026-04-27.mp3#t=1:08:21), quoting [[Hebrews]] 12:24: "...and to [[Jesus]], the mediator of a new covenant, and to the sprinkled blood that speaks a better word than the blood of Abel."

[[Fr. Stephen]] explains that [[Christ]]'s entire life, not just his death, was a form of suffering. He voluntarily took on the "blameless passions" of human life—fatigue, hunger, thirst—and submitted to rejection, slander, and ultimately the most horrific death humanity had invented.

[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2026-04-27.mp3#t=1:23:32): "By suffering all of these things, by facing all of this testing, [[Christ]] makes that suffering that we share, he makes it salvific for us. He gives it a purpose... It becomes the means by which we are purified, we are sanctified, we are made ready for eternal life."

The episode concludes by connecting these themes to the life of the Church. The [[Eucharist]] is the participation in [[Christ]]'s self-offering, where believers offer themselves, their lives, and each other to [[God]]. This offering, united with [[Christ]]'s perfect sacrifice, is how repentance and purification are actualized in the New Covenant. The blood of [[Christ]] cleanses from all sin, transforming human suffering into a means of salvation and [[Theosis]].
