# The Prophet of the [[Most High God]]

## Introduction & Opening Prayer

The episode begins with a reading from [1 [[Enoch]] 48:4-5](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=00:00:00):

> He will be a staff for the righteous with which for them to stand and not to fall, and he will be the light of the nations and the hope of those whose hearts are troubled. All who dwell on the earth will fall down and worship him, and they will praise and bless and celebrate with song the [[Lord of Spirits]].

## Act 1: The Paradigmatic Prophet

[[Fr. Andrew]] and [[Fr. Stephen]] begin their discussion on St. [[John]] the Forerunner and Baptist by first examining his prototype: the [[Prophet Elijah]]. While [[Moses]] is a type of [[Christ]] as the lawgiver, [Elijah is the paradigmatic prophet in the Old Testament](httpshttps://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lord_of_spirits_2020-08-27.mp3#t=03:51), setting the pattern for what it means to be a prophet.

The narrative of [[Elijah]] and his successor [[Elisha]] is unique in that it breaks the typical format of the Books of [[Kings]] (Kingdoms). [For several chapters, the focus shifts entirely from the succession of kings to the detailed lives and actions of these two prophets](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=07:25), even though neither of them were kings. This narrative shift highlights their importance, establishing [[Elijah]] as the mold for prophethood.

[Elijah's ministry is a direct confrontation with the worship of Baal](httpshttps://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lord_of_spirits_2020-08-27.mp3#t=11:32), brought to [[Israel]] by Queen Jezebel. He demonstrates [[Yahweh]]'s authority over the domains [[Baal]] claimed to rule, such as weather and storms.
*   [[Elijah]] declares a drought, and it does not rain for years.
*   On Mount Carmel, he challenges the prophets of [[Baal]] to call down fire from heaven. They fail, but [[Yahweh]] answers [[Elijah]]'s prayer by sending fire (lightning) to consume the sacrifice, even though it was soaked in water. This is a direct display of power over the storm god's own domain.
*   [This act is described as "distributive justice"](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=29:17) for Jezebel's murder of [[Yahweh]]'s prophets. Just as God brought judgment upon the "gods of [[Egypt]]" during the [[Exodus]], this is a judgment upon [[Baal]] and his representatives.

Even in his moment of despair in the wilderness, [[Elijah]] receives a crucial revelation: God has preserved a "remnant" of 7,000 who have not bowed the knee to [[Baal]]. [This concept of the faithful remnant becomes central to understanding God's work throughout history](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=34:16), a theme [[St. Paul]] later picks up in his [[Epistle to the Romans]].

The culmination of [[Elijah]]'s earthly ministry is his ascension into heaven in a chariot of fire. This isn't just a dramatic exit; it signifies his entry into the [[Divine Council - Unseen Realm]]. A true prophet is one who has "stood in the council of the Lord" ([[Jeremiah]] 23:18) and becomes a messenger for that council. [[Elijah]] is taken up into this council to continue his work.

His successor, [[Elisha]], asks for a "double portion" of his spirit. [This isn't a request for twice the power, but a claim of inheritance as the firstborn son](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=44:49), who traditionally received a double portion of the father's estate. It signifies the continuation of their relationship and ministry. [[Elisha]] then demonstrates this inheritance by parting the Jordan River with [[Elijah]]'s mantle, just as [[Elijah]] had done, and mirroring [[Joshua]]'s entry into the promised land.

## Act 2: He will go before Him in the Spirit and Power of [[Elijah]]

[[St. John the Baptist]] is explicitly connected to [[Elijah]] through [[Old Testament]] prophecy and [[New Testament]] fulfillment.

*   **[[Malachi]]'s Prophecy:** [[Malachi]] prophesies that God will send [[Elijah]] back before the "great and awesome day of the Lord" to "turn the hearts of fathers to their children." This messenger prepares the way for God's arrival, lest the land be struck with a curse.

*   **The Angel's Proclamation:** The [[Archangel Gabriel]] tells [[Zechariah]] that his son, [[John]], will "go before him [the Lord] in the spirit and power of [[Elijah]]." This directly links [[John]]'s mission to [[Elijah]]'s.

[This connection shows that [[John]] is continuing [[Elijah]]'s ministry of preparing a remnant for the Lord's coming](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=01:06:55). Just as [[Elisha]] received the spirit of [[Elijah]], [[John]] operates with the same prophetic spirit and power. He is the fulfillment of [[Malachi]]'s prophecy.

The episode addresses the apparent contradiction where [[Jesus]] identifies [[John]] as [[Elijah]], but [[John]] himself denies it. [[Fr. Stephen]] explains:
> "[John] will go before him in the spirit and power of [[Elijah]]. Where have we seen that kind of language before? Well, at the transition from Saint Elias, from [[Elijah]] to [[Elisha]], where he wants a double portion of his, of [[Elijah]]'s spirit. Right. And so what this is setting up is the idea that in the same way [[Elisha]] continued to have this relationship with his friend and mentor who had now been exalted to the heavenly places, in the same way, now St. [[John]] has a relationship with St. Elias, who has been exalted to the heavenly places and is part of the [[Divine Council]]. And so, would have been one of those beings who he was growing up among."
> [Listen from [01:13:30](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=01:13:30)

[[John]] isn't [[Elijah]] reincarnated, but he continues his mission, guided by [[Elijah]] from the [[Divine Council]].

The traditional account of [[John]]'s childhood is that after his father [[Zechariah]] was martyred in the [[Temple]] for refusing to reveal his location, his mother Elizabeth fled with him into the wilderness to escape Herod's slaughter of the innocents. She died 40 days later, and [John was raised by angels in the desert, literally living the "angelic life."](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=01:14:50) This experience mirrors that of the [[Old Testament]] prophets who entered into the [[Divine Council]].

## Act 3: The Forerunner in the Council

[[St. John the Baptist]]'s role extends into the heavenly reality of the [[Divine Council - Unseen Realm]]. This is most clearly depicted in the Deisis icon.
> **[[Fr. Stephen]]:** "On the Deisis icon, you have [[Christ]] enthroned at the center, and then on his right hand, you have his mother, the [[Theotokos]]. And on his left hand, you have St. [[John]] the Forerunner... We've talked about how the royal court of [[David]] in particular was an icon of the [[Divine Council]], the heavenly council. And so in the same way that we see the queen mother at the right hand of [[David]], at the left hand of [[David]] is the prophet of the Lord who is there in the court."
> [Listen from [01:17:21](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=01:17:21)

St. [[John]] occupies the place of the court prophet, the chief advisor and messenger. In the iconography and hymnography of the Church, [St. [[John]]'s role in the [[Divine Council]] is seen as replacing that of Satan](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=01:19:10). The devil, once a cherub or seraph and a throne guardian, fell from his position. St. [[John]], by preparing the way for [[Christ]] and calling people to repentance to approach God's holiness, now fulfills that role of guarding the throne of God.

The angel wings often depicted on icons of St. [[John]] are not literal. [They signify his role as a messenger (Greek: *angelos*)](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=01:21:46), connecting him to the angelic hosts and his function within the [[Divine Council]].

This relationship between a great saint in the council ([[Elijah]]) and a person on earth ([[John]]) serves as the model for the concept of patron saints.
> **[[Fr. Andrew]]:** "That person does not get between you and God. That person is there to lead you to God. They are a guide in salvation, a guide in the [[Divine Council]]. They're not a, you know, someone who stands between you and God. That's ridiculous, and they would never see themselves that way."
> [Listen from [01:44:27](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-02-10.mp3#t=01:44:27)

Patron saints are not intermediaries in a transactional sense, but guides and fellow members of the community of God, leading us into a deeper relationship with Him. The entire work of God is communal, aimed at gathering His people, His remnant, together. [[Salvation]] is not an individualistic project but a journey into this divine community.
