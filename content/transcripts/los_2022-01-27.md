### Core Theological Concepts

#### [[Priesthood and Tabernacle]]

##### The First [[Priests]] and the First [[Kings]]
*   [[Fr. Stephen]] explains that [[Adam]] was a [[Priest]] in [[Paradise]]. The creation account in [[Genesis 1]] and [[Genesis 2]] corresponds to ancient Near Eastern [[Temple]] dedication texts. The final step in these rituals was placing the image of the deity within the [[Temple]]. This is inverted in the [[Genesis]] account: [[Adam]], as the image of [[God]], is placed in the Garden, which is a [[Temple]], and is meant to actively image [[God]] to the world. [Listen from 00:03:49](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-01-27.mp3#t=00:03:49)
*   The essential function of a [[Priest]] is to represent [[God]] to creation and to represent creation back to [[God]]. [[Fr. Stephen]] defines this dual role, explaining that [[Adam]] was meant to continue [[God]]'s work of creation by bringing order and life to the world outside [[Eden]]. This expansion of [[Eden]] was [[Adam]]'s offering. [Listen from 01:00:10](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-01-27.mp3#t=01:00:10)
*   [[Fr. Stephen]] describes [[Priesthood]] as an inherent element of human nature, a "directedness of being" with a teleological purpose. The end (telos) of a human being is [[God]]. The role of a [[Priest]], therefore, is not a [[Job]] or an office but a way of relating to the world and being in relationship with [[God]]. [Listen from 01:03:32](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-01-27.mp3#t=01:03:32)
*   [[Fr. Andrew]] points out the union of kingship and [[Priesthood]] in the ancient world and in the person of [[Adam]]. The [[Patriarchs]] ([[Abraham]], [[Isaac]], [[Jacob]], [[Job]]) acted as [[Priests]] for their extended families, offering sacrifices and interceding for them. [[Priesthood]] and fatherhood are deeply connected. [Listen from 01:21:58](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-01-27.mp3#t=01:21:58)
*   The [[Priesthood]] becomes fractured after [[Moses]] fails in his priestly duty as a [[Father]] by not circumcising his son ([[Exodus 4]]). Because he failed to be a [[Priest]] for his own family, the broader [[Priesthood]] is taken from him and given to his brother, [[Aaron]], and his descendants. This separates the roles of king (leader) and [[High priest]]. [Listen from 01:51:30](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-01-27.mp3#t=01:51:30)
*   The Levitical [[Priesthood]] is further defined and restricted after incidents like the Golden Calf ([[Exodus 32]]) and Korah's rebellion ([[Numbers 16]]). The [[Levites]] who stood with [[Moses]] were given the priestly role, which was taken from the elders of the other tribes. This creates concentric circles of holiness: the [[High priest]], the [[Priests]], the [[Levites]], and then the rest of [[Israel]]. [Listen from 02:02:49](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-01-27.mp3#t=02:02:49)
*   [[Israel]] as a whole [[Acts]] as a "kingdom of [[Priests]]" ([[Exodus 19:6]]) in relation to the 70 nations of the world. They are held to a higher standard of holiness because they serve this priestly function for the other nations, as seen in the 70 bulls offered for the nations at the [[Feast of Tabernacles]] ([[Numbers 23]]). [Listen from 02:03:59](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-01-27.mp3#t=02:03:59)
*   In the New [[Covenant]], [[Christ]] reunites the roles of [[Priest]] and king, fulfilling what was separated in [[Moses]] and [[Aaron]]. He is the ultimate [[High priest]] because he perfectly represents [[God]] to humanity (as [[God]]) and humanity to [[God]] (as man). This fulfillment is not an abolition but a "filling up" of the [[Old Testament]] [[Priesthood]]. [Listen from 01:21:05](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-01-27.mp3#t=01:21:05)
*   The Church continues this priestly function. There are concentric circles of [[Priesthood]]: the [[Bishop]], the presbyters ([[Priests]]), and the deacons, which parallel the [[Old Testament]] structure. The faithful laity also form a "royal [[Priesthood]]" ([[1 [[Peter]] 2:9]]) that represents [[God]] to the world and intercedes for it. [Listen from 01:32:41](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-01-27.mp3#t=01:32:41)
*   A true [[Priest]] [[Acts]] as a transparent conduit. [[Idolatry]], in contrast, is when this conduit gets "stopped up," and the intermediary begins to absorb the worship or power for himself, usurping [[God]]'s authority. This is what happened with pagan [[Priest]]-[[Kings]] and corrupt [[Priests]] like the [[Sadducees]]. [Listen from 01:41:58](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-01-27.mp3#t=01:41:58)
