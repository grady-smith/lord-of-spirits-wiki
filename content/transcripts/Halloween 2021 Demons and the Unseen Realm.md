# [[Halloween]] 2021: [[Demons]] and the Unseen Realm

## Introduction

This is the annual [[Halloween]] episode of [[Lord of Spirits]]. [[Fr. Andrew]] and [[Fr. Stephen]] discuss the darker side of the spiritual world, focusing on demons and how they were understood in the ancient world, particularly in Greek, Roman, [[Second Temple]] Jewish, and early Christian contexts. A parental advisory is given, as the episode contains mature themes, especially in the third half. [Listen from 02:51](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2021-10-27.mp3#t=171).

The central thesis of the episode is that in the ancient Christian understanding, demons are not fallen angels themselves, but rather the disembodied spirits of the dead [[Nephilim - Giants]], the offspring of the fallen angels and human women. The opposite of a demon is not an angel, but a saint.

## The Greek and Roman Understanding of [[Demons]]

[[Fr. Stephen]] begins by explaining the etymology of the word "demon" from the Greek *daimon* (δαίμων), which comes from a root meaning "to divide" or "apportion." [Listen from 07:27](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2021-10-27.mp3#t=447). These were seen as spiritual beings who divided up lots, fates, and destinies. In Latin, the related concept is the *genius*, a spirit associated with a person, place, or people group, from the root *genē* (to beget or produce offspring).

According to ancient Greek writers like Hesiod, the *daemones* (demons) were originally the spirits of the dead heroes from the golden age. [Listen from 14:43](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2021-10-27.mp3#t=883). These spirits were thought to wander the earth, and shrines were built to them next to temples to placate them and keep them from causing trouble. This idea of placating angry, wandering spirits was a common feature of paganism. Ruined cities were considered haunted and dangerous because the local gods and demons were no longer being worshiped and were therefore angry. [Listen from 21:40](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2021-10-27.mp3#t=1300).

These beings occupied a realm between gods and humans. They were primarily "energetic" beings, meaning their influence was experienced rather than being physically seen or directly interacted with in most cases. The original meaning of "demonic possession" was simply to have a demon associated with you, which, as in the case of Socrates's *daimonion*, was not always considered a negative thing. [Listen from 23:23](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2021-10-27.mp3#t=1403).

## The [[Second Temple]] Jewish and Early Christian Understanding

The discussion transitions to the [[Second Temple]] Jewish understanding, which forms the basis for the [[New Testament]] and early Christian view. This view is primarily found in Enochic literature like the [[Book of Enoch]] and Jubilees.

The core idea is that the unclean spirits, or demons, are the disembodied spirits of the [[Nephilim - Giants]]. [Listen from 39:40](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2021-10-27.mp3#t=2380). When the [[Nephilim]] were killed in the flood, their spirits were not allowed to go to their final rest and were condemned to wander the earth, tormenting humanity until the final judgment. The Book of Jubilees describes how Mastema, one of their leaders, begs [[God]] to allow a portion of these spirits to remain on earth to afflict wicked people, which [[God]] allows as a means of bringing them to repentance. [Listen from 47:45](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2021-10-27.mp3#t=2865).

This view was carried forward by early Christian writers:
- **Josephus**, a 1st-century Pharisee, directly connects the Greek stories of giants with the offspring of angels and women from the posterity of Seth. [Listen from 42:07](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2021-10-27.mp3#t=2527).
- **St. Justin Martyr** (2nd century) states that the demons are the children of the fallen angels and women, and that these demons are the ones worshiped by the pagans as gods like Zeus. [Listen from 48:36](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2021-10-27.mp3#t=2916).
- **Athenagoras** (2nd century) explicitly says, "the souls of the giants are the demons who wander about the world." [Listen from 49:27](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2021-10-27.mp3#t=2967).
- **Commodianus** (3rd century) adds that the images and idols that pagans worship are representations of these dead giants. [Listen from 49:46](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2021-10-27.mp3#t=2986).

## St. Augustine, Demon Sex, and the Great Shift

In the 5th century, there was a shift in interpretation, most famously represented by St. Augustine. He and others began to interpret the "sons of [[God]]" in [[Genesis]] 6 as the righteous line of Seth, not angels. However, even in rejecting the literal angel-human procreation, St. Augustine did not reject the reality of sexual interaction between demons and humans.

In *City of [[God]]*, he discusses the widespread belief in incubi (male demons) and succubi (female demons). [Listen from 1:33:32](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2021-10-27.mp3#t=5612). He posits a theory, shared by St. Thomas Aquinas later, that a demon could appear as a succubus to a man, collect his seed, and then appear as an incubus to a woman to impregnate her. In this view, the offspring would still be fully human, but the demon would have acted as an intermediary. This demonstrates that even the later Fathers who reinterpreted [[Genesis]] 6 still held to a very real and interactive view of demons.

[[St. John Chrysostom]] also spiritualizes the concept of becoming a demon. He argues that it is not dying a violent death that makes a soul a wandering spirit, but living a life of sin. [Listen from 59:31](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2021-10-27.mp3#t=3571). Through sinful desires and actions, a person's soul can become like a demon.

## Conclusion: The Opposite of a Demon is a Saint

[[Fr. Stephen]] concludes by stating that the true opposite of a demon is not an angel, but a saint. [Listen from 1:07:22](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2021-10-27.mp3#t=4042). A demon is the spirit of a dead human (a [[Nephilim]]) who has given themselves over to evil and is now bound to tormenting others. A saint is a human who has united themselves to [[God]] through [[Christ]] and now lives in eternal glory, interceding for and helping humanity. This reframes the spiritual battle not just as a conflict between humanity and external evil forces, but as the ultimate choice each human soul must make.

The exorcisms performed at baptism are a direct confrontation with this reality. They cast out the demonic spirit that may be yoked to a person's life and replace it with a Guardian Angel, who guides and protects the person on their path to holiness. [Listen from 49:27](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/lordofspirits_2021-10-27.mp3#t=2967).
