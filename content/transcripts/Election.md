## Defining "Chosen" and "Elect"

[[Fr. Stephen]] clarifies the original Hebrew and Greek terms translated as "elect" or "chosen."

[The Hebrew word is *bahir*. This word is an adjective, not a participle. This is a critical distinction because it describes a quality of a person, not an action done *to* them. It means something like "given a role," "set apart for a purpose," or "assigned."](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-04-24.mp3#t=09:44)

[The Greek word is *eklektos*. While the related verb, *eklegomai*, can mean "to call out," the adjective *eklektos* means something more like "identified" from a group for a particular purpose.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-04-24.mp3#t=11:45) He contrasts this correct understanding with a common misinterpretation, citing Leighton Flowers' example of "USDA Choice meat," which implies a quality grade rather than an assigned role.

## Old Testament Examples of "Election"

[[Fr. Stephen]] and [[Fr. Andrew]] then walk through several Old Testament examples to illustrate the correct meaning of being "chosen."

1.  **The "Chosen People" (Israel)**: The most common example is Israel. [Fr. Stephen points out that Israel is not one of the 70 nations listed in [[Genesis]] 10. Rather, God *creates* Israel from Abraham, who was called *out* of one of those nations.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-04-24.mp3#t=15:17) Israel's "chosen" status is not about being picked over others for salvation, but about being assigned a unique role or purpose: to be a "kingdom of priests" for the other nations. They were to represent God to the nations and the nations to God. A concrete example of this is the practice during the Feast of Tabernacles of [offering 70 bulls for the 70 nations](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-04-24.mp3#t=19:35).

2.  **The "Chosen City" (Jerusalem)**: Jerusalem is called the "chosen city" because it is the place God designated for His name to dwell, i.e., the site of the Temple. [This choice was an assignment of purpose.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-04-24.mp3#t=21:19)

3.  **Chosen Subgroups**:
    *   **The Elders of Israel**: In [[Numbers]] 1:16, the elders are referred to as "chosen." These men were already tribal chiefs. Their "election" was about being assigned the new, specific role of assisting Moses in governing. [It was not about God picking them from a lineup.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-04-24.mp3#t=21:52)
    *   **Military Units**: In passages like [[1 Kingdoms (1 Samuel)]] 24:2, military units are called "the chosen." This simply refers to the able-bodied men who were *qualified* for military service. [They were mustered for a task, not predestined for a status.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-04-24.mp3#t=22:47)

4.  **The "Elect One" (The Messiah)**: In [[Second Temple]] Jewish literature, "the Elect One" is used interchangeably with "the Anointed One" (Messiah). [This refers to the one given the office and role of Messiah. It is not about God selecting one person out of many potential candidates.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-04-24.mp3#t=24:12)

## Elect [[Angels]] and the Problem for Calvinist Soteriology

[[Fr. Stephen]] argues that the concept of "elect angels" (from [[1 Timothy]] 5:21) poses a significant problem for the Calvinist understanding of election, which centers on predestination to salvation.

[Angels do not have salvation or redemption offered to them in the same way as humans. Therefore, "elect angels" cannot mean "angels chosen for salvation."](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-04-24.mp3#t=25:04) Instead, it refers to those angels who have the quality of faithfulness and serve a particular role in God's heavenly host, as opposed to the fallen angels.

This leads to a discussion of the two major Calvinist views on God's decrees:
*   **Infralapsarianism**: God decreed the Fall of Man, and then, looking at a fallen humanity, decreed to save some (the elect) from it.
*   **Supralapsarianism**: God first decreed to save some and damn others for His glory, and then decreed the Fall as the means by which to accomplish this.

[Fr. Stephen notes that John Calvin himself inherited these predestinarian ideas from the medieval Dominican theological tradition, particularly from St. Thomas Aquinas, though Calvin took them in a different direction.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-04-24.mp3#t=28:10)

## The Righteousness of the Law and of Faithfulness in [[Romans]]

The main body of the episode is an exegesis of key passages in the book of [[Romans]] that are central to the debate on election.

### [[Romans]] 8

[Fr. Stephen points out that in [[Romans]] 8:28-30, all the key verbs ("foreknew," "predestined," "called," "justified," "glorified") are in the past tense (aorist). St. Paul is not laying out a timeless, abstract sequence of salvation for all individuals. Instead, he is pointing to people *in the past* as evidence of God's faithfulness, showing how God has worked in their lives to demonstrate that "all things work together for good."](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-04-24.mp3#t=35:30)

### [[Romans]] 9

This chapter is often used to support individual predestination. [[Fr. Stephen]] offers a different reading:

*   **Not All Israel is Israel**: [[St. Paul]]'s argument that "not all who are descended from Israel belong to Israel" ([[Romans]] 9:6) is about covenantal and historical roles, not individual salvation. Just as Ishmael was not the heir of the promise despite being Abraham's son, and Esau was not the heir despite being Isaac's son, so too not all ethnic Jews are part of the faithful remnant.
*   **Jacob and Esau**: The prophecy "the older will serve the younger" ([[Romans]] 9:12) refers to the nations of Edom (descended from Esau) and Israel (descended from Jacob). Historically, Edom became a vassal state to Israel. [This is about the roles of nations in salvation history, not the eternal destiny of the individuals Jacob and Esau.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-04-24.mp3#t=38:14)
*   **"Jacob I loved, Esau I hated"**: This quote ([[Romans]] 9:13) is from [[Malachi]], written during the exile. It refers to God's covenantal actions toward the nations of Israel and Edom, particularly after Edom betrayed Judah during the Babylonian invasion. It is not a statement about God's feelings toward the two men before they were born.
*   **Pharaoh and the Potter's Clay**: The hardening of Pharaoh's heart ([[Romans]] 9:17-18) and the analogy of the potter and the clay ([[Romans]] 9:20-23) are also about God using nations and individuals for His purposes in history. [Pharaoh becomes an object lesson for all nations, including Egypt, demonstrating God's power. The potter makes different vessels (nations) for different purposes (e.g., Moab as God's "washpot").](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-04-24.mp3#t=39:15) This demonstrates God's sovereignty over history, not His arbitrary choice of who goes to heaven and hell.

### [[Romans]] 10

[[St. Paul]] contrasts the "righteousness based on the law" with the "righteousness based on faith."

[Fr. Stephen highlights a crucial point often missed: St. Paul quotes Moses (from Leviticus and Deuteronomy) to describe *both* forms of righteousness. He is not contrasting Moses with a new idea. The righteousness that comes from faithfulness *is* the message of Deuteronomy. The word is not far off; Christ has already done the work of ascending and descending. What remains is for people to be faithful to the call.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-04-24.mp3#t=42:10)

### [[Romans]] 11

*   **The Remnant**: [[St. Paul]] argues that God has not rejected Israel. There has always been a faithful remnant, just as in the days of Elijah there were 7,000 who had not bowed to Baal. [This remnant is "chosen by grace," meaning their status comes from God's action, not from their "works of the law" (ethnic boundary markers).](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-04-24.mp3#t=43:10)
*   **The Olive Tree**: The central metaphor of the chapter. Unbelieving branches (of Israel) have been broken off, and wild olive shoots (the Gentiles) have been grafted in. [[St. Paul]] warns the Gentiles not to be arrogant, because they stand by faithfulness, and if they become faithless, they too can be broken off. Crucially, he states that the original branches can be grafted back in. [This shows that one's status is not fixed by an eternal decree but is contingent on ongoing faithfulness.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-04-24.mp3#t=46:17)
*   **The Fullness of the Gentiles and All Israel**: [[St. Paul]] reveals a "mystery": a partial hardening has come upon Israel *until* the "fullness of the Gentiles" has come in. [[Fr. Stephen]] explains that the "fullness of the Gentiles" is a direct reference to Jacob's prophecy over Ephraim in [[Genesis]] 48, where Ephraim's offspring would become the "fullness of the nations." The northern kingdom of Israel (led by the tribe of Ephraim) was scattered and assimilated into the Gentile nations. [St. Paul's argument is that when these lost tribes return to God by coming to Christ through the Gentile nations they are part of, then "all Israel will be saved" — both Judah and the restored northern tribes.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-04-24.mp3#t=47:49)

## Conclusion: Making Your Calling and Election Sure

[[Fr. Stephen]] concludes by connecting all these threads to [[2 Peter]] 1:5-11.

[St. Peter exhorts Christians to "make every effort to supplement your faithfulness with virtue..." and so on, in order to "confirm your calling and election." This indicates that one's election is not an unchangeable, fixed decree from eternity past. It is something that must be made "sure" or "firm" through effort, struggle, and the acquisition of virtues through faithfulness.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/episode_name.mp3#t:01:03:00)

The calling God gives is not too hard. He has provided everything needed for salvation. Despair is not from God. The Christian life is about responding to God's call with faithfulness, making the effort to acquire the virtues, and in doing so, working out one's salvation. [God is faithful and will bring to completion the good work He has begun in those who remain faithful to Him.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/episode_name.mp3#t:01:03:25)
