## Recap: Creation in [[Genesis]]

To understand the [[Fall]], it is necessary to recap key themes from [[Genesis]] 1 and 2.

1.  **Creation as a [[Temple]] Narrative**: [Listen from 05:14](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-10-08.mp3#t=05:14) The creation account in [[Genesis]] 1 follows the general pattern of ancient Near East [[temple]] construction narratives. Creation is depicted as a [[temple]], and on the seventh day (the Sabbath), [[God]] is enthroned within it. This act is not one of rest from weariness but of enthronement and rule.
2.  **Order and Life**: [Listen from 06:09](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-10-08.mp3#t=06:09) The six days of creation show a pattern: the first three days involve bringing order to chaos, and the next three days involve filling that ordered creation with life. The Garden of [[Eden]] is presented as the perfect combination of this divine order and life.
3.  **Humanity's Vocation**: [Listen from 06:44](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-10-08.mp3#t=06:44) This pattern of ordering and filling is then handed to humanity as their core vocation: to "fill the earth and subdue it." They are to extend the order and life of the Garden to the rest of creation.
4.  **Creation of Angelic Beings**: [Listen from 10:40](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-10-08.mp3#t=10:40) The invisible creation, including angelic beings, is part of the same creative act described in [[Genesis]] 1. The Church Fathers generally place the creation of [[angels]] within the six days, often on Day 1 (creation of light) or Day 4 (creation of heavenly bodies). This understanding contrasts with ideas like that found in Milton's *Paradise Lost*, which posits a war in heaven *before* the [[Genesis]] creation. [[Genesis]] 1 is about the creation of *everything*, visible and invisible.

## The [[Fall]] of the [[Devil]]

The [[fall]] of the [[devil]] is not a pre-creation event but occurs within the narrative of [[Genesis]], specifically in [[Genesis]] 3. Just as [[Genesis]] 2 is a "zoom-in" on the creation of man, [[Genesis]] 3 is a "zoom-in" on the [[fall]] of both humanity and the [[devil]].

-   **Patristic and Scriptural Accounts**:
    -   St. Andrew of Caesarea, commenting on [[Revelation]] 12, states that the [[devil]] was cast down *after* the creation of the perceptible world due to pride and envy of humanity. This is linked to [[St. Paul]]'s reference to the "prince and power of the air" ([[Ephesians#Chapter 2|[[Ephesians]] 2:2]]). [Listen from 19:10](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-10-08.mp3#t=19:10)
    -   [[Isaiah#Chapter 14|[[Isaiah]] 14:13-15]] describes the [[devil]]'s ambition to exalt his throne above the stars of [[God]] (the [[Divine Council - Unseen Realm]]) on the "mount of assembly," a reference to Baal's mythological home on Mount Zaphon. This ambition results in him being cast down to [[Sheol]]. [Listen from 20:53](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-10-08.mp3#t=20:53)
    -   [[Ezekiel#Chapter 28|[[Ezekiel]] 28:13-17]] identifies the fallen one as an "anointed guardian cherub" in [[Eden]], the garden of [[God]], who was cast out from the mountain of [[God]] because of his pride. [Listen from 21:33](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-10-08.mp3#t=21:33)
    -   [[Revelation#Chapter 12|[[Revelation]] 12]] depicts St. [[Michael]] casting down the dragon after the birth of [[Christ]], which St. Andrew of Caesarea reconciles by positing two "falls" or senses of the [[fall]]: the original expulsion from [[Eden]] ([[Genesis]] 3), and a later casting down during [[Christ]]'s ministry, as referenced in [[Luke#Chapter 10|[[Luke]] 10:18]]. [Listen from 22:48](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-10-08.mp3#t=22:48)

-   **The Nature of the Rebellion**:
    -   The [[devil]]'s rebellion was not a direct military attempt to overthrow [[God]], as that is impossible for a creature. Instead, it was an act of envy and an attempt to usurp worship. He tempted humanity to serve and worship *him* within [[God]]'s [[temple]] (creation), thereby corrupting it. [Listen from 27:16](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-10-08.mp3#t=27:16)
    -   The [[devil]] was envious of humanity's unique destiny: [[Theosis]]. Angelic ranks are static, but humanity was created with the potential for "upward mobility" to become united with [[God]] through the [[Incarnation]]. Knowing he would become less glorious than humanity, the [[devil]] sought to destroy them. [Listen from 29:35](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-10-08.mp3#t=29:35)

## The [[Fall]] of Humanity and the Garments of Skin

A caller, Kyle from California, asked about the distinction between the fig leaves Adam and Eve made and the garments of skin [[God]] made for them. [Listen from 39:35](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-10-08.mp3#t=39:35)

-   **Fig Leaves and Nakedness**: The immediate problem Adam and Eve sought to solve was not guilt, but the shame of their nakedness. This nakedness symbolizes their loss of innocence. They were like children who had reached an age of maturity where innocence is lost and shame is experienced. The fig leaves are their human attempt to cover this new reality of shame and exposure. [Listen from 42:50](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-10-08.mp3#t=42:50)

-   **The Garments of Skin**: The "garments of skin" represent a fundamental change in human nature.
    -   The expulsion from Paradise was a compassionate act by [[God]]. To remain in [[God]]'s immediate presence—a "consuming fire"—in their fallen state would have meant their destruction. To prevent them from eating of the [[Tree of Life]] and living forever in sin, [[God]] sent them into exile. [Listen from 41:16](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-10-08.mp3#t=41:16)
    -   The garments are not about an appeasement of wrath. [[God]] kills an animal to show them what death is—the physical image of their spiritual death (separation from [[God]]). This new mode of existence is mortality. Their bodies become "passible," meaning subject to corruption, suffering, passions, and death, more like the animals. [Listen from 45:10](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-10-08.mp3#t=45:10)

## The Hope of Repentance and [[Theosis]]

Death, while a consequence of the [[Fall]], becomes a tool for salvation.

-   **Spiritual vs. Physical Death**: St. [[John]] of Damascus distinguishes between spiritual death (the separation of the soul from [[God]]) and physical death (the separation of the soul from the body). The spiritual death happened immediately after the sin, leading to the process of physical death. The body, cut off from the soul's life (which comes from [[God]]), begins to die. [Listen from 54:19](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-10-08.mp3#t=54:19)

-   **The Two Journeys**: After the [[Fall]], humanity is on two simultaneous journeys:
    1.  **Repentance**: A journey of healing from the passions, sin, and corruption.
    2.  **[[Theosis]]**: The original journey toward our destiny of becoming like [[God]].
    
    This mortal life is a "parenthesis" or "speed bump" on the road to [[Theosis]]. It is a time for repentance and healing before we can resume our progress toward [[God]] in its fullness. [Listen from 48:38](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-10-08.mp3#t=48:38)

-   **Mutability and Hope**: [[Fr. Andrew]] concludes that the mutability and mortality given to us through death is a profoundly hopeful thing. It means we are not stuck in our fallen state. We can change. As St. [[Isaac]] the Syrian says, "This life has been given to you for repentance. Do not waste it in vain pursuits." By aiming our lives at [[Christ]], our true destiny, we can transform our mortal existence into a path toward [[God]], and in doing so, find that all other good things are "thrown in." [Listen from 54:19](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-10-08.mp3#t=54:19)

-   **The Uniqueness of [[Christ]]**: [[Fr. Stephen]] emphasizes that while other philosophies and religions may offer insights into navigating this mortal life, only [[Christ]] reveals and makes possible the true human destiny of union with [[God]]. Our goal is not merely to avoid sin, but to actively become like [[Christ]]. [Listen from 56:29](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2020-10-08.mp3#t=56:29)
