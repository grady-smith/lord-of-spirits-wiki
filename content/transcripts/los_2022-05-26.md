## [[Prophets]]

### [[Prophecy]] as a Sub-species of Divination

**Context:** In the ancient world, [[Prophecy]] was understood as a sub-species of a broader category called divination. Divination is any attempt to access the knowledge and perspective of the divine realm, which is not normally accessible to humans.

**Quote:** "[[Prophecy]] as such is sort of a subspecies of divination. Divining. And people may not have noticed before that divination involves the word 'divine.' ... So there's a divine realm, right? There's a divine perspective which is greater than the perspective of humans. Right? So there is a wisdom that divine beings have or an understanding or a knowledge that they have, a perspective they have that human beings aren't otherwise privy to." [Listen from 05:05:07](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lord_of_spirits_prophets.mp3#t=05:05:07)

### Distinction from Other Forms of Divination

**Context:** Unlike other forms of divination that rely on techniques (like astrology, reading entrails, or observing animal movements), true [[Prophecy]] is characterized by a direct encounter or relationship with a divine being. The [[Prophet]]'s authority comes from this direct experience, not from a learned skill.

**Quote:** "For the most part, [[Prophecy]] did not involve that kind of technique. It was not about reading signs. It was about a direct, more direct encounter and direct relationship between the person who's considered a [[Prophet]] and the deity." [Listen from 12:35:10](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lord_of_spirits_prophets.mp3#t=12:35:10)

### The Deir Alla Inscription and Balaam

**Context:** An archaeological find from Deir Alla in modern-day Jordan provides significant extrabiblical evidence for the figure of Balaam. The inscription, dated to the late 9th or early 8th century BC, mentions "Balaam, the son of Beor," a "[[Prophet]] of the gods," showing he was a known figure in the region, independent of the biblical narrative.

**Quote:** "There was an inscription found in Deir Alla, which is now in Jordan, but which was in Moab... from the late 9th or early 8th century BC. And the inscription is in a wall... it refers to Balaam the son of Beor, right, so literally the same name. And it identifies him as a [[Prophet]] of Ashtar." [Listen from 26:40:27](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lord_of_spirits_prophets.mp3#t=26:40:27)

### The Prophetic Role in the Church

**Context:** The prophetic role did not end with the [[Old Testament]] but continues in the Church, primarily through the monastic tradition. Monastics, as "outsiders" dedicated to a life of [[Repentance]] and prayer, function as a corrective to the established church hierarchy, calling them to faithfulness, much like the [[Old Testament]] [[Prophets]] called the [[Kings]] and [[Priests]] to account.

**Quote:** "That prophetic role is now fulfilled by [[Monasticism]], by the monastic [[Saints]]. Who are these people who are outside the structure, who from the desert... come and tell emperors to repent." [Listen from 44:48:20](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lord_of_spirits_prophets.mp3#t=44:48:20)
