## The [[Word of God]] in Scripture and Tradition

**[[Fr. Stephen]]:** [In the Bible, the phrase "[[Word of God]]" or "Word of the Lord" is never used to refer to the Bible. The Bible doesn't call itself anything in the Bible, really, because the Bible doesn't talk about the Bible.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-25.mp3#t=09:09:44) It refers to itself as "the scriptures" or "written things." The significance of something being "written" in the ancient world was immense due to the cost and effort involved, making written texts literary monuments.

**[[Fr. Andrew]]:** [To get a copy of something the size of the book of Romans, just the book of Romans, which is 16 chapters, a copy of that would have cost the equivalent of $5,000 in the ancient world.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-25.mp3#t=15:52:16) This underscores the weight and value given to written texts.

**[[Fr. Stephen]]:** [Most of modern scholarship has viewed [[John]]'s introduction as the locus classicus for discussing the [[Logos]], or the [[Word of God]]. This has come out of early modern [[Protestant]] theological movements, where a distinction was made between what is "Hebraic" or "Semitic" on one hand, and what is "Greek" or "Hellenic" on the other.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-25.mp3#t=23:53:26) This paradigm led scholars to interpret [[John]]'s Gospel through the lens of Greek philosophy, seeing the [[Logos]] concept as derived from thinkers like Heraclitus ([[Logos]] as ordered structures), the Stoics ([[Logos]] as an active, ordering force), and especially [[Philo of Alexandria]].

**[[Fr. Stephen]]:** [The typical read of Philo is that he's a Middle Platonist who Hellenized Judaism. The reappraisal, by scholars like [[Daniel]] Boyarin, is that he Judaized [[Hellenism]], using Greek ideas in service of Jewish ones.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-25.mp3#t=34:31:07) Philo's [[Logos]] is a supreme mediator presiding over the [[Divine Council - Unseen Realm]], created by God but serving as the instrument of creation. He is a person and is identified with the [[Angel of the Lord]].

**[[Fr. Stephen]]:** [The Aramaic Targums are not just translations but are often expansions or commentaries on the Hebrew scriptures. In places where [[Yahweh]] is seen or has body parts, they insert the Aramaic word *Memra* (word). The Word of the Lord acts as a person, an intermediary, to reconcile these theophanies with God's transcendence.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-25.mp3#t=38:29:43)

## The Word in the [[Old Testament]]

The discussion moves to specific [[Old Testament]] examples where "the Word of the Lord" is more than just a message, but a person who acts and is identified as [[Yahweh]].

*   **[[Genesis]] 15:** [The Word of [[Yahweh]] comes to Abram in a vision. Abram addresses this figure as "Lord [[Yahweh]]," and the figure accepts this title, speaking as God in the first person. This shows the Word is both distinguished from and identified with [[Yahweh]].](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-25.mp3#t=45:30:11)
*   **1 [[Samuel]] (1 Kingdoms) 3:** [The call of [[Samuel]] begins by stating that "the word of [[Yahweh]] was rare... there were not many visions." [[Samuel]]'s inability to recognize God is because "the word of [[Yahweh]] had not yet been revealed (unveiled) to him." [[Yahweh]] then comes and *stands* by [[Samuel]]'s bed. The chapter concludes, "[[Yahweh]] revealed himself to [[Samuel]] at Shiloh by the word of the Lord." The knowledge of God comes through a visual encounter with this person.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-25.mp3#t=50:31:21)
*   **[[Jeremiah]] 1:** [The Word of [[Yahweh]] comes to [[Jeremiah]] and says, "Before I formed you in the womb, I knew you." The Word speaks as the Creator, and then physically "put out his hand and touched" [[Jeremiah]]'s mouth, putting his words into it. This is a personal, physical interaction.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-25.mp3#t=51:30:27)
*   **Psalm 18:30 (17:31 LXX):** ["This God, his way is perfect; the word of the Lord proves true; he is a shield for all those who take refuge in him." The pronoun "he" refers back to the Word of the Lord, identifying the Word as a person who acts.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-25.mp3#t=59:41:00)
*   **Psalm 33:6 (32:6 LXX):** ["By the word of the Lord the heavens were made, and by the breath of his mouth all their host." This verse links the creative act to both the Word and the Breath (Spirit) of the Lord, a key text for Trinitarian theology.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-25.mp3#t=1:00:26)

**[[Fr. Andrew]]:** [So, there are three things that are really important to understand. Number one is that this is a person... Number two, that this person is seen and interacts bodily... And then the third one is that this person is [[Yahweh]]... and is also distinguished from [[Yahweh]].](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-25.mp3#t=54:57:04)

## The Word in St. [[John]]'s Gospel

The final segment is a detailed exegesis of the Prologue of St. [[John]]'s Gospel ([[John]] 1:1-18), showing how it builds upon these [[Old Testament]] concepts.

*   **[[John]] 1:1-2:** ["In the beginning was the Word..." The Greek verb *ēn* is in the imperfect tense, meaning "was being" or "already was." Before creation, the Word already existed. "The Word was with God (pros ton Theon)" means He was co-located with "the God." "And the Word was God (Theos ēn ho logos)" uses "God" without an article as a predicate, defining the Word's nature. He is distinct from the Father, yet shares the same divine nature.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-25.mp3#t=1:15:28)
*   **[[John]] 1:3:** ["All things were made (egeneto - came into being) through him..." The Word is the uncreated instrument of creation, distinct from the created order.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-25.mp3#t=1:22:10)
*   **[[John]] 1:4-5:** ["In him was life, and the life was the light of men." This connects directly to [[Genesis]] 1:3 ("Let there be light"). The light of creation is the very life of God in [[Christ]], which is salvation and [[Theosis]]. The purpose of creation from the beginning was for humanity to share in God's divine life.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-25.mp3#t=1:33:38)
*   **[[John]] 1:10-13:** The Word was in the world (pre-incarnation), came to His own ([[Israel]]), but was rejected. However, a remnant who were "faithful to his name" received Him and were given the authority to become children of God, "begotten of God." This is the story of the [[Old Testament]].
*   **[[John]] 1:14:** ["And the Word became flesh and tabernacled among us." This is the [[Incarnation]], the new event. His glory is that of the "unique" (*monogenes*) Son from the Father. He is from the Father in a way that is different from prophets or adopted children.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-25.mp3#t=1:47:35)
*   **[[John]] 1:16-17:** ["Grace after grace." The Law given through [[Moses]] was a grace. The [[Incarnation]], where grace and truth "came" or "arrived" through [[Jesus Christ]], is a new and greater grace.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-25.mp3#t=1:58:35)
*   **[[John]] 1:18:** ["No one has ever seen God (the Father)... the unique God/Son, who is at the Father's side, he has made him known." This confirms that all [[Old Testament]] theophanies, all encounters where God was "seen," were encounters with the Son, the Word of the Lord, who reveals the unseen Father.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-25.mp3#t=2:02:40)
