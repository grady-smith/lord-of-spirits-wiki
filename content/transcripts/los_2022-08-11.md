### [[1 Enoch]]
*   **[[1 Enoch]] 48:4-5**: [00:00:00](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-08-11.mp3#t=00:00:00) Quoted in the introduction as the opening epigraph for the episode. The text describes the Lord of Spirits as a source of stability for the righteous, a light for the nations, and the object of universal worship. It sets a messianic and universal tone for the episode.

### [[Deuteronomy]]
*   **[[Deuteronomy]] 2:5**: Referenced in the context of land allotments given to [[Abraham]]'s other descendants. [[God]] commands [[Israel]] not to provoke the sons of Esau (Edomites), stating, "you don't get a single foot of it" because their land was given to them as a possession.
*   **[[Deuteronomy]] 2:9**: Referenced in the context of land allotments. [[God]] commands Israel not to harass or contend with the Moabites, the descendants of Lot, as their land has been given to them as a possession.
*   **[[Deuteronomy]] 2:19**: Referenced in the context of land allotments. [[God]] commands Israel not to harass the Ammonites, also descendants of Lot, because their land has been given to them as their inheritance.
*   **[[Deuteronomy]] 4:19**: This verse warns Israel not to worship the sun, moon, and stars, which God has "allotted to all the peoples under the whole heaven." This is presented as the flip side of [[Deuteronomy]] 32, explaining that while [[God]] assigned angelic beings to govern the nations, Israel was commanded not to worship them. This is linked to the [[Divine Council - Unseen Realm]].
*   **[[Deuteronomy]] 30:12**: Referenced to explain that the purpose of ascending to heaven in ancient thought was often to bring something down. [[Moses]] asks rhetorically if the commandments are in heaven, requiring someone to ascend and bring them down, showing this was a conceptual possibility in their worldview.
*   **[[Deuteronomy]] 32:8**: This is a foundational verse for the episode's topic. [[Fr. Stephen]] explains how [[God]], after the Tower of Babel, "fixed the borders of the peoples according to the number of the sons of [[God]]" (or "angels of [[God]]" in the LXX), assigning angelic rulers to the 70 nations. This act is central to understanding the [[Divine Council - Unseen Realm]] and the events of Babel.
*   **[[Deuteronomy]] 32:17**: Quoted to show that the nations fell into idolatry, sacrificing "to demons that were no gods." This explains the corruption that occurred after [[God]] assigned angelic rulers; the nations began to worship those beings and other demons instead of [[God]]. This is connected to the [[Divine Council - Unseen Realm]].
*   **[[Deuteronomy]] 32:43**: Quoted to show God's ultimate plan of redemption for all nations, not just Israel. The verse calls on the heavens and the gods (or angels) to rejoice with [[God]] as He avenges and cleanses the land, pointing to the eschatological restoration of all creation. This relates to [[Theosis]] and the defeat of the fallen council.

### [[Genesis]]
*   **Genesis 10:** Referred to as the "Table of Nations," which lists 70 nations descended from [[Noah]]'s sons. This number corresponds to the 70 "sons of [[God]]" in [[Deuteronomy]] 32, to whom these nations were allotted for governance. This is a key part of the [[Divine Council - Unseen Realm]] discussion.
*   **[[Genesis]] 10:8-9**: These verses introduce Nimrod, a descendant of Ham, as "the first on earth to be a mighty man (*gibbor*)." This term connects him to the giants of [[Genesis]] 6, and his name is linguistically linked to the Mesopotamian god Ninurta, identifying him as a giant figure central to the rebellion at Babel.
*   **Genesis 11:** The central text for the episode, describing the building of the city and tower of Babel. The fathers explain this event as humanity's third great fall, an attempt to create a unified world empire and "bring [[God]] down" to serve their purposes, which led to [[God]] confusing their languages and scattering them.
*   **[[Genesis]] 11:4**: Quoted to explain the motivation of the builders of Babel: "Come, let us build ourselves a city and a tower with its top in the heavens, and let us make a name for ourselves." This is interpreted as an act of hubris and an attempt to unify humanity for its own glory, in opposition to [[God]]. This is a core part of the [[The Fall of Man]] discussion.
*   **[[Genesis]] 11:9**: This verse provides the biblical etymology for "Babel," connecting it to the Hebrew verb *bilbel* ("to confuse"). [[Fr. Stephen]] argues this is a deliberate theological commentary, reframing [[Babylon]]'s self-proclaimed identity as "Gate of the Gods" (*Bāb-ili*) into "Place of Confusion."
*   **[[Genesis]] 15:5**: This verse contains God's promise to [[Abraham]] that his descendants will be as numerous as the stars of heaven. This is interpreted not just quantitatively but qualitatively, meaning they will become like the stars—angelic, divine beings—which is a key component of [[Theosis]].
*   **[[Genesis]] 22:17**: Reiterates the promise to Abraham, stating his offspring will be like the stars of heaven and the sand on the seashore, and that they will "possess the gate of his enemies." This promise is about defeating the hostile spiritual powers and taking their place as rulers in [[God]]'s council, which is central to the concept of [[Theosis]].

### [[Joshua]]
*   **[[Joshua]] 11:23**: This verse states that "[[Joshua]] took the whole land" as an inheritance for Israel, and "the land had rest from war." This is cited to show the fulfillment of the *sign* of the land promise made to [[Abraham]], which points to the greater, spiritual inheritance.
*   **[[Joshua]] 21:43-45**: This passage confirms the fulfillment of the land promise, stating "Not one word of all the good promises that the Lord had made to the house of Israel had failed; all came to pass." This reinforces that the physical land allotment was completed, allowing the focus to shift to the ultimate spiritual fulfillment of the promise in [[Theosis]].

### [[Romans]]
*   **[[Romans]] 10:6**: St. Paul quotes Deuteronomy, asking, "Do not say in your heart, 'Who will ascend into heaven?'" and adds the interpretation, "that is, to bring Christ down." This is used to explain the sin of Babel: it was not a literal attempt to reach heaven, but a spiritual attempt to control and manipulate the divine, to bring [[God]] down to their level. This connects to [[The Fall of Man]].

### [[Daniel]]
*   **[[Daniel]] 12:3**: Quoted to support the qualitative interpretation of the promise to Abraham: "And those who are wise shall shine like the brightness of the sky above; and those who turn many to righteousness, like the stars forever and ever." This shows that becoming "like the stars" means participating in divine glory and righteousness, a core element of [[Theosis]].

### [[Revelation]]
*   **[[Revelation]] 3:21**: Christ promises, "The one who conquers, I will grant him to sit with me on my throne, as I also conquered and sat down with my Father on his throne." This verse is central to the theme of [[Theosis]], showing that the ultimate destiny of the faithful is not just to be saved from sin but to co-reign with [[Christ]], fulfilling the role of the [[Divine Council]].
*   **[[Revelation]] 4:4**: Describes the vision of "twenty-four [[Thrones]], and seated on the thrones were twenty-four elders, clothed in white garments, with golden crowns on their heads." These 24 elders represent humanity redeemed and enthroned, participating in the governance of the cosmos, which is a key image of [[Theosis]].
*   **[[Revelation]] 5:10**: The four living creatures and 24 elders sing a new song to the Lamb, saying He has made them "a kingdom and priests to our God, and they shall reign on the earth." This again highlights the theme of redeemed humanity taking on a royal and priestly role, reigning with [[God]], which is the fulfillment of [[Theosis]].
*   **[[Revelation]] 16:19**: Mentioned as an "ominous statement where God remembers Babylon." This shows that [[Babylon]] remains a symbol of worldly empire in opposition to [[God]] throughout Scripture, awaiting its final judgment.
*   **Revelation 17-18:** Referenced as containing the full depiction of Babylon's final judgment, personified as the "Harlot of Babylon." This section of [[Revelation]] details the fall of the ultimate worldly empire, which is the typological fulfillment of the original Babel.
*   **[[Revelation]] 20:4**: [[John]] sees thrones and "the souls of those who had been beheaded for the testimony of [[Jesus]]...They came to life and reigned with Christ for a thousand years." This passage powerfully illustrates the destiny of the [[Martyrs]] and saints, who conquer through their faithfulness and are granted the authority to judge and reign with [[Christ]], embodying the promise of [[Theosis]].


### The Fall of Man
*   **The Tower of Babel:** The episode frames the Tower of Babel as the third great fall of humanity. The sin was not simply building a tall structure but a spiritual rebellion aimed at creating a unified human empire for its own glory ("let us make a name for ourselves"). This act sought to control the divine by "bringing [[God]] down" through idolatrous practices, rather than ascending to Him.

*   **The Nature of Babel's Sin:** The sin is explained through a parallel in [[Romans]] 10, where St. Paul clarifies that "ascending to heaven" is a metaphor for "bringing Christ down." The builders of Babel were attempting to seize divine power and authority for themselves, thereby repeating the pattern of [[Adam]] and [[Eve]]'s fall and the transgression of the [[Watchers]]. This rebellion led to [[God]] confusing their languages and scattering them across the earth.

*   **Eusebius's Commentary:** [[Eusebius of Caesarea]]'s *Demonstration of the [[Gospel]]* is quoted at length to explain the consequences of this fall. He describes how, after [[God]] scattered the nations, they fell away from the true [[God]] and were enslaved by "earthy powers and evil spirits," including the [[DEVIL]] himself, who established a rival empire of wickedness. [[Christ]]'s [[Incarnation]] is presented as the divine intervention to overthrow this demonic kingdom and restore humanity.

### [[Divine Council]] - [[Unseen Realm]]
*   **The Allotment of the Nations:** The episode centers on the interpretation of [[Deuteronomy]] 32:8, which states that after the Tower of Babel, God "fixed the borders of the peoples according to the number of the sons of God." This act established a new cosmic order where the 70 nations listed in [[Genesis]] 10 were placed under the governance of 70 angelic beings from the divine council, while [[God]] reserved Israel for Himself.

*   **The Fall of the Nations' Rulers:** The nations under these angelic rulers fell into idolatry, as described in [[Deuteronomy]] 32:17, where they "sacrificed to demons that were no gods." The nations began to worship their angelic guardians and other demonic spirits, leading to widespread spiritual corruption. The angels assigned to them either fell by accepting this worship or were unable to prevent it, and this rebellion of the council is a major theme.

*   **Parallel Traditions (Plato):** [[Fr. Andrew]] reads from Plato's *Critias* and *Laws*, which present a parallel tradition. Plato describes a "golden age" when the gods received different regions of the earth "by allotment" and governed humanity as gentle shepherds, a memory that echoes the biblical account of the divine council's initial, uncorrupted governance.

*   **Patristic and Early Christian Interpretation:** St. Dionysius the Areopagite and Eusebius of Caesarea are quoted to show this understanding was present in the early Church. Dionysius identifies [[Michael]] as the prince of Israel, while Eusebius details how the nations fell under the sway of "the mighty demon" ([[Satan]]) and his powers after abandoning their angelic guardians. [[Christ]]'s coming is the campaign to reclaim these nations and defeat the rebellious members of the council.

### [[Theosis]]
*   **The Promise to Abraham:** The promise that Abraham's descendants would be "like the stars of heaven" ([[Genesis]] 15:5) is interpreted not just quantitatively but qualitatively. Citing [[Daniel]] 12:3, the fathers explain that this means the righteous will shine with divine glory, becoming divine beings like the stars (a term for angels). This is the ultimate goal of the [[Covenant]].

*   **Reigning with Christ:** The fulfillment of this promise is seen in the New Testament, particularly in [[Revelation]]. Passages like [[Revelation]] 3:21 ("The one who conquers, I will grant him to sit with me on my throne") and [[Revelation]] 20:4 show that the destiny of the faithful is to co-reign with [[Christ]], replacing the fallen angelic rulers and becoming the new members of the [[Divine Council - Unseen Realm]].

*   **Conquering the Self:** [[Fr. Stephen]] concludes by framing [[Theosis]] as a form of conquest—not of earthly empires, but of the self. To "conquer" in the Christian sense is to achieve self-mastery over passions, pride, and sin, thereby purging oneself of demonic influence and becoming a vessel for [[God]]'s grace. This personal battle is the way to participate in [[Christ]]'s victory and ultimately share in His reign.

### [[Nephilim]] - [[Giants]]
*   **Nimrod as a Gibbor:** The episode identifies Nimrod, the founder of Babylon, as the "first on earth to be a mighty man (*gibbor*)" ([[Genesis]] 10:8). This term directly connects him to the [[Nephilim]] and giants of [[Genesis]] 6:4, establishing the rebellion at Babel as being led by a giant-figure.

*   **The Amorites and Giant-Clans:** The Amorites, the people group of Hammurabi who conquered Babylon, are identified as a "catch-all" term for giant-clans in the Old Testament. This connection reinforces the theme that the land promises to [[Abraham]]'s descendants (Israel, Edom, Moab, Ammon) involved displacing giant-clans who had inhabited the lands.

*   **Earthly and Demonic Natures:** In his commentary, Eusebius of Caesarea distinguishes between the fallen angels ("demons") and the "earthly" spirits that plague humanity. [[Fr. Stephen]] clarifies that this "earthly" nature refers to the spirits of the dead giants (*gigantes*), whose name literally means "earth-born" in Greek. This shows the patristic understanding of the ongoing threat posed by the spirits of the deceased [[Nephilim]].

### [[Salvation]] and [[Martyrdom]]
*   **The Crystallized State and Prayers for the Dead:** In response to a caller's question, Fr. Stephen clarifies the state of the soul after death. He explains that while the departed can no longer repent or change their fundamental disposition towards [[God]] on their own, the final sorting of "sheep and goats" does not happen until the [[Last Judgment]]. Our prayers can still aid them because [[Christ]]'s relationship to time is different from ours; our prayers now can be part of what [[God]] uses to bring someone to repentance in their life.

*   **Conquering and Reigning with Christ:** [[Revelation]] 20:4 is quoted, showing that martyrs who were beheaded for their testimony "came to life and reigned with Christ." This highlights martyrdom not as a defeat but as the ultimate victory and the means by which saints participate in [[Christ]]'s conquest over the demonic powers and are enthroned with Him. This is the ultimate expression of [[Salvation]] as [[Theosis]].
