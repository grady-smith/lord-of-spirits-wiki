# [[Ritual Purity]]

## Introduction

[[Fr. Andrew]] and [[Fr. Stephen]] discuss the theology of ritual purification from the Old Testament, making a critical distinction between moral uncleanness (sin) and ritual or ceremonial uncleanness (being "common" rather than "holy"). They explain how this understanding of purification continues into the New Covenant and is reflected in the practices of the [[Orthodox Church]], particularly in the prayers for a mother and child after birth and in the preparation required to approach the holy things of [[God]].

## The Two Types of Uncleanness

[[Fr. Stephen]] distinguishes between two kinds of uncleanness in the [[Torah]]: moral uncleanness and ritual/ceremonial uncleanness. Moral uncleanness is directly tied to sin and is avoidable in theory, while ritual uncleanness is often an unavoidable part of common, everyday life.

[Listen from 08:46](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-01-27.mp3#t=08:46)
> **[[Fr. Stephen]]:** There are two different kinds of uncleanness in the Old Testament, in the [[Torah]], really, is where we're starting. And they are not the same thing. The first is the one that is usually the cause of conflation. That is, there is moral uncleanness. You do something evil that renders you unclean.

[Listen from 23:55](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-01-27.mp3#t=23:55)
> **[[Fr. Stephen]]:** Moral uncleanness is avoidable, at least in theory, because it involves just not sinning. So it is theoretically possible not to commit any given sin, therefore it is theoretically possible not to commit any.

Ritual uncleanness, however, is largely unavoidable and is not a moral category. It concerns being in a "common" state rather than a "holy" one, and purification is required to transition from the common to approach the holy.

## Holy vs. Common

The core distinction is not between good and evil, but between what is holy (set apart for [[God]]) and what is common (part of everyday life). Ritual uncleanness is a state of being "common" and requires purification to become "holy" before approaching [[God]].

[Listen from 10:27](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-01-27.mp3#t=10:27)
> **[[Fr. Andrew]]:** So it says, "You are to distinguish between the holy and the common and between the unclean and the clean." So you've got this parallel there: holy and common, unclean and clean.
>
> **[[Fr. Stephen]]:** Right. And so the idea is here, the holy thing is the clean thing. And the unclean thing is the common thing. That's a very different distinction than good and bad or good and evil.

## The Sin of Approaching the Holy While Unclean

The sin is not in becoming ritually unclean, but in refusing to be purified and attempting to approach the holy things of [[God]] while in that state. This defiles the sanctuary and is an act of irreverence.

[Listen from 41:21](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-01-27.mp3#t=41:21)
> **[[Fr. Stephen]]:** It can become a sin issue if you attempt to approach [[God]] for worship after doing one of these things without cleansing, without washing, without purifying yourself. The refusal to cleanse yourself properly afterwards and the approach to [[God]], then it becomes a sin issue because you've brought uncleanness into the presence of a holy [[God]]. You failed to consecrate yourself; you failed to set yourself apart before worship.

## [[Atonement]] and the Cleansing of Creation

The discussion connects ritual purity to the [[Day of Atonement]], explaining that its purpose was to cleanse the [[Tabernacle]] and the people from the accumulated "taint" of sin that was not covered by the regular sacrifices. This concept is extended to [[Christ]]'s sacrifice, which is described as the atonement not just for human sins, but for the cleansing of the entire created world.

[Listen from 52:25](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-01-27.mp3#t=52:25)
> **[[Fr. Stephen]]:** We talked about there how the [[Day of Atonement]] was an additional annual ritual, additional to the regular cycle of sin offerings and peace offerings, etc. that were going on in the [[Tabernacle]] all the time. The [[Day of Atonement]] was concerned with sort of the taint, the uncleanness left behind by the sins of the people over the course of the year.

[Listen from 53:05](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-01-27.mp3#t=53:05)
> **[[Fr. Stephen]]:** [Referring to [[1 John]] 2:2] [[Christ]] is the atonement for our sins, and not only for our sins, but also for the whole world. And how that's talking about the world, the creation, the material creation, is cleansed and purified by [[Christ]]'s blood in sort of an eschatological [[Day of Atonement]]. That the created order is cleansed from the stain, the effects of man's sin.

## The Continuing Need for Purification

Even in the New Covenant, there is still a distinction between the holy and the common. Christians live in the "common world" but must prepare themselves to enter the holy presence of [[God]], especially in worship. This preparation includes repentance, cleansing, and setting oneself apart.

[Listen from 58:25](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2022-01-27.mp3#t=58:25)
> **[[Fr. Stephen]]:** The common remains. There are still things that are holy and things that are common... We live our whole world still, in the new covenant, in the New Testament, today as Christians, we still live in the world of the common, right, the common world, the unclean world. And then we come at certain times to approach [[God]], to present ourselves to [[God]]. We come to encounter the holy. And when we come to encounter the holy, we prepare ourselves.
