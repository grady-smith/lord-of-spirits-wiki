## On Ecclesiastical Authority and Obedience

### Question from Anthony: Obedience of Roman Catholic Priests

[Listen from [05:04](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-10-26.mp3#t=05:04) Anthony from Memphis, Tennessee, asks for justification for a Roman Catholic priest being disobedient to his bishop. He references a previous episode where [[Fr. Stephen]] mentioned a priest in Louisiana who baptized against his bishop's edicts. He contrasts this with the Orthodox understanding of obedience to one's bishop.

**[[Fr. Stephen]]'s Response:**
[Fr. Stephen begins his response](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-10-26.mp3#t=06:40) by stating that, from an Orthodox perspective, salvation for those outside the Orthodox Church hinges on a degree of disobedience to their own ecclesiastical bodies. He argues:
> "If you are in some kind of ecclesial body or religious body other than the Orthodox Church, to a certain extent, and a differing extent depending on the group, your salvation hinges on you being disobedient... The reason why, I think, people should become Orthodox is that the Orthodox Church has correctly guarded and preserved the way of salvation. To varying degrees, other religious bodies have not perfectly preserved it. Therefore, the guides and authorities in those bodies are not going to 100% lead you down the correct path." [Listen from [06:42](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-10-26.mp3#t=06:42)

He calls this a "blessed inconsistency," where individuals in other traditions do not follow the problematic parts of their doctrine, which is a good thing.

**[[Fr. Andrew]]'s Response:**
[Fr. Andrew connects this to the American suspicion of authority](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-10-26.mp3#t=09:12) and clarifies the Orthodox position on obedience. He references [[Acts 5:29]], where the apostles declare, "We must obey God rather than men." This isn't a declaration of anti-authoritarianism, but a recognition that God's authority is supreme. The apostles themselves were establishing an authoritative structure, not advocating for individual interpretation against the Church. The legitimacy of any authority must be rooted in the authority of the apostles, which comes from [[Christ]].

### Question from [[Jonah]]: Obedience During Iconoclasm

[Listen from [10:12](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-10-26.mp3#t=10:12) [[Jonah]] asks about episcopal authority in the context of iconoclasm, mentioning the Council of Hieria and the resistance of monks like [[St. Stephen the Younger]]. He questions how to reconcile the command to obey one's bishop with instances where saints disobeyed heretical authorities, and how one can discern between guidance from the [[Holy Spirit]] and personal pride.

**[[Fr. Stephen]]'s Response:**
[[Fr. Stephen]] emphasizes the gravity of such a situation:
> "They were executed... They went to their deaths at the hands of the authorities." [Listen from [11:47](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-10-26.mp3#t=11:47)

He points out a critical distinction: these saints did not try to set up a separate, parallel church hierarchy. Their struggle was *for* the legitimate episcopal see, not to create a new one, which would be schism. He warns against modern hypotheticals where individuals fantasize about being heroic stand-alone confessors.
> "You are not St. Maximos the Confessor. You are not St. Stephen the New... God has not called you to make a heroic stand against the whole church which has fallen into error. And if you start thinking that way at all, that is what our Slavic friends call *prelest*. That is spiritual delusion." [Listen from [14:04](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-10-26.mp3#t=14:04)

This kind of thinking, he argues, comes from pride and is not the calling of the average Christian, whose vocation is to live "a calm and peaceful life in all godliness and sanctity." [[Prelest - Spiritual Delusion]]

### Question from [[James]]: Populist Disobedience

[Listen from [17:16](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-10-26.mp3#t=17:16) [[James]] asks about a story of an iconoclast bishop being driven out of town by his parishioners, questioning if this represents a legitimate "grassroots" exercise of authority or the [[Holy Spirit]] working through the laity.

**[[Fr. Stephen]]'s Response:**
[Fr. Stephen uses the example of Isidore of Kiev at the Council of Florence](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-10-26.mp3#t=18:46) to debunk romanticized "populist uprising" narratives. He then explains that God's judgment can be enacted through people who are not themselves righteous.
> "The judgment of God comes at the hands of people who are not necessarily doing good and holy things. Like when the Assyrians came in and carried off the ten northern tribes of Israel... This is interpreted in the scripture as being the judgment of God. The scriptures do not say that the Assyrians are good and holy and righteous people." [Listen from [22:07](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-10-26.mp3#t=22:07)

Therefore, a mob driving out a heretical bishop does not sanctify the mob's actions. He reiterates that [[Orthodoxy]] is a lived reality, not an abstract system of doctrines to be judged from the outside. Constantly evaluating one's spiritual father from a critical distance undermines the very relationship of spiritual guidance.

### Question from Milan: The Census in [[Numbers]]

[Listen from [38:36](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-10-26.mp3#t=38:36) Milan from Toronto asks about the large census numbers in the book of [[Numbers]], which seem to imply a population of over a million people in the desert. He asks if these numbers should be taken literally.

**[[Fr. Stephen]]'s Response:**
[Fr. Stephen explains that ancient numbering systems were complex and often symbolic](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-10-26.mp3#t=40:01). The Hebrew word for "thousand" (`eleph`) can also mean a clan, a military unit, or simply a large group or "heap."
> "When you read 20,000, that's really saying there's like 20 clans of people... That big group of people might have been 150, or 132, or 187." [Listen from [44:21](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-10-26.mp3#t=44:21)

The numbers in the census are not meant to be a precise, literal headcount but rather to convey the relative sizes and strengths of the tribes. The purpose of the text is not demographic accuracy but to show the ordering of Israel and the continuity of God's people from the generation that left Egypt to the one that entered the Promised Land.

### Question from David: A Hindu Ritual and the [[Nephilim]]

[Listen from [70:01](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-10-26.mp3#t=70:01) David from Huntsville, Alabama, asks if a Hindu Tantric ritual called Maithuna, in which a couple invokes deities to embody them, is a continuation of a [[Nephilim - Giants]] ritual.

**[[Fr. Stephen]]'s Response:**
[Fr. Stephen explains that while the ritual involves invoking deities during a sexual act, it differs significantly from the Nephilim account](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-10-26.mp3#t=72:15). A key goal of the Maithuna ritual is to avoid conception, whereas the [[Nephilim]] rituals were specifically aimed at producing giant offspring to be ruled by kings. Not every pagan sexual ritual is a [[Nephilim]] ritual. To qualify, it must involve a king or ruler and be intended to produce a giant heir.

### Question from Zach: The Passover and the [[Eucharist]]

[Listen from [88:34](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-10-26.mp3#t=88:34) Zach from Joplin, Missouri, asks why the early Christians, if they understood the [[Eucharist]] as the fulfillment of the Passover, began celebrating it weekly as a "thank offering" rather than annually.

**[[Fr. Stephen]]'s Response:**
[Fr. Stephen clarifies that they were, in fact, celebrating the Passover weekly](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-10-26.mp3#t=90:09). There was a deep connection in [[Second Temple]] Judaism between the weekly Sabbath and the annual Passover.
> "The assumption that they weren't doing that... no, that's exactly what they were doing. They were commemorating [[Christ]]'s resurrection... Every Sunday is [[Pascha]]. [[Pascha]] is the [[Pascha]] of Paschas." [Listen from [90:25](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-10-26.mp3#t=90:25)

The weekly cycle on earth was seen as participating in the eternal reality of God's rest and the Passover. The early Christians understood every Sunday (the Lord's Day) as a participation in [[Christ]]'s [[Pascha]], making the [[Eucharist]] a weekly Passover celebration.

### Question from [[Matthew]]: Sinlessness of [[Christ]] vs. the Saints

[Listen from 1[36:54](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-10-26.mp3#t=136:54) [[Matthew]] from Longmont, Colorado, asks how the Church can say that [[Christ]] alone is without sin, while also saying that the [[Theotokos]] and [[St. John]] the Forerunner were sinless.

**[[Fr. Stephen]]'s Response:**
[Fr. Stephen explains the distinction](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-10-26.mp3#t=137:20). To be "without sin" in the unique way [[Christ]] is means to be free from the power and influence of sin as a force. [[Christ]], being God, is impassible to sin; it has no effect on Him. For the saints, "not committing any sins" means they did not personally transgress God's commandments.
> "There is a difference between saying someone is without sin, which we say [[Christ]] alone is without sin... and saying someone did not commit any sins... [[Christ]] is impassible... sin is not at work on him because it can't be. Whereas [[St. John]] the Forerunner is still mortal. He died. The Theotokos is mortal. She died." [Listen from 1[37:22](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2023-10-26.mp3#t=137:22)

The saints, while not committing personal sins, were still mortal and subject to the cosmic effects of sin at work in the world, which [[Christ]] was not.
