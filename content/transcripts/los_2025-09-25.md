## Core Concepts

### [[Divine Council]] - [[Unseen Realm]]
* **[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-09-25.mp3#t=1:16:00):** [[Fr. Stephen]] explains that Babylonian religion, like other ancient systems, was not a simple pantheon but a complex web of local deities that were later synthesized. The chief [[god]] of [[Babylon]], [[Marduk]], rose to prominence as the city did. His original name was Amar-Utu, "the calf of Utu," linking him to the sun [[god]] Shamash. Through texts like the *Enuma Elish*, Marduk is depicted as overthrowing older gods like Tiamat and Apsu, eventually being acclaimed as the king of the gods and absorbing the titles of other deities like Enlil and Bel, receiving 50 names in total.
* **[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-09-25.mp3#t=1:28:12):** In [[Daniel 4]], [[Nebuchadnezzar]]'s dream features a "watcher, a holy one," who decrees his judgment. [[Fr. Stephen]] identifies this as a rare canonical reference to the class of [[angels]] known as [[Watchers]], who are part of [[God]]'s [[divine council]]. The decree is from "the [[watchers]]" and "the holy ones," showing the council acting to execute the judgment of the Most High, demonstrating that even the king of [[Babylon]] is subject to this heavenly authority.
* **[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-09-25.mp3#t=1:40:37):** Following his madness and restoration in [[Daniel 4]], [[Nebuchadnezzar]] praises the [[God]] of Israel as the "King of heaven" who rules over both the "host of heaven" (the lesser gods/[[angels]]) and the "inhabitants of the earth." [[Fr. Stephen]] points out that this is [[Nebuchadnezzar]]'s confession that [[Yahweh]] is not just *a* [[god]], but the [[God]] over all other spiritual beings, including the gods of [[Babylon]] he had previously worshipped.

### [[Nephilim]] - [[Giants]]
* **[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-09-25.mp3#t=1:25:31):** [[Fr. Stephen]] identifies the Amorites, who founded the Old Babylonian Empire under Hammurabi, as being biblically identified with [[giants]]. The narrative of the Tower of Babel in [[Genesis 11]] is spiritually connected to the rise and [[fall]] of this Amorite-founded civilization during the Bronze Age, which claimed to possess pre-flood wisdom. This connects the project of [[Babylon]] to the pre-flood world of the [[giants]].

### The [[Fall]] of Man
* **[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-09-25.mp3#t=1:40:00):** [[Fr. Stephen]] argues that the story of the Tower of Babel in [[Genesis 11]] is a spiritual account of the historical Bronze Age Collapse. He draws parallels: a globalized civilization (the Old Babylonian Empire) centered in [[Babylon]], building an unfinished ziggurat (Etemenanki), which claimed to possess pre-flood wisdom associated with [[giants]] (the Amorites). [[God]]'s judgment, which confused their languages and scattered them, corresponds to the historical collapse of this civilization, which broke down trade, communication, and imperial unity across the ancient world.

### Salvation and Martyrdom
* **[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-09-25.mp3#t=1:53:05):** In response to a caller's question about the cycle of victimhood and revenge, [[Fr. Stephen]] contrasts the way of the Zealots with the way of the Maccabean [[martyrs]] and [[Christ]]. He explains that the canonical scriptures present a path of overcoming evil with good, not repaying violence with violence. The [[martyrs]] were willing to die for what is true and good, not to kill for it, which is the path that ultimately transforms the world, as seen in the [[Christian]] transformation of the [[Roman Empire]].

### Priesthood and [[Tabernacle]]
* **[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-09-25.mp3#t=54:30):** [[Fr. Stephen]] explains that in the ancient world, the king was also the [[high priest]]. He notes that before becoming emperor, [[Nebuchadnezzar]] II held the official title of "[[high priest]] of Ea in Uruk." This demonstrates the integration of religious and political authority, where the monarch was seen as the primary intermediary with the divine realm and responsible for the cult.
* **[Listen from here](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2025-09-25.mp3#t=58:51):** As part of his effort to restore [[Babylon]]'s past glory, [[Nebuchadnezzar]] undertook massive building projects. These included renovating the Esagila, the ancient [[temple]] of the [[god]] [[Marduk]], and completing the Etemenanki, a massive ziggurat dedicated to Marduk that had lain unfinished for centuries. These structures served as the central cultic sites of the Neo-Babylonian Empire.
