## Introduction

[[Fr. Andrew]] and [[Fr. Stephen]] begin a four-part series on the Christology of the Old Testament. [This first episode focuses on the figure of the Angel of the Lord](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=01:32:00), a being mentioned dozens of times in Scripture whose identity is often unclear to modern Christians. The hosts will explore who this figure is, whether he is an angel, a symbol, or the Son of [[God]] Himself, and what his role reveals about [[Christ]] before the [[Incarnation]].

The introduction to the episode includes a reading from [1 Enoch 48:4-5](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=00:18), describing the Son of Man who is also called the "Lord of Spirits."

## The Identity of the [[Angel of the Lord]]

### Linguistic Breakdown

[[Fr. Stephen]] breaks down the phrase "the [[Angel of the Lord]]" to establish its meaning from the original languages. [Listen from 02:20](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=02:20)

1.  **"The"**: The Hebrew uses the definite article (*ha*), indicating a specific, singular being. This is not just "an" angel of the Lord but *the* [[Angel of the Lord]]. [[Fr. Stephen]] contrasts this with passages like [[Matthew 1:20]], where "an angel of the Lord" appears to Joseph, versus [[Matthew 1:24]], where the text refers back to "the angel." [The Greek article in Matthew is not a definite article in the same way the Hebrew *ha* is](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=04:34), but the Hebrew phrase *ha-mal'akh [[Yahweh]]* consistently points to one particular being.

2.  **"Angel"**: The Hebrew word *mal'akh* and the Greek word *angelos* both mean "messenger." It describes a job or a role, not a species. In the ancient world, this term was used for spiritual beings who served pagan gods as well, such as in [[Targums - Enochic Literature|Ugaritic]] texts. [Listen from 05:34](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=05:34)

3.  **"of the Lord"**: This refers to [[Yahweh]], the [[God]] of Israel. The phrasing can be interpreted in two ways:
    *   **Possessive**: "[[Yahweh]]'s angel," meaning an angel who belongs to or serves [[Yahweh]].
    *   **Descriptive/Attributive**: "The [[Yahweh]] angel," which implies an identification with [[Yahweh]] Himself. This is supported by Second [[Temple]] literature, such as the Apocalypse of [[Abraham]], which names an angelic figure "Yahoel," meaning "[[Yahweh]] [[God]]." [Listen from 09:27](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=09:27) This second interpretation suggests that the [[Angel of the Lord]] is a divine figure.

### The Angel and the Law

Scripture indicates that the Law of [[Moses]] was mediated through angels. 
- [[Acts 7:53]] says the Israelites received the law "as delivered by angels."
- [[Hebrews 2:2]] refers to "the message declared by angels." 

[This tradition is not explicitly detailed in the book of Exodus but was a commonly held belief in the Second Temple period, as shown in the writings of Josephus and the New Testament authors](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=20:26). The book of [[Jubilees]] identifies the specific figure who gave the Law and the account of creation to [[Moses]] as the "Angel of the Presence."

In [[Galatians 3:19-20]], St. [[Paul]] refers to the law being put in place "through angels by an intermediary." [He then clarifies that this intermediary is not separate from God, saying, "Now an intermediary implies more than one, but God is one."](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=31:42) [[Fr. Stephen]] explains this means that [[Christ]] gave the [[Torah]] to manage sin until He Himself came in the flesh.

### Is the [[Angel of the Lord]] a Post-Resurrectional [[Christ]]?

A caller from Ireland, Kyle, asks if the [[Angel of the Lord]] is to be understood as the second person of the [[Trinity]], [[God]] the Son, in a post-resurrectional body. [Fr. Stephen answers with a definitive "Yes."](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=40:32)

He elaborates after the break, explaining that since "heaven" is not a physical place with linear time, the eternal reality of [[Christ]]'s resurrected human body is not bound by our sequence of events. [Christ is "the same yesterday and today and forever" (Hebrews 13:8), and His incarnation occurred "without change or alteration."](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=42:32) The reality of who [[Christ]] is, including his resurrected humanity, enters into our experience at different points in our history.

## The Narrative Arc of the [[Angel of the Lord]]

[[Fr. Andrew]] and [[Fr. Stephen]] outline a narrative arc for the [[Angel of the Lord]], showing him primarily as a military commander who leads [[God]]'s people, protects them, and executes judgment.

### The Commission ([[Exodus]] 23)

[In [[Exodus 23:20-23]], God announces that He is sending an Angel before Israel to lead them into the promised land.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=50:52) This Angel is unique because [[God]] says, "My name is in him," signifying that the Angel carries [[God]]'s own identity and authority. Israel is commanded to obey him, as he will not pardon their transgression. This mission is explicitly one of conquest—a *gigantomachy*—to blot out the pagan giant clans like the Amorites and Hittites by destroying their religious practices and altars.

### The Commander in Action
The Angel is depicted as a divine warrior and protector throughout Israel's early history.

- **The Red Sea ([[Exodus 14:19-20]])**: The "angel of [[God]]" who was leading Israel moves to their rear, along with the pillar of cloud, to stand between them and the pursuing Egyptian army, protecting them through the night. [This is presented as a manifestation of the Trinity: the voice of the Father, the Angel (Christ), and the pillar of cloud (the Holy Spirit).](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=1:01:25)
- **Hagar ([[Genesis 16]])**: [The very first appearance of the Angel of the Lord in Scripture is to Hagar in the wilderness](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=1:04:38), where he shows her mercy and promises that her son Ishmael will become a great nation.
- **The [[Sacrifice]] of [[Isaac]] ([[Genesis 22]])**: The [[Angel of the Lord]] calls from heaven to stop [[Abraham]] from sacrificing [[Isaac]], providing a ram instead. [Listen from 1:05:42](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=1:05:42).
- **Balaam's Donkey ([[Numbers 22]])**: The [[Angel of the Lord]] stands in the path of Balaam with a drawn sword, visible only to Balaam's donkey at first, to prevent him from cursing Israel. [Listen from 1:06:29](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=1:06:29).
- **Gideon ([[Judges 6]])**: The [[Angel of the Lord]] appears to Gideon, and the text seamlessly switches between "the angel" and "[[Yahweh]]" speaking. [Gideon offers a meal, which the Angel consumes with fire from his staff, revealing his divine nature.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=1:00:10)
- **Samson's Birth ([[Judges 13]])**: The Angel announces Samson's birth to his parents. [When asked his name, the Angel replies, "Why do you ask my name, seeing it is wonderful?" This directly connects to the Messianic title "Wonderful Counselor" in Isaiah 9:6.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=1:08:16)
- **[[David]]'s Census ([[2 Samuel 24]] & [[1 Chronicles 21]])**: After [[David]] sinfully takes a census, the [[Angel of the Lord]] is seen over Jerusalem with a drawn sword, ready to bring a plague as judgment. [Listen from 1:09:48](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=1:09:48).

### The Confrontation at Jericho ([[Joshua]] 5)

[The midpoint of this narrative arc occurs in [[Joshua 5:13-15]], just before the battle of Jericho.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=1:09:25) [[Joshua]] encounters a man with a drawn sword and asks if he is for Israel or their adversaries. The figure responds, "No; but I am the commander of the army of the Lord. Now I have come."

This encounter contains three key identifiers linking this figure to the [[Angel of the Lord]] and to [[Yahweh]] Himself:
1.  **The Drawn Sword**: This imagery connects him to his appearances to Balaam and [[David]].
2.  **Receiving Worship**: [[Joshua]] falls on his face and worships, and the figure does not correct him, unlike created angels in other parts of Scripture.
3.  **Holy Ground**: The commander tells [[Joshua]], "Take off your sandals from your feet, for the place where you are standing is holy." This is the exact command given by the [[Angel of the Lord]]/[[God]] from the burning bush in [[Exodus 3]].

### The Departure ([[Judges]] 2)

[The narrative arc concludes in [[Judges 2:1-5]].](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=1:15:35) The [[Angel of the Lord]] ascends from Gilgal to Bochim and confronts Israel for their disobedience and idolatry. He speaks in the first person, identifying himself as the one who brought them out of [[Egypt]] and made a covenant with them. Because they have broken the covenant, he declares, "I will not drive them out before you, but they shall become thorns in your sides, and their gods shall be a snare to you." With his departure as their active commander, the period of the [[Judges]] begins, characterized by a cycle of sin, oppression, repentance, and deliverance. His departure is an act of mercy, as their continued rebellion in his direct presence would have led to their destruction.

## Conclusion

The figure of the [[Angel of the Lord]] in the Old Testament is a direct manifestation of the second person of the [[Trinity]], the Son of [[God]], before his incarnation as [[Jesus]] of Nazareth. [He is the divine warrior, the commander of God's armies, who executes justice and leads his people.](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-11-11.mp3#t=1:20:45) This is not a contradiction to the humble, suffering [[Christ]] of the Gospels. Rather, it reveals the fullness of His identity. The same one who could command legions of angels and destroy armies with a word chose to offer himself in humility for the salvation of the world. Understanding this eternal reality relativizes our day-to-day struggles and successes, pointing us toward our true identity—who we are becoming in [[Christ]] for eternity.
