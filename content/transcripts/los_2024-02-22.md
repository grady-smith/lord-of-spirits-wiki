# Core Theological Concepts

## [[Atonement]] - [[Sacrifice]].md
### The Forty Days of Lent
The Lenten period is fundamentally about repentance and preparation, which are key aspects of [[Atonement - Sacrifice]]. [[Fr. Stephen]] links the 40-day fast of Lent to several Old Testament precedents that carry a strong atoning character. The most significant is [[Moses]]' second 40-day fast on Mount Sinai, where he abstained from all food and water as an act of intercession and repentance on behalf of the people for the sin of the Golden Calf.

This idea of a preparatory period of repentance is also seen in the 40 years of wandering in the wilderness, which served as a purification for the nation of Israel before they could enter the Promised Land.
> "This introduces another element... the idea of atonement for sin. [[Atonement]] in the sense that the nation of Israel... has to be purified of certain wicked members." [Listen from 22:17](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/the_forty_days_of_lent.mp3#t=22:17)

Great Lent, therefore, is not about earning salvation but about participating in [[Christ]]'s life. It is a time for the "mortification of the flesh," which means putting to death the sinful desires rooted in our mortality and subjecting them to the [[Holy Spirit]]. This process of discipline and repentance is how Christians reaffirm their baptism, where they first died to the "old man" (Adam) and were raised to new life in [[Christ]]. The culmination of this Lenten journey is Pascha and [[Pentecost]], where the results of [[Christ]]'s ultimate sacrifice are fully realized in the life of the Church through the gift of the [[Holy Spirit]].

## The Fall of Man.md
### The Forty Days of Lent
The discussion on the origins of Lent touches upon the consequences of [[The Fall of Man]], specifically our mortality and our subsequent weakness to sinful passions. [[Fr. Stephen]] explains that after Adam's fall, humanity received "garments of skin," meaning mortal flesh. This mortality is the root of our blameless passions (like hunger, the need for sleep, and the drive to procreate), which, when uncontrolled, become sinful passions (gluttony, sloth, and lust).
> "We all are born in the likeness of Adam, of the old man. Right? We are born in his likeness. We also are mortal... and because of our mortality comes our weakness to the sinful passions... Because I am mortal, there are things that I need because if I don't get them, I will die." [Listen from 90:02](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/lordofspirits/the_forty_days_of_lent.mp3#t=90:02)

The Christian life, initiated at baptism, is about reversing this fallen state by "putting off the old man," which is Adam and his mortal, passion-driven existence. In its place, we "put on the new man," which is [[Christ]]. Great Lent is a focused season for this spiritual work. The "mortification of the flesh" practiced during Lent is not a gnostic hatred of the body, but a disciplined effort to bring the desires of our fallen, mortal nature under the control of the [[Holy Spirit]]. This process of repentance and asceticism is a reaffirmation of the baptismal rejection of the fallen Adamic life and an embrace of the renewed, resurrected life offered in [[Christ]].
