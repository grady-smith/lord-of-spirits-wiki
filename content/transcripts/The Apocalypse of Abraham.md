## The Apocalypse of [[Abraham]]: An Overview

[[Fr. Stephen]] explains that the *Apocalypse of [[Abraham]]* is a piece of [[Targums - Enochic Literature|Enochic literature]] from the late 1st century AD (c. 80 AD), likely written after the destruction of the Second [[Temple]]. Though preserved only in medieval Slavonic manuscripts by the heretical Bogomil sect, its origins are identifiably Jewish due to linguistic evidence, such as Semitic names translated directly into Slavonic, bypassing Greek. [Listen from 12:24](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-07-22.mp3#t=12:24)

The book is structured in three main parts:

1.  **[[Abraham]]'s Youth**: The text portrays [[Abraham]]'s father, [[Terah]], as a professional idol-maker. [[Abraham]] engages in a dispute with his father, arguing that the spiritual forces the idols represent are in conflict with each other and therefore cannot be ultimate. [[God]] resolves the argument by sending fire from heaven, which kills Terah and destroys his idol shop. [Listen from 36:20](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-07-22.mp3#t=36:20)

2.  **The [[Atonement]] [[Sacrifice]]**: [[Abraham]] is brought to Mount Horeb to offer a sacrifice that mirrors the ritual of the [[Atonement - Sacrifice|Day of Atonement]]. He is guided by a powerful angelic priest named [[Yehoel]], whose name means "[[Yahweh]] [[God]]" and who appears to be an instance of the "Second Power in Heaven" or the [[Angel of the Lord]]. During the ritual, the demon [[Azazel]] appears, first as an unclean raven and then in his dragon form, attempting to disrupt the sacrifice. [Listen from 48:26](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-07-22.mp3#t=48:26)

3.  **The Apocalyptic Vision**: Yehoel takes [[Abraham]] on a spiritual journey, showing him not the actual cosmos, but a divine "blueprint" or "prototype" of it. This vision reveals the spiritual reality behind historical events, including:
    *   [[The Fall of Man]], explicitly linked to the later exile of Israel.
    *   Cain being possessed by [[Azazel]] to murder Abel.
    *   A vision of "a man," a descendant of [[Abraham]], who is worshiped by some Gentiles and some of [[Abraham]]'s children, but beaten by others. This figure is described as relieving the persecution of the Jewish people and being "stood up on the last day." [[Fr. Stephen]] identifies this as a non-Christian Jewish author's interpretation of [[Jesus]] of Nazareth as the Messiah ben Joseph—a messianic figure expected to gather Gentiles to worship the [[God]] of Israel. [Listen from 01:12:07](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-07-22.mp3#t=01:12:07)

## The Expectation of a Cosmic [[Atonement]]

[[Fr. Stephen]] explains that the annual [[Day of Atonement]] ([[Yom Kippur]]) ritual described in [[Leviticus]] 16 points to its own limitations. Its yearly repetition and its place alongside other daily and weekly sin offerings indicated to Second [[Temple]] Jews that it was not a final solution to the problem of sin. The exile had not truly ended, and the people still suffered under the consequences of sin. This led to an expectation for a final, "cosmic" [[Day of Atonement]] that would purify not just the sanctuary or the camp, but the entire cosmos. [Listen from 01:24:06](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-07-22.mp3#t=01:24:06)

This cosmic event was expected to accomplish several things:
*   A final purification of the entire creation.
*   The ultimate defeat of demonic powers, particularly [[Azazel]]/the devil.
*   The definitive removal of sin as a power in the world.
*   The true freedom and restoration of humanity.

[[Fr. Stephen]] connects this expectation to the two great feasts of the Hebrew Bible:
1.  **[[Passover]] (Pascha)**: This feast is about manumission (freedom from slavery) and the judgment of the demonic "gods of [[Egypt]]."
2.  **[[Day of Atonement]] ([[Yom Kippur]])**: This is about purification and sending sin back to its source, [[Azazel]].

St. [[John]] the Forerunner's proclamation, ["Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sins of the world,"](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-07-22.mp3#t=01:49:54) masterfully combines the imagery of both feasts, presenting [[Christ]] as the one who fulfills both the [[Passover]] and the [[Day of Atonement]] on a cosmic scale.

### [[Atonement]] vs. Penal Substitution

The fathers contrast this understanding of cosmic atonement with [[Penal Substitutionary Atonement]] (PSA). [[Fr. Stephen]] explains that PSA, a core doctrine in Calvinist tradition, posits that [[God]] punished [[Christ]] on the cross for the specific sins of specific individuals (the elect). A central and necessary component of this model is that [[Christ]] had to experience **spiritual death**—separation of His human soul from [[God]].

From an Orthodox perspective, this is heretical for several reasons:
*   **Christological Heresy**: It necessitates a separation within the person of [[Christ]], either separating His humanity from His divinity (Nestorianism) or denying Him a human soul (Apollinarianism), which would mean [[Christ]] was not truly human.
*   **Trinitarian Heresy**: It implies a separation between the Father and the Son, which is impossible as they are of one essence.
*   **It's a "Smaller" [[Atonement]]**: The PSA model is focused on individual sins and individual salvation, whereas the Jewish expectation and the Orthodox understanding is of a cosmic renewal that deals with sin and death as universal powers affecting all of creation. [Listen from 01:24:49](https://media.ancientfaith.com/lordofspirits/los_2021-07-22.mp3#t=01:24:49)

The [[Orthodox Church]] teaches that [[Christ]] experienced physical death (the separation of His human soul from His human body) but never spiritual death. His divinity remained united to both His soul in [[Hades]] and His body in the tomb. The core message of Christianity is that [[God]] loves humanity and is actively working to save, heal, and restore all of creation.
