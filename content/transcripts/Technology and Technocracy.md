# Technology and Technocracy

## Introduction

[Listen from 02:24](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/los_2023_12_07.mp3#t=144)
[[Fr. Andrew]] begins the episode by introducing the topic of technology. While it has been discussed in previous episodes, this episode is dedicated to taking a "deep dive" into the subject. He humorously references several science fiction tropes related to technology, including Skynet from *Terminator*, the Borg and transporter technology from *Star Trek*, and *The Matrix*, questioning if we are living in a simulation and if our loved ones have been assimilated.

[Listen from 05:32](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/los_2023_12_07.mp3#t=332)
[[Fr. Stephen]] frames the discussion by revisiting the concept of "technē," one of the classical ways of knowing. He explains that while the modern world often draws arbitrary lines about what constitutes "technology," from an ancient perspective, any product of technē—the know-how to manipulate things to achieve a result—is technology. This includes everything from the first use of a rock as a tool to the latest smartphone.

## The Nature of Technē and Technology

[Listen from 14:13](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/los_2023_12_07.mp3#t=853)
[[Fr. Stephen]] defines technē not as knowledge *of* a thing, but as instrumental knowledge—knowing *how* something can be used or manipulated to bring about a certain result. It's about understanding the potential relationships between things to achieve a purpose.

> **[[Fr. Stephen]]:** "[Technē] is not knowledge of a thing or of the relationships between things per se... Now, technē would be knowing how to make a chair, right? But knowing how to make a chair or how to fix a chair is not the same thing as knowledge of the chair itself. [Listen from 15:51](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/los_2023_12_07.mp3#t=951)

This know-how extends beyond physical objects to include concepts, ideas, and even worldviews. [[Fr. Stephen]] points out that ideas are also instruments that people use, but they do not have agency in themselves. This is a critique of idealistic or Platonic thinking, where ideas are treated as active agents.

> **[[Fr. Stephen]]:** "When we're talking about an iPhone, an iPhone can't do things by itself. You may have realized that yours is listening to you and giving you ad recommendations based on stuff you've said around it, right? But it's still not actually the iPhone doing that. It's a piece of software installed on your phone by a human who created that piece of software who is harvesting your data. The phone itself is not the agent of this. Any more than a hammer can drive a nail by itself. Any more than a gun can shoot someone by itself. [Listen from 29:50](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/los_2023_12_07.mp3#t=1790)

## The Spiritual Origins of Technology

[Listen from 38:12](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/los_2023_12_07.mp3#t=2292)
[[Fr. Stephen]] explains that in the ancient world, there was no sharp division between the material and spiritual realms. Therefore, technē also applied to spiritual realities, leading to what could be called "spiritual technology."

*   **Magic:** This is a form of spiritual technology where a specific formula or ritual is used to compel a result, viewing spiritual forces as things to be manipulated without their own agency.
*   **Idolatry:** This involves creating an image to house or trap a spiritual being (a god) to manipulate it, though it still acknowledges the being's agency to some extent.
*   **Sacrificial Systems:** Ancient pagan sacrifices were also a form of technology aimed at influencing the gods.

[Listen from 38:25](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/los_2023_12_07.mp3#t=2305)
[[Fr. Stephen]] contrasts the Babylonian myth of the [[Apkallu]] (seven pre-flood sages who brought knowledge to humanity) with the biblical account of the origins of technology in the line of Cain found in [[Genesis#Genesis 419-24|Genesis 4]]. While the Babylonian story frames this knowledge as a positive gift justifying kingship, the biblical narrative presents it as part of a descent into sin.

*   **Jabal:** The father of those who dwell in tents and have livestock, representing the instrumentalization of animals.
*   **Jubal:** The father of all those who play the lyre and pipe, representing the creation of music for pagan rituals.
*   **Tubal-cain:** The forger of all instruments of bronze and iron, representing metallurgy primarily for making weapons.

This technology, received from a corrupt lineage, leads to violence and arrogance, as exemplified by Lamech's boastful "Song of the Sword." This narrative is further developed in extra-biblical literature like the [[Targums - Enochic Literature|Book of Enoch]] and the Book of [[Jubilees]], which explicitly state that fallen angels taught humanity forbidden knowledge, including sorcery, metallurgy for weapons, and the arts of seduction.

A parallel is drawn with the Greek myth of **Prometheus**, who steals fire from the gods to give to humanity. The "fire" in this context is not just for warmth but represents sacrificial technology—the knowledge of how to perform sacrifices, specifically how to trick the gods by offering them the undesirable parts of an animal. This act of giving forbidden, instrumental knowledge leads to Prometheus's punishment.

## Technology, Community, and Fragmentation

[Listen from 1:04:11](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/los_2023_12_07.mp3#t=3851)
[[Fr. Stephen]] and [[Fr. Andrew]] discuss the effects of technology on human community. They observe a significant difference between the technological boom of the early 20th century and the one of the late 20th and early 21st centuries.

> **[[Fr. Stephen]]:** "The technological explosion at the beginning of the 20th century primarily connected people... The more recent technological explosion at the end of the 20th and beginning of the 21st century has done the exact opposite. It is isolating, it is alienating to people. This is separating us." [Listen from 1:10:24](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/los_2023_12_07.mp3#t=4224)

Early 20th-century technologies like the radio, telephone, and automobile helped create a shared culture and connected people over long distances. In contrast, modern internet and social media technologies tend to fragment society, allowing individuals to retreat into personalized echo chambers where they are only exposed to ideas they already agree with. This fractures the possibility of a common basis for conversation and community.

## Final Thoughts: Freedom in Community

[Listen from 1:15:35](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/los_2023_12_07.mp3#t=4535)
[[Fr. Andrew]] emphasizes that human beings are "permeable." When we isolate ourselves from fellow humans and our parish community, we do not become truly independent; instead, we become permeable to other, often demonic, spiritual influences. The only way to combat this is through an "asceticism of attention" and a dedicated commitment to real, "3D parish life." Internet ministries like Lord of Spirits should point people toward this reality, not become a substitute for it.

[Listen from 1:16:51](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/los_2023_12_07.mp3#t=4611)
[[Fr. Stephen]] concludes by defining true freedom not as radical individualism but as the freedom to flourish as a human being, which can only be achieved within a community. He argues that modern ideologies and technologies often enslave people by pitting them against each other and isolating them.

> **[[Fr. Stephen]]:** "Israeli people and Palestinian people will become free when they become free together, by throwing off their masters who have pitted them against each other to their own destruction. We could say the same thing about Ukrainian people and Russian people. But you could say the same thing about Norwegian people, about American people, about Australian people... We have been tricked into doing [things that make our lives worse] by a prolonged indoctrination in certain ideas, lying to us about where our happiness lies, lying to us about what we need to be afraid of, lying to us about other people to keep us separated from them." [Listen from 1:17:39](https://media.ancientfaith.com/audio/podcasts/los_2023_12_07.mp3#t=4659)

The path to freedom is to collectively reject these enslaving ideologies. The primary place where this work can begin is within the parish community, where people can come together to find true humanity and be set free from sin to serve the living [[God]].
