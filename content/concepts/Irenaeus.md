---
title: "Irenaeus"
tags: [concept]
---
St. Irenaeus of Lyons (c. 130–202 AD) was a deeply influential early Church Father and bishop in Gaul (modern-day France). As a disciple of Polycarp (who himself was a disciple of the Apostle John), Irenaeus represents a direct, unbroken link to apostolic teaching. He is best known for his monumental work, *Against Heresies*, which systematically dismantled the complex mythological systems of early Gnosticism.

In Orthodox theology, Irenaeus is foundational for his articulation of "recapitulation" (anakephalaiosis). Drawing from the Apostle Paul, Irenaeus taught that Jesus Christ, as the Second Adam, retraced the steps of human history. Where the first Adam failed through disobedience, bringing death, Christ succeeded through perfect obedience, bringing life. Christ sanctified every stage of human life—infancy, youth, and adulthood—by living it perfectly and ultimately defeating death through the cross.

The *Lord of Spirits* often highlights Irenaeus's profound understanding of creation and materiality. Against the Gnostics who viewed the physical world as evil and the creation of a lesser deity, Irenaeus fiercely defended the goodness of the material cosmos created by the one true God. He affirmed that salvation is not an escape from the physical body, but the resurrection and transfiguration of the entire created order.
