The First Epistle of Peter is a profound theological text that heavily relies on Second Temple Jewish traditions, particularly those found in 1 Enoch. In the *Lord of Spirits* podcast, 1 Peter is famous for its enigmatic passage regarding Christ's descent to the underworld to "preach to the spirits in prison" who were disobedient in the days of Noah. This is understood as the Harrowing of Hell, but specifically as Christ declaring His absolute victory and doom over the rebellious Watchers who had orchestrated the corruption of humanity before the Flood.

St. Peter explicitly connects this cosmic victory to the sacrament of Baptism. He uses the Flood of Noah as a type or precursor to Baptism; just as the waters of the Flood destroyed the wicked world and saved Noah's family, the waters of Baptism represent a death to the old world and a deliverance from the demonic powers that hold humanity captive. Baptism is described as an "appeal to God for a good conscience," operating as a pledge of loyalty to Christ the King.

Furthermore, 1 Peter emphasizes the identity of the Church as a holy priesthood and a chosen nation. In the ancient world, priests maintained the sanctity of the temple and offered sacrifices. Peter teaches that all Christians are called to live holy lives, serving as priests in the world by offering spiritual sacrifices, resisting the devil, and partaking in the suffering of Christ in order to share in His ultimate glory.
