---
title: "Holy of Holies"
tags: [concept]
---
The Holy of Holies (or the Most Holy Place) was the innermost sanctuary of the ancient Israelite Tabernacle and the Jerusalem Temple. Separated from the rest of the sanctuary by a thick veil, it was the focal point of God's manifest presence on earth. It contained the Ark of the Covenant, representing God's throne, where the Cherubim overshadowed the Mercy Seat.

In ancient Near Eastern cosmology, the Holy of Holies was understood as the intersection between heaven and earth, a localized Eden where the divine realm overlapped with the material world. It was a place of extreme holiness and danger; only the High Priest could enter, and only once a year on the Day of Atonement (Yom Kippur), bringing sacrificial blood to cleanse the cosmos of sin and impurity.

In Orthodox theology and the New Testament, the veil of the Holy of Holies was torn at the crucifixion of Christ, signifying that access to the direct presence of God is now open to all humanity through Jesus. Furthermore, Christ’s ascension is understood as His entry into the true, heavenly Holy of Holies. Today, the altar in an Orthodox Church conceptually mirrors the Holy of Holies, serving as the place where heaven meets earth during the Eucharist.
