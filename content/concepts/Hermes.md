title: "Hermes"
tags: [concept]
---
In the Greco-Roman pantheon, Hermes (known as Mercury to the Romans) was the herald and messenger of the gods, the guide of the dead to the underworld (psychopomp), and the patron of boundaries, travelers, merchants, and thieves. He was closely associated with language, writing, and esoteric knowledge, often syncretized in late antiquity with the Egyptian god Thoth to form the figure of Hermes Trismegistus.

Within the ancient Near Eastern and Orthodox framework discussed on the *Lord of Spirits*, the pagan gods are understood not as myths, but as actual fallen spiritual beings—rebellious members of the Divine Council who accepted the worship of humanity. Hermes represents a specific demonic principality whose "domain" involves the manipulation of communication, the mediation of forbidden, occult knowledge, and the facilitation of magic and trickery.

The Apostolic encounter with the cult of Hermes is notably recorded in Acts 14, where the people of Lystra mistake Paul for Hermes because he was the chief speaker. Early Christianity directly opposed the powers represented by Hermes by proclaiming Jesus Christ as the true Logos (Word) and the sole mediator between God and humanity, stripping these rebellious entities of their perceived authority and reconciling all true knowledge and communication back to the Creator.
