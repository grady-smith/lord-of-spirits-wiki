---
title: "Baptism"
tags: [concept]
---

In Orthodox theology, Baptism is far more than a mere symbol of personal faith; it is a profound cosmological event and a sacrament of initiation into the life of Christ. Rooted in the Ancient Near Eastern understanding of water as representing chaos, death, and the abyss (the Leviathan-inhabited deep), descending into the baptismal waters is a literal participation in the death and descent of Christ into Hades. The baptized person is drowning the old, fallen nature in the chaotic waters that have been conquered and sanctified by Christ's own immersion.

Rising from the water is a participation in Christ's resurrection, marking a transfer of allegiance from the fallen principalities and powers to the Kingdom of God. In the context of the *Lord of Spirits*, Baptism is understood as an act of spiritual warfare and cosmic relocation. Before the water ritual, the catechumen formally renounces Satan and all his angels—an explicit rejection of the fallen divine council—and is united to Christ. The newly baptized is thus grafted into the faithful Divine Council, given the Holy Spirit, and equipped to participate in the ongoing healing of creation.
