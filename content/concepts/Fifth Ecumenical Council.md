---
title: "Fifth Ecumenical Council"
tags: [concept]
---

The Fifth Ecumenical Council, also known as the Second Council of Constantinople, was held in 553 AD under the Emperor Justinian I. Its primary purpose was to resolve the "Three Chapters" controversy, aiming to reconcile the non-Chalcedonian (Miaphysite) Christians by condemning certain writings of Theodore of Mopsuestia, Theodoret of Cyrus, and Ibas of Edessa, which were seen as tainted by Nestorianism.

In Orthodox theology, this council is crucial for solidifying the Christological synthesis of the early Church. It reaffirmed the teachings of the previous councils, emphasizing that the one person (hypostasis) of Jesus Christ is the divine Logos who suffered in the flesh. The council's defense of this precise understanding safeguards the reality of the Incarnation, ensuring that it was truly God who entered into human history, suffered, and defeated death, a concept central to the ancient Christian understanding of salvation.
