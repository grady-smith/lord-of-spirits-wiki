---
title: "Sin"
tags: [concept]
---

In the biblical and ancient Near Eastern worldview, "sin" (Hebrew: *chata*, Greek: *hamartia*) is fundamentally understood as "missing the mark" or failing to achieve the purpose for which humanity was created: communion with God. However, sin is not merely a legal infraction or the breaking of a rule. In the context of the *Lord of Spirits* podcast, sin is often described as a parasite, a disease, or a destructive power that corrupts human nature and the cosmos. It is a state of alienation from the source of life, inevitably leading to death.

Orthodox theology strongly contrasts with Western models that view sin primarily as an inherited guilt (original sin) that demands legal punishment. Instead, Orthodoxy emphasizes "ancestral sin"—humanity inherits the *consequences* of Adam and Eve's rebellion, primarily mortality and a corrupted, passion-prone nature, but not their personal guilt. Because human beings are born into a fallen world governed by death and spiritual powers of darkness, they are inevitably drawn into personal acts of sin, further fracturing their relationship with God and each other.

Salvation, therefore, is not primarily a legal acquittal from the guilt of sin, but an ontological healing from the *disease* of sin and deliverance from the *power* of death. Christ's incarnation, crucifixion, and resurrection are the means by which He enters into the depths of human corruption to destroy sin and death from the inside out. Through the sacramental life of the Church and ascetic struggle, believers are gradually healed from the passions, participating in Christ's victory over sin and moving toward *theosis*.
