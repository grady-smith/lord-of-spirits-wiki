---
title: "James"
tags: [concept]
---
In the New Testament and the early Church, "James" primarily refers to two distinct, highly significant figures: James, the son of Zebedee (one of the original Twelve Apostles), and James, the "Brother of the Lord" (also known as James the Just), who became the first bishop of Jerusalem. 

James the son of Zebedee, along with his brother John and Peter, was part of Christ's inner circle. He was uniquely privileged to witness the Transfiguration on Mount Tabor and the agony in Gethsemane, intimately experiencing the uncreated light of Christ's divinity and the depths of His human suffering. He was the first Apostle to be martyred, executed by Herod Agrippa, signifying the violent clash between the Kingdom of God and worldly powers.

James the Just, the brother (or stepbrother/cousin) of Jesus, played a monumental role in the early Church. As the head of the Jerusalem council (Acts 15), he authored the authoritative decree regarding Gentile converts. His New Testament Epistle is a profound piece of wisdom literature, emphasizing that true faith must be manifested in tangible works of love and justice. His martyrdom in Jerusalem marked a definitive break between the early Christian community and the established Second Temple Jewish leadership.
