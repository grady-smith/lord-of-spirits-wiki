---
title: "Covenant"
tags: [concept]
---
In the ancient Near Eastern context, a covenant (*berith* in Hebrew) was a binding, solemn treaty or pact between two parties, often modeled after suzerain-vassal treaties where a great king promises protection and land to a lesser king in exchange for absolute loyalty and tribute. In the biblical narrative, Yahweh establishes covenants with humanity (e.g., through Noah, Abraham, Moses, and David) to mediate His presence, restore the cosmos, and establish the parameters of relationship between God and His people. 

Within Orthodox theology, covenants are profoundly relational, sacramental, and liturgical. They are not merely legal contracts, but frameworks of kinship, often ratified by blood sacrifice and shared meals, which bond God and humanity together. The "New Covenant" established by Christ is understood as the ultimate fulfillment of these ancient patterns. Through the Incarnation, crucifixion, and the Eucharist, Christ assumes the role of both the faithful Suzerain and the obedient Vassal, uniting human and divine natures and inviting believers to participate eternally in the Divine Life.
