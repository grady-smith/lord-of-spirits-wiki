---
title: "St. Athanasius"
tags: [concept]
---

Saint Athanasius of Alexandria (c. 296–373) was a towering Church Father, a patriarch of Alexandria, and the chief defender of Trinitarian orthodoxy against Arianism. He famously attended the First Council of Nicaea in 325 as a deacon, where he rigorously argued for the doctrine of *homoousios*—that the Son is "of one essence" with the Father. Because of his unwavering stance against the Arians, who had significant political backing, Athanasius was exiled from his see five times during his life, leading to the phrase *Athanasius contra mundum* ("Athanasius against the world").

In the context of the *Lord of Spirits* and Orthodox theology, Athanasius is essential for his articulation of the Incarnation and salvation. In his foundational work, *On the Incarnation*, he argues that the Word of God had to become flesh to solve the double problem of human corruption and death caused by the Fall. Because humans are made in the image of the Logos, only the Logos could recreate and restore them. Athanasius famously summarized the core of Orthodox soteriology (theosis) with the phrase: "God became man so that man might become god."

Athanasius also played a crucial role in shaping Christian asceticism and angelology through his *Life of Antony*, a biography of St. Antony the Great. This highly influential text detailed the reality of demonic warfare in the desert and the power of Christ, through the humble ascetic, to defeat the spiritual forces of darkness. It profoundly shaped the trajectory of monasticism throughout the Christian world.
