---
title: "St. John Chrysostom"
tags: [concept]
---

Saint John Chrysostom (c. 347–407) was the Archbishop of Constantinople and is considered one of the greatest preachers in Christian history, earning him the epithet "Chrysostom," which means "Golden-Mouthed." He is one of the Three Holy Hierarchs in the Orthodox Church, alongside St. Basil the Great and St. Gregory the Theologian. His extensive homilies and biblical commentaries profoundly shaped the exegetical tradition of the Eastern Church.

In the context of the *Lord of Spirits* and Orthodox praxis, Chrysostom is perhaps most immediately recognized for the *Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom*, the primary eucharistic service celebrated in the Orthodox Church throughout the year. His theology was deeply pastoral and incarnational, constantly bridging high theological concepts with everyday Christian living. He was a fierce advocate for social justice, frequently preaching against wealth inequality, greed, and the abuse of power, insisting that the true altar of Christ is the poor.

Chrysostom's eschatology and soteriology are famously encapsulated in his Paschal Homily, read in Orthodox churches every Easter. The homily dramatically declares the complete overthrow of Death and Hades by Christ's resurrection. He vividly describes Hades being "embittered" and mocked, having anticipated a mere mortal body but instead encountering the divine life of God. This reflects the classic Orthodox understanding of salvation as the victorious defeat of the cosmic powers that held humanity captive.
