---
title: "Catholicism"
tags: [concept]
---

Catholicism (specifically Roman Catholicism) is the largest branch of Christianity, centered on the authority of the Pope (the Bishop of Rome) and characterized by its distinct theological, liturgical, and legal traditions developed over centuries in the West. Following the Great Schism (traditionally dated to 1054 AD), Catholicism diverged from Eastern Orthodoxy over issues such as universal papal jurisdiction, the insertion of the *Filioque* into the Nicene Creed, and later dogmas like purgatory, the immaculate conception, and papal infallibility.

In the discussions on the *Lord of Spirits*, Catholicism is often analyzed in terms of its historical trajectory away from the shared, ancient worldview of the first millennium Church. Orthodox critiques frequently highlight how Catholicism, influenced by Western philosophical movements like scholasticism (e.g., Thomas Aquinas), began to systematize theology into rigid, rational categories. This shift often led to a more juridical and forensic understanding of salvation, sin, and the atonement, moving away from the older, more mystical and participatory framework of cosmic healing and *Christus Victor* preserved in the East.
