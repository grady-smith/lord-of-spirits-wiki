The Epistle to the Colossians is a New Testament letter written by the Apostle Paul to the church in Colossae, a city in Asia Minor. The letter was written to combat a local heresy (often described as the "Colossian heresy") that blended Jewish legalism, early Gnostic elements, and the veneration of angels. The false teachers in Colossae appeared to argue that believers needed to appease intermediate spiritual beings or follow strict ascetic and dietary codes to achieve spiritual fullness.

In the *Lord of Spirits* framework, Colossians contains some of the most concentrated and explicit "divine council" and spiritual warfare theology in the New Testament. In chapter 2, Paul famously declares that Christ has "disarmed the principalities and powers, making a public spectacle of them, triumphing over them by the cross." Paul uses this letter to assert the absolute supremacy of Christ over all creation, visible and invisible. He assures the Colossians that because Christ is the head of all principality and power, they do not need to worship or fear the fallen angels governing the nations; they are already complete in Him.
