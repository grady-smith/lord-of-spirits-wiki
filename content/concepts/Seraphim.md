---
title: "Seraphim"
tags: [concept]
---

In biblical angelology and Orthodox theology, the Seraphim are a high-ranking choir of angelic beings, most famously depicted in the prophet Isaiah's vision of the divine throne room (Isaiah 6). The word "seraphim" is derived from the Hebrew root *saraf*, which means "to burn." They are often described as "fiery, flying serpents" or burning ones, indicating their proximity to the uncreated, consuming fire of God's holy presence. In Isaiah's vision, they have six wings: two to cover their faces, two to cover their feet, and two to fly, while they perpetually cry out, "Holy, holy, holy is the Lord of hosts."

The *Lord of Spirits* podcast frequently explores the ancient Near Eastern context of these beings. In Egyptian and Canaanite iconography, throne guardians were often depicted as winged, fiery serpents (uraeus). The biblical depiction appropriates this imagery, demonstrating that Yahweh's throne is guarded by the most terrifying and majestic cosmic beings. Their burning nature is not destructive, but a reflection of their intense, unceasing participation in divine love and holiness.

In the liturgical life of the Orthodox Church, the Seraphim are constantly invoked. During the Divine Liturgy, the congregation joins mystically with the Seraphim in singing the "Thrice-Holy" hymn (Sanctus). Furthermore, when the priest or deacon censes the altar, it recalls the action of the Seraph who took a burning coal from the heavenly altar to purify Isaiah's lips—a direct typological prefiguration of the Eucharist, which burns away sin and purifies the communicant.
