---
title: "Atonement"
tags: [concept]
---

In Orthodox theology, Atonement (often understood as "at-one-ment") is the process by which humanity and the cosmos are reconciled to God, primarily through the incarnation, life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Unlike later Western penal substitutionary models that focus primarily on the legal satisfaction of God's wrath, the ancient Orthodox and biblical understanding emphasizes "Christus Victor"—Christ's triumph over sin, death, and the fallen spiritual powers. Atonement involves the purification and cleansing of creation from the defilement of sin, as well as the ontological healing of human nature.

In the Ancient Near East and Israelite religion, atonement was deeply connected to ritual purity and the Temple system. The Day of Atonement (Yom Kippur) involved the cleansing of sacred space so that God could continue to dwell among His people. The blood of the sacrifice was not seen as a payment to God, but as a ritual detergent used to wipe away the taint of sin. Christ is the ultimate fulfillment of this reality; His blood cleanses the entire cosmos, defeating the demonic powers that claimed ownership over humanity through death, and restoring the union between heaven and earth.
