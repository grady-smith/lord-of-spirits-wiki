---
title: "Dragons"
tags: [concept]
---
In ancient Near Eastern mythology and biblical literature, dragons (or sea monsters, such as Leviathan or Tanninim) are not merely mythological beasts, but symbols of primeval chaos, destruction, and spiritual rebellion against the divine order. In cultures across the ancient world, the supreme deity was often depicted engaging in *Chaoskampf*—a cosmic battle against a multi-headed dragon representing the untamed, destructive forces of the sea and the abyss.

In Orthodox theology and *The Lord of Spirits*, this imagery is deeply theological. The dragon is frequently associated with the Devil, the ultimate source of spiritual chaos and death. When Christ walks on water, commands the sea to be still, or when He is depicted in iconography trampling the gates of Hades, He is executing the ultimate *Chaoskampf*, subduing the chaotic waters and defeating the dragon. The Book of Revelation explicitly identifies Satan as the "great red dragon," whose final defeat inaugurates the new creation where "the sea was no more" (Rev 21:1), symbolizing the total eradication of chaos and evil.
