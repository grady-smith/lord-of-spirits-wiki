The Book of Tobit is a deuterocanonical text (part of the Apocrypha in Protestant terms) that forms an integral part of the Orthodox Old Testament. Set during the Assyrian exile, it tells the story of a righteous Israelite, Tobit, and his son Tobias, whose faithful adherence to the law and almsgiving invoke divine assistance. The narrative heavily features the Archangel Raphael, who assumes human form to guide Tobias, heal Tobit's blindness, and deliver Sarah from the demon Asmodeus.

The *Lord of Spirits* podcast frequently relies on Tobit to illustrate the Second Temple Jewish understanding of angelology and demonology. The text portrays the unseen realm as intensely active in everyday human life; demons like Asmodeus cause physical harm and death, while angels like Raphael act as tangible protectors, healers, and intercessors. Tobit firmly establishes that the spiritual warfare of the ancient world was not merely an abstract concept but involved real, personal entities whose actions intersected with the physical world, requiring faith, prayer, and ritual purity to combat.
