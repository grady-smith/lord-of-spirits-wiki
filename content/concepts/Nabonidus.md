---
title: "Nabonidus"
tags: [concept]
---

Nabonidus was the last king of the Neo-Babylonian Empire before it fell to Cyrus the Great of Persia. Historically, he is known for elevating the moon god Sîn over the traditional Babylonian supreme deity Marduk, which alienated the powerful priesthood of Babylon, and for spending a significant portion of his reign in self-imposed exile in the Arabian oasis of Tayma.

In biblical studies and the context of the *Lord of Spirits* podcast, Nabonidus is an important figure for understanding the background of the Book of Daniel. Fragments found among the Dead Sea Scrolls, such as the "Prayer of Nabonidus," recount a story of a Babylonian king who was afflicted with a terrible disease for seven years until an exiled Jewish diviner intervened, heavily paralleling the biblical narrative of Nebuchadnezzar's madness in Daniel 4.

The figure of Nabonidus, much like Nebuchadnezzar, fits into the broader biblical motif of the arrogant pagan tyrant who is humbled by the true God. This theme underscores the reality that earthly empires, no matter how powerful, are temporary and ultimately subject to the judgment of Yahweh, who brings down the proud kings and their demonic patrons.
