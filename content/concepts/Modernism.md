---
title: "Modernism"
tags: [concept]
---

Modernism, in the theological and philosophical context, refers to the worldview that emerged following the Enlightenment, heavily characterized by rationalism, materialism, and a strict division between the physical and spiritual realms. It operates on a mechanistic view of the universe where God, if He exists at all, is a distant clockmaker who set the world in motion but does not actively participate in its day-to-day operations.

The *Lord of Spirits* podcast frequently critiques Modernism as a fundamental distortion of the biblical and ancient Christian worldview. Modernism "disenchants" the world, stripping away the reality of spiritual beings, the Divine Council, and demonic powers, reducing angels and demons to mere psychological metaphors or pre-scientific superstitions. This framework makes ancient texts nearly unintelligible to modern readers, as it imposes a materialistic lens onto a profoundly spiritual ancient world.

In contrast to Modernism, Orthodox theology maintains an "enchanted" or sacramental worldview. Matter and spirit are deeply intertwined; physical objects (like water, oil, and bread) can convey divine grace, and spiritual entities are actively involved in the governance of the cosmos. Overcoming the Modernist bias is considered essential for properly understanding the scriptures and the lived reality of the Orthodox tradition.
