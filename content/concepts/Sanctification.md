---
title: "Sanctification"
tags: [concept]
---

Sanctification, in the biblical and Orthodox Christian understanding, is the process of being made holy, set apart for God, and transformed into the likeness of Christ. In the ancient Near Eastern context, to be "sanctified" or "holy" (from the Hebrew root *qdsh*) meant to be withdrawn from ordinary, profane use and dedicated entirely to the divine realm. Objects, spaces, and people could be sanctified through specific rituals and sacrifices, allowing them to safely host or interact with the presence of God.

The *Lord of Spirits* podcast often stresses that sanctification is not a one-time event but a continuous, lifelong process of *theosis* (deification). It is the realization of salvation in the life of the believer. While justification and sanctification are often separated in Western theology, Orthodoxy views them as inseparable aspects of the same reality: union with God. Through ascetic struggle, prayer, repentance, and participation in the sacraments, the Holy Spirit purifies the heart and mind, burning away the passions and the lingering effects of sin.

Sanctification is essentially the restoration of the image and likeness of God in humanity, which was marred by the Fall. As a person is sanctified, they increasingly participate in the uncreated energies of God, becoming a conduit of grace to the surrounding world. This process culminates in the age to come, but it begins deeply in the present life, transforming the individual into a living temple of the Holy Spirit.
