title: "St. Augustine"
tags: [concept]
---

Saint Augustine of Hippo (354–430) was a profoundly influential bishop and theologian in Roman Africa. Following a dramatic conversion from Manichaeism and Neoplatonism, detailed in his *Confessions*, he became a prolific writer defending the Church against various heresies, including Donatism and Pelagianism. His writings—such as *The City of God* and *On the Trinity*—are foundational to Western Christian theology, profoundly shaping the intellectual traditions of both Catholicism and Protestantism.

In the *Lord of Spirits* podcast and modern Orthodox discourse, St. Augustine is often viewed with nuance. While recognized as a saint and a brilliant thinker, Orthodox theology critiques several of his theological developments that diverged from the consensus of the Eastern Fathers. Most notably, his specific articulation of "original sin"—which posited that humanity inherits the actual guilt of Adam's sin, often tied to biological transmission through concupiscence—is generally rejected by the East in favor of the concept of "ancestral sin" (inheriting the consequences, particularly mortality, but not the guilt).

Furthermore, Augustine's later writings heavily emphasized absolute predestination and irresistible grace, frameworks that would later be expanded upon by figures like John Calvin. The Orthodox tradition, rooted in the earlier patristic consensus, maintains a stronger emphasis on human free will (synergy) cooperating with divine grace in the process of salvation. Despite these differences, Augustine remains a venerated saint in the Orthodox Church, recognized for his deep piety, his defense of the Church, and his profound psychological insights into the human condition.
