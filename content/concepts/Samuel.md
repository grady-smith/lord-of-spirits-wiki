---
title: "Samuel"
tags: [concept]
---

Samuel is a pivotal figure in the Hebrew Bible, serving as the last of the biblical Judges and the first of the major prophets. He stands at the crucial transition period between the tribal confederacy of Israel and the establishment of the Israelite monarchy. Dedicated to God by his mother Hannah and raised in the sanctuary at Shiloh, Samuel functioned as a priest, prophet, and military leader, uniting the tribes and calling them to repentance from idolatry.

In the context of the ancient Near East, Samuel's role was highly significant. He was a "seer" and an intermediary who communicated the divine will to the people. His resistance to the people's demand for a king—seeing it as a rejection of Yahweh's direct kingship—highlights the tension between Israel's calling to be a distinct, holy nation and their desire to assimilate into the political structures of surrounding nations. When instructed by God, Samuel anointed both Saul and, subsequently, David, thereby establishing the Davidic dynasty.

From an Orthodox perspective, Samuel is venerated as a great prophet who prefigures Christ in his multifaceted roles. His miraculous birth points to the birth of John the Baptist and Christ Himself. Samuel's life of dedicated service, his uncompromising stance against corruption in the priesthood (the sons of Eli), and his role in inaugurating the kingdom of David make him a central figure in the unfolding history of salvation and the preparation for the ultimate King, Jesus Christ.
