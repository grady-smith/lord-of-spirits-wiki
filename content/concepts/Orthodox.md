---
title: "Orthodox"
tags: [concept]
---

The word "Orthodox" comes from the Greek words *orthos* (meaning "right," "true," or "straight") and *doxa* (meaning "glory" or "worship/belief"). Thus, it translates to "right belief" or, more accurately in the liturgical context, "right worship." It describes the theology and practice that aligns truly with the revelation of God in Jesus Christ.

In the *Lord of Spirits* framework, being Orthodox is not merely adhering to a checklist of doctrinal statements; it is participating correctly in the life of the cosmos. Right worship is how humanity properly interfaces with the Divine Council and the Creator. Because humans become like what they worship, Orthodox worship transforms the believer into the likeness of Christ, while heterodox or pagan worship subjects the human person to the degrading influence of fallen spiritual entities.

The term historically gained prominence during the early Church councils as a way to distinguish the true, apostolic faith from emerging heresies (like Arianism or Gnosticism) that distorted the nature of the Trinity or the Incarnation. To be Orthodox is to maintain the unbroken continuity of faith, practice, and spiritual warfare inherited from the Apostles.
