---
title: "FALL"
tags: [concept]
---

The Fall refers to the narrative in Genesis 3 where Adam and Eve disobey God by eating from the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil, resulting in their expulsion from the Garden of Eden. In Orthodox theology, the Fall is understood less as the transmission of inherited "original guilt" and more as an infection of mortality and a distortion of humanity's vocation. By succumbing to the temptation of the serpent—a rebellious spiritual being—humanity subjected itself to the power of death and the dominion of hostile spiritual forces.

In the *Lord of Spirits* context, the Fall of humanity is just one of several cosmic rebellions (alongside the fall of the Watchers and the events at the Tower of Babel) that fractured the relationship between God, humanity, and the spiritual realm. The Fall represents a failure of Adam and Eve to fulfill their priestly role as mediators between heaven and earth. Consequently, the entirety of the biblical narrative is the story of God working to reverse the effects of the Fall, culminating in the incarnate Christ, the New Adam, who defeats death and restores humanity to its rightful place in the divine council.
