The Second Epistle of Peter is a profound New Testament text that issues severe warnings against false teachers and heavily incorporates the apocalyptic worldview of Second Temple Judaism. In the *Lord of Spirits* podcast, 2 Peter is frequently referenced for its explicit mention of "Tartarus" (translated as "hell" in many English Bibles). Peter states that God "did not spare angels when they sinned, but cast them into hell [Tartarus] and committed them to chains of gloomy darkness."

This passage is a direct reference to the Enochic narrative of the Watchers—the rebellious angels of Genesis 6 who descended to Mount Hermon and corrupted humanity. By using the Greek term Tartarus, the deepest abyss of the underworld associated with the imprisonment of the Titans in Greek mythology, Peter translates the ancient Hebrew cosmology into a Hellenistic context. This underscores the reality of the cosmic war and the ultimate judgment that awaits the rebellious divine beings.

Furthermore, 2 Peter anticipates the Day of the Lord, describing a cosmic dissolution by fire where the "elements" (stoicheia, often understood as the spiritual powers ruling the cosmos) will be melted. This is not merely an annihilation of the physical universe, but a final purification of creation, making way for "new heavens and a new earth in which righteousness dwells," free from the corrupting influence of the demonic powers.
