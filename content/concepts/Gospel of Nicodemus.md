---
title: "Gospel of Nicodemus"
tags: [concept]
---

The Gospel of Nicodemus, also known as the Acts of Pilate, is an apocryphal Christian text that likely originated in the 4th century, though it claims to be derived from official Roman records kept by Pontius Pilate. The text is divided into two main sections: the first recounts the trial, crucifixion, and resurrection of Jesus, expanding on the canonical accounts; the second, and more famous, section is the *Descensus Christi ad Inferos*, a dramatic narrative of Christ's descent into Hades.

In the context of Orthodox theology and the *Lord of Spirits*, the Gospel of Nicodemus is significant not as canonical scripture, but as a vivid illustration of the early Church's profound theological understanding of the Harrowing of Hell. It poetically describes Christ breaking the gates of brass, binding Satan, and liberating the righteous dead—including Adam and Eve and the patriarchs. This text deeply influenced the iconography and liturgical hymnography of Holy Saturday and Pascha (Easter) in the Orthodox tradition.
