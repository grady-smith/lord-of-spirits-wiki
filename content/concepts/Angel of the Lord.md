---
title: "Angel of the Lord"
tags: [concept]
---
In the Hebrew Bible, the "Angel of the Lord" (Malakh Yahweh) is a unique and prominent figure who plays a central role in the interactions between God and humanity. The *Lord of Spirits* podcast heavily emphasizes that this figure is not a created angel, but a Christophany—a pre-incarnate appearance of the Logos, the Second Person of the Trinity. He acts as the chief messenger and commander of Yahweh’s Divine Council.

The text frequently blurs the line between Yahweh and the Angel of the Lord. For example, when appearing to Moses in the burning bush, the figure is first identified as the Angel of the Lord, but then speaks as God Himself. The Angel carries the "Name" of Yahweh, which in ancient Near Eastern thought means carrying the very presence, essence, and authority of the deity. He accepts worship, forgives sins, and executes divine judgment—actions reserved exclusively for God.

Early Christians and Church Fathers universally understood these appearances as the Son of God interacting with the Patriarchs and Prophets before His incarnation. Recognizing the Angel of the Lord as Christ provides crucial continuity between the Old and New Testaments, demonstrating that the God who leads Israel out of Egypt and fights their cosmic battles is the same Jesus Christ who defeats death on the cross.
