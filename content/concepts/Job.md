---
title: "Job"
tags: [concept]
---
The Book of Job is a profound piece of ancient wisdom literature that grapples with the mystery of innocent suffering, divine justice, and the limitations of human comprehension. Job is depicted as a righteous man from the land of Uz who undergoes catastrophic loss—his wealth, children, and health—due to a cosmic wager in the heavenly courts.

A crucial element of Job, heavily discussed in the *Lord of Spirits*, is its opening scene depicting the Divine Council. The "sons of God" present themselves before Yahweh, and among them is "the satan" (the adversary or accuser). In this early context, "satan" is functioning as a title or role within the divine bureaucracy—a prosecuting attorney figure whose job is to test the loyalties of humans, reflecting the complex hierarchy of the unseen realm before the final rebellion of the devil.

In Orthodox theology, Job is read as a profound prefiguration of Jesus Christ. Job's suffering was not a punishment for his sins (as his friends falsely argued), but a cosmic ordeal. Like Christ, Job is the righteous sufferer who is stripped, humiliated, and brought to the dust of death, only to be ultimately vindicated and restored by God. God's response to Job out of the whirlwind does not answer the "why" of suffering but reveals the overwhelming majesty of the Creator who rules the chaotic forces (Leviathan and Behemoth) of the universe.
