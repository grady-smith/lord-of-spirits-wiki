---
title: "Vulgate"
tags: [concept]
---

The Vulgate is the late 4th-century Latin translation of the Bible, primarily undertaken by St. Jerome. Commissioned by Pope Damasus I, it became the definitive and officially promulgated Latin version of the Bible in the Roman Catholic Church. Jerome heavily favored translating the Old Testament directly from the Hebrew Masoretic texts (the *Hebraica veritas*) rather than relying primarily on the Greek Septuagint, which had been the standard for the early Church.

The *Lord of Spirits* frequently contrasts the Vulgate and its reliance on the later Hebrew text with the Eastern Orthodox reliance on the Greek Septuagint. The hosts point out that the Septuagint often preserves older textual traditions—many of which explicitly support the Christian understanding of the Divine Council and Christology (such as the rendering of Deuteronomy 32:8)—which were sometimes obscured or altered in the later Rabbinic Hebrew texts that Jerome used. Consequently, the Vulgate represents a slight shift in Western theology away from the Second Temple apocalyptic framework maintained in the Greek East.
