---
title: "Chaoskampf"
tags: [concept]
---

*Chaoskampf* (German for "struggle against chaos") is a prominent mythological motif in the religions of the Ancient Near East, describing the battle between a supreme deity (usually a storm god) and a monstrous entity representing primordial chaos, often symbolized by the sea or a sea dragon. Classic examples include the Babylonian Marduk defeating Tiamat, and the Canaanite Baal defeating Yamm or Lotan. The victory of the storm god results in the establishment of cosmic order, the creation of the world, and the building of the god's temple.

The *Lord of Spirits* podcast heavily emphasizes how the biblical writers consciously appropriated the *Chaoskampf* motif to subvert pagan mythologies and declare the supremacy of Yahweh. In texts like Psalm 74 and Job, God is described as crushing the heads of Leviathan or Rahab. However, unlike pagan myths where the god often struggles or requires weapons forged by others, Yahweh’s victory over chaos is absolute and effortless. Christ's walking on water, His calming of the storm, and His descent into Hades are the ultimate fulfillments of the *Chaoskampf*, demonstrating His sovereignty over the chaotic abyss and His role as the true creator and orderer of the cosmos.
