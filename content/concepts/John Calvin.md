---
title: "John Calvin"
tags: [concept]
---
John Calvin (1509–1564) was a French theologian, pastor, and major reformer during the Protestant Reformation, whose systematic theology, notably articulated in the *Institutes of the Christian Religion*, fundamentally shaped Reformed theology (Calvinism). He emphasized the absolute sovereignty of God, the total depravity of humanity, and the doctrine of predestination.

From an Orthodox theological perspective, particularly as analyzed in discussions like the *Lord of Spirits*, Calvin's framework represents a significant departure from the consensus of the early Church Fathers. Calvin's view of absolute, deterministic predestination—where God unconditionally ordains some to salvation and others to eternal damnation—is seen as severely compromising human free will and distorting the fundamental nature of God as a God of universal, self-emptying love.

Furthermore, Calvinism often relies heavily on a forensic, legalistic understanding of salvation (Penal Substitutionary Atonement), where God's wrath must be satisfied through the punishment of Christ. The Orthodox tradition, in contrast, views salvation not as a legal transaction in a cosmic courtroom, but as an ontological healing and rescue mission—Christ defeating death and the demonic powers to offer participation in the divine life (*theosis*) to all who freely choose to cooperate (synergy) with His grace.
