---
title: "Icons"
tags: [concept]
---
Icons are the sacred images venerated within the Orthodox Church, depicting Jesus Christ, the Virgin Mary (Theotokos), the angels, and the saints. They are central to Orthodox worship, adorning the walls of churches, the iconostasis (the screen separating the altar from the nave), and the homes of the faithful, creating an environment where the worshiper is visibly surrounded by the "great cloud of witnesses" (Hebrews 12:1).

The theology of icons was decisively defended and defined at the Seventh Ecumenical Council (787 AD) against the Iconoclasts (image-smashers). The Council clarified the crucial distinction between *latreia* (absolute worship and sacrifice, which belongs to God alone) and *proskynesis* (honor and veneration, which can be offered to holy people and objects). 

In the cosmic worldview of the *Lord of Spirits*, icons stand in direct opposition to pagan idols. While pagans used rituals to bind a localized deity into an idol to manipulate it, icons operate on the principle of free, loving communion. They do not contain the divine nature, but by the grace of the Holy Spirit, they become conduits of God's uncreated energies, facilitating prayer and drawing the believer's mind toward the heavenly reality.
