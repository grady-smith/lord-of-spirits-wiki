title: "Most High God"
tags: [concept]
---

The title "Most High God" (Hebrew: *El Elyon*) is used throughout the Old Testament to emphasize Yahweh’s absolute supremacy over all other spiritual beings and gods. In the ancient Near East, the title "Elyon" denoted the supreme deity or the head of a pantheon. By attributing this title to Yahweh, the biblical authors are making a definitive claim about His unique and uncreated status above the Divine Council.

In the *Lord of Spirits* framework, understanding God as the "Most High" is crucial for interpreting the spiritual topography of the ancient world. The biblical text acknowledges the existence of other spiritual beings (elohim) who rule over the nations, but fiercely insists that Yahweh is *El Elyon*—He is not merely the greatest among peers, but the uncreated Creator to whom all other beings owe their existence and allegiance.

This title appears prominently in the story of Melchizedek (Genesis 14), who was a priest of "God Most High," demonstrating that true knowledge of the supreme Creator existed outside the lineage of Abraham. It is also used heavily in demonic encounters in the New Testament (e.g., Mark 5:7), where unclean spirits are forced to recognize the ultimate authority of Jesus as the Son of the Most High God, signaling the defeat of the rebellious powers.
