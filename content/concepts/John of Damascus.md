---
title: "John of Damascus"
tags: [concept]
---
St. John of Damascus (c. 675–749 AD) was a Syrian monk, priest, and brilliant theologian who lived under early Islamic rule. He is often considered the last of the great Eastern Church Fathers. He is best known for his comprehensive synthesis of Christian theology, *An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith*, and his profound hymnography, which still shapes the liturgical life of the Orthodox Church today.

His most critical theological contribution was his fierce defense of holy icons during the Iconoclast controversy. Drawing deeply upon the reality of the Incarnation, St. John argued that because the invisible, uncircumscribable God took on physical flesh and matter in Jesus Christ, creating images of Christ is not only permissible but essential. To reject the icon is to implicitly deny that the Word truly became flesh and sanctified the material world.

In the *Lord of Spirits* framework, St. John of Damascus perfectly articulates the difference between pagan idolatry and Christian veneration. He famously stated, "I do not worship matter, I worship the God of matter, who became matter for my sake." He clarified that the honor given to an icon passes to its heavenly prototype, affirming that the physical creation is inherently good and capable of mediating the uncreated grace of God.
