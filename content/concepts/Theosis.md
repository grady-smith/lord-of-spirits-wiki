---
title: "Theosis"
tags: [concept]
---

Theosis, or deification, is the central soteriological concept in Eastern Orthodox theology. It describes the transformative process by which human beings, through the grace of God and union with Christ, become "partakers of the divine nature" (2 Peter 1:4). It is not a process of becoming exactly what God is in His essence, which remains strictly unapproachable, but of participating fully in His uncreated energies—His love, glory, and eternal life. As St. Athanasius famously stated, "God became man so that man might become god."

Within the *Lord of Spirits* framework, theosis is deeply tied to the concept of the Divine Council and the original vocation of humanity. Adam and Eve were created to mature and eventually join the divine council as rulers alongside the spiritual beings. Because of the Fall, this destiny was interrupted, but Christ's victory over the principalities and powers reopens the path. Theosis is thus understood not merely as personal moral improvement, but as humanity reclaiming its rightful place in the cosmic hierarchy, ruling and reigning with Christ over the spiritual and material realms.
