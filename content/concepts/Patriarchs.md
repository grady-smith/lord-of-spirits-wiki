---
title: "Patriarchs"
tags: [concept]
---

The Patriarchs are the foundational fathers of the nation of Israel, specifically identified as Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. The Book of Genesis heavily focuses on their lives, detailing how God established His covenant with them, promising them numerous descendants and the land of Canaan.

In the theology of the *Lord of Spirits*, the narrative of the Patriarchs is a story of God creating a new humanity and a new portion for Himself following the division of the nations to rebellious spiritual beings at the Tower of Babel. By calling Abraham out of Ur (a center of Mesopotamian paganism and demonic idolatry), Yahweh initiates His long-term campaign to reclaim the entire world. The Patriarchs interacted directly with the Angel of the Lord, functioning as early participants in the Divine Council.

The God of Orthodoxy is frequently identified as the "God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob." This specific, historical identification grounds Christian theology in reality, rejecting abstract philosophical deities. Christ asserts that God is "not the God of the dead, but of the living," indicating that the Patriarchs are currently alive in the heavenly realm, participating in the Divine Council and awaiting the final resurrection.
