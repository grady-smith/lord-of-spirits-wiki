---
title: "Christology"
tags: [concept]
---

Christology is the branch of theology concerned with the nature, person, and work of Jesus Christ. In the Orthodox tradition, Christology was heavily debated and definitively articulated during the first several Ecumenical Councils, particularly at Chalcedon. The orthodox definition asserts that Christ is one divine Person (the hypostasis of the Logos) possessing two complete and distinct natures—fully God and fully man—united without confusion, change, division, or separation. 

Within the ancient cosmic worldview discussed on the *Lord of Spirits*, correct Christology is crucial because it dictates the reality of salvation. If Christ is not fully God, He lacks the power to defeat death and the demonic realm. If He is not fully human, He cannot heal human nature and represent us within the Divine Council. Christology is therefore not an abstract philosophical puzzle, but the blueprint of the *Christus Victor* atonement. The Incarnation itself is seen as an invasion of enemy-occupied territory, where the Creator assumes the very nature of the enslaved creation to liberate it from within.
