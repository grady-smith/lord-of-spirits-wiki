title: "John"
tags: [concept]
---
In the context of the New Testament and Orthodox tradition, "John" most prominently refers to the Apostle John, the son of Zebedee, known as the "Beloved Disciple" and "John the Theologian." He was a member of Christ’s inner circle alongside Peter and James, uniquely present at the Transfiguration, the crucifixion (where Christ entrusted His Mother to him), and the empty tomb.

The Gospel of John, along with his three Epistles and the Book of Revelation, forms a cornerstone of Christian theology. Unlike the Synoptic Gospels, John's Gospel begins not with an earthly genealogy, but in eternity: "In the beginning was the Word (Logos)..." This firmly establishes Christ's pre-existence and absolute divinity, providing the precise vocabulary the early Church used to defend the Trinity against later heresies.

In the *Lord of Spirits* context, John’s writings are steeped in Second Temple Jewish concepts of the unseen realm and cosmic warfare. The Book of Revelation is the ultimate unveiling of the spiritual reality behind human history, depicting the final victory of the Lamb over the Dragon (Satan), the fallen angelic powers, and their earthly political proxies, culminating in the marriage of heaven and earth in the New Jerusalem.
