---
title: "Mount Sinai"
tags: [concept]
---

Mount Sinai (also known as Horeb) is the sacred mountain where Yahweh appeared to Moses and the Israelites, forging a covenant with them and delivering the Law (Torah). In ancient Near Eastern cosmology, mountains were understood as the dwelling places of the gods and the intersection between heaven and earth. Sinai serves as the ultimate cosmic mountain in the Torah, the place where the heavenly throne room descended to interface with humanity.

The *Lord of Spirits* emphasizes that the events at Mount Sinai were profoundly liturgical and experiential. When Moses ascended the mountain, he was not just receiving legal texts; he was entering into the Divine Council. The pattern of the Tabernacle given to Moses was a direct earthly copy of the heavenly reality he witnessed on Sinai, allowing the divine presence to travel with the Israelites.

Sinai stands in direct opposition to the pagan cosmic mountains (like Mount Zaphon or Mount Hermon). It represents the restoration of divine order, where God makes Himself known not to tyrannical giant-kings, but to a people liberated from slavery, providing them with the ritual purity laws necessary to safely host His holy presence in a fallen world.
