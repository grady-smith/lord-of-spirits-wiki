---
title: "Adonai"
tags: [concept]
---
"Adonai" is a Hebrew title meaning "Lord" or "Master." In the tradition of Second Temple Judaism and continuing into Orthodox Christianity, it became the standard spoken substitute for the Tetragrammaton, the divine name of God (Yahweh). Out of extreme reverence and to prevent taking the name of God in vain, Jewish readers would say "Adonai" whenever they encountered the divine name in the biblical text, a practice reflected in most English Bibles which render it as "LORD" in all capital letters.

In the context of the ancient Near East and the *Lord of Spirits* podcast, the title Adonai strongly emphasizes God's absolute sovereignty and kingship. In a world populated by many spiritual beings and national gods, calling Yahweh "Adonai" asserts that He is the supreme ruler over the entire Divine Council and all creation. He is not merely one god among many, but the Master to whom all other powers must ultimately submit.

This title is deeply significant in Christology. When the New Testament authors, particularly St. Paul, apply the Greek equivalent *Kyrios* (Lord) to Jesus Christ (e.g., "Jesus is Lord"), they are making a profound, radical claim. They are directly attributing the title Adonai, the supreme sovereignty of the God of Israel, to Jesus, identifying Him as the incarnate Yahweh who has come to judge the rebellious powers and reclaim His creation.
