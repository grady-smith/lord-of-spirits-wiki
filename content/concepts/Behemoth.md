---
title: "Behemoth"
tags: [concept]
---

Behemoth is a primordial creature mentioned in the Book of Job (Job 40), often paired with the sea monster Leviathan. While modern readers frequently attempt to identify Behemoth as a hippopotamus or dinosaur, in the context of the Ancient Near East and biblical cosmology, Behemoth represents a chaotic, untamable land beast of mythical proportions. Derived from the plural form of the Hebrew word for "beast" (an intensive plural denoting the "ultimate beast"), Behemoth embodies the raw, terrifying, and chaotic power of the terrestrial world, just as Leviathan embodies the chaos of the sea.

In the *Lord of Spirits* framework, God's speech to Job regarding Behemoth and Leviathan is not merely a natural history lesson, but a declaration of divine sovereignty over the forces of chaos. God boasts that He alone created Behemoth and He alone can tame or conquer him. This imagery serves to comfort Job by revealing that while chaos and suffering exist in the fallen world—often animated by hostile spiritual powers—they are ultimately subordinate to the Most High God, who will finally subjugate all chaos and bring complete order to His creation.
