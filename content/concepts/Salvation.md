---
title: "Salvation"
tags: [concept]
---

In Orthodox Christian theology, salvation (soteriology) is understood not as a legal transaction, but as a dynamic, ongoing process of healing and transformation. It is deeply rooted in the concept of *theosis* or deification—the process by which human beings, through grace, become participants in the divine nature. This contrasts sharply with Western models that often view salvation primarily in terms of penal substitution, where Christ takes the punishment for human sin to satisfy divine justice.

The *Lord of Spirits* podcast emphasizes that salvation in the biblical worldview is intrinsically linked to deliverance from the oppressive powers of sin, death, and the devil. Humanity, captured by these dark forces following the Fall, requires rescue. Christ’s incarnation, crucifixion, descent into Hades, and resurrection constitute the ultimate victory over these captors. Salvation, therefore, is an exodus from slavery to the demonic realm into the freedom of God's Kingdom.

Furthermore, salvation is deeply communal and cosmic. It is not merely about an individual soul going to heaven, but about the restoration of all creation. Through participation in the sacramental life of the Church—especially Baptism and the Eucharist—believers are incorporated into the Body of Christ and begin to experience the resurrected life here and now. Salvation is a continuous journey of repentance, purification, and illumination, culminating in eternal communion with the Holy Trinity.
