---
title: "Uriel"
tags: [concept]
---

Uriel is traditionally recognized in Orthodox and Second Temple Jewish literature as one of the primary Archangels. His name translates to "God is my light" or "Fire of God." While not explicitly named in the canonical Protestant or Roman Catholic Bibles, Uriel figures prominently in the Book of Enoch, 2 Esdras, and other apocalyptic texts. He is frequently depicted as a guide for prophets, an angel of profound wisdom, and a stern executor of divine justice.

The *Lord of Spirits* frequently references Uriel precisely because of his prominence in the Enochic literature, which is highly valued in the Orthodox tradition. In the Book of Enoch, Uriel is the angel who oversees Tartarus and the imprisoned, rebellious Watchers; he guides Enoch through the cosmos, explaining the mechanics of the heavens and the judgment awaiting the wicked spiritual beings. He represents the fierce, holy order of the Divine Council maintaining the boundaries of creation against the forces of chaos.
