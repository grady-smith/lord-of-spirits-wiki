---
title: "Isaiah"
tags: [concept]
---
Isaiah was a major prophet of the 8th century BC in the southern Kingdom of Judah, active during a period of massive geopolitical upheaval caused by the expanding Assyrian Empire. His extensive prophetic book is one of the most theological and frequently quoted texts in the New Testament, offering profound visions of God's holiness, judgment, and ultimate restoration.

A central element of Isaiah's ministry, highly relevant to the *Lord of Spirits*, is his dramatic calling in Isaiah 6. He receives a vision of Yahweh seated on a throne in the heavenly temple, surrounded by the Seraphim. This is a direct glimpse into the Divine Council. When a seraph purifies Isaiah's lips with a burning coal from the heavenly altar, it symbolizes the purification necessary for a human to participate in the council and speak as God's true prophet.

Furthermore, Isaiah is uniquely significant in Orthodox theology for his vivid prophecies regarding the Messiah. He foretells the virgin birth (Immanuel, "God with us") and provides the haunting description of the "Suffering Servant" in Isaiah 53, who bears the transgressions of the people. This imagery fundamentally shaped the early Church's understanding of Jesus Christ's incarnation, atoning death, and subsequent glorification over the fallen powers.
