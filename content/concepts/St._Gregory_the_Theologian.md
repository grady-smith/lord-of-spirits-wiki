Saint Gregory the Theologian (c. 329–390), also known as Gregory of Nazianzus, is one of the three Cappadocian Fathers and one of only three saints in the Orthodox Church explicitly granted the title "Theologian" (along with St. John the Apostle and St. Symeon the New Theologian). He served briefly as Archbishop of Constantinople and was instrumental in the final defeat of Arianism and the formalization of Trinitarian dogma at the Second Ecumenical Council (381).

In Orthodox theology, St. Gregory is most celebrated for his *Five Theological Orations*, delivered in Constantinople. These sermons brilliantly articulated the orthodox understanding of the Holy Trinity: that God is one essence (*ousia*) in three distinct persons (*hypostases*). He provided crucial terminology to distinguish the persons, stating that the Father is "unbegotten," the Son is "begotten," and the Holy Spirit "proceeds" from the Father. This language fundamentally shaped the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed.

The *Lord of Spirits* podcast emphasizes the patristic understanding of theology as a way of life, an idea perfectly embodied by Gregory. For him, a "theologian" was not merely an academic, but someone whose heart had been purified through asceticism and prayer, allowing them to truly "see" God. He eloquently taught that Christ assumed human nature in its entirety in order to heal it in its entirety, famously stating against the Apollinarian heresy: "That which is unassumed is unhealed, but that which is united to His Godhead is also saved."
