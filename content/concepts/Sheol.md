---
title: "Sheol"
tags: [concept]
---

Sheol is the Hebrew term used in the Old Testament to describe the underworld, the abode of the dead. In the ancient Near Eastern worldview, shared by early Israelites, Sheol was not initially differentiated into heaven and hell as understood in later Christian theology. Instead, it was a shadowy, subterranean existence where all the dead—both righteous and wicked—went after life. It was a place characterized by dust, silence, and forgetfulness, where the dead were cut off from the land of the living and, seemingly, from the active praise of Yahweh.

The *Lord of Spirits* podcast heavily emphasizes that Sheol (often translated as Hades in the Greek Septuagint) was understood as a realm of captivity. Death was not merely a biological cessation but a cosmic power that held humanity hostage following the Fall. The shadowy existence in Sheol represented a diminishing of the human person, a state of profound alienation from the source of life, God Himself. Throughout the Old Testament, there is a developing hope that God will eventually rescue His faithful ones from the power of Sheol (e.g., Psalm 49:15).

In Orthodox theology, the ultimate answer to the problem of Sheol is Christ's descent into Hades (the Harrowing of Hell) following His crucifixion. By entering the realm of the dead, the Author of Life shattered the gates of Sheol, binding the strong man (Satan or Death personified) and liberating the captives, beginning with Adam and Eve. Sheol is thus defeated, and the righteous dead are brought into Paradise, transforming the afterlife from a shadowy prison into a state of communion with the resurrected Christ.
