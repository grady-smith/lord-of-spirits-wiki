---
title: "Zion"
tags: [concept]
---

Mount Zion originally referred to the fortress of the Jebusites conquered by King David, becoming the City of David in Jerusalem. It later became synonymous with the Temple Mount, the dwelling place of Yahweh's presence on earth. In the prophetic literature, Zion is elevated beyond a mere geographical location to represent the eschatological, cosmic mountain of God, the ultimate center of the renewed creation from which divine law and life flow to all nations.

In the theology of the *Lord of Spirits*, Zion represents the definitive intersection of the seen and unseen realms. It is the earthly counterpart to the heavenly throne room of the Divine Council. Following the Incarnation, Resurrection, and Ascension of Christ, the concept of Zion is fulfilled in the Church. The Orthodox liturgy explicitly states that when the faithful gather for the Eucharist, they have "come to Mount Zion, and unto the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem," participating directly in the eternal, heavenly worship of the Most High.
