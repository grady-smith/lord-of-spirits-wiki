---
title: "Orthros"
tags: [concept]
---

Orthros (Greek for "dawn" or "daybreak") is the longest and most complex of the daily cycle of services in the Orthodox Church, corresponding to the Western office of Matins. It is typically celebrated in the morning, immediately preceding the Divine Liturgy on Sundays and major feast days.

In the *Lord of Spirits* and Orthodox liturgical theology, Orthros is a profound act of joining the angelic choirs in their unceasing praise of God. The service is heavily structured around the chanting of the Psalms (particularly the Hexapsalmos or Six Psalms, and the Praises), bridging the darkness of night with the arrival of the true Light, Jesus Christ. It is a daily re-enactment of the transition from the Old Covenant to the New, culminating in the proclamation of the Gospel.

By participating in Orthros, the faithful are actively engaging the spiritual world. The singing of the Psalms has long been understood as a primary weapon of spiritual warfare, driving away demonic forces and aligning the human heart with the order of the Divine Council. The service prepares the community, both physically and spiritually, to enter into the presence of God in the Eucharist.
