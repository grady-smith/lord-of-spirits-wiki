---
title: "Nicene Creed"
tags: [concept]
---

The Nicene Creed (more accurately the Niceno-Constantinopolitan Creed) is the foundational statement of faith for the Orthodox Christian Church, originally formulated at the First Ecumenical Council in Nicaea (325 AD) and finalized at the Second Ecumenical Council in Constantinople (381 AD). It was written primarily to combat the Arian heresy, which claimed that the Son of God was a created being.

In the context of the *Lord of Spirits*, the Creed is not merely a list of theological propositions; it is the definitive mapping of the Divine Council and the nature of the Godhead. By declaring that the Son is "of one essence" (*homoousios*) with the Father, the Creed violently severs Christianity from pagan and Arian hierarchies of lesser gods. It asserts that the Angel of the Lord who appeared in the Old Testament is not a created angel, but the uncreated God Himself.

The Creed is recited at every Divine Liturgy, serving as a boundary marker for Orthodox belief and a confession of allegiance to the Holy Trinity. It articulates the total narrative of salvation history—from the creation of the visible and invisible worlds, to the Incarnation, the Harrowing of Hades, the Resurrection, and the final eschatological judgment.
