---
title: "Elohim"
tags: [concept]
---
In biblical Hebrew, *elohim* is a grammatically plural noun most commonly translated as "God" when referring to Yahweh, the Creator. However, it is also frequently used in the Hebrew Bible to refer to other spiritual beings, such as the gods of the foreign nations, members of the Divine Council, demons, and even the disembodied spirits of the human dead (as with the spirit of Samuel in 1 Samuel 28).

As thoroughly explored in *The Lord of Spirits* podcast, *elohim* is not a term that denotes a specific set of omnipotent attributes (like the modern English word "God"). Instead, it is a "place of residence" term—it refers to any entity whose primary domain is the spiritual, Unseen Realm rather than the physical world. While all gods, angels, and spirits are *elohim*, Yahweh is the unique, uncreated Creator of all other *elohim*. Understanding this ancient taxonomy is crucial for making sense of passages like Psalm 82, where God (Yahweh) stands in the divine council to judge the other gods (*elohim*).
