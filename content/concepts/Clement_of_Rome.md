---
title: "Clement of Rome"
tags: [concept]
---

St. Clement of Rome, also known as Pope Clement I, was an Apostolic Father and the bishop of Rome in the late 1st century. According to early tradition (attested by Irenaeus and Tertullian), Clement was consecrated by the Apostle Peter and is often identified as the Clement mentioned by Paul in Philippians 4:3. His most famous surviving work, the *First Epistle of Clement to the Corinthians* (1 Clement), was written around 96 AD to address a rebellion against the local presbyters in the church of Corinth, and it was so highly regarded that it was read alongside scripture in many early churches.

In the context of the *Lord of Spirits* and early Christian ecclesiology, Clement's letter is a vital testament to the apostolic succession and the hierarchical order of the Church. Clement explicitly ties the structure of the earthly Church (bishops, presbyters, and deacons) to the order of the heavenly realm and the Old Testament priesthood. He argues that rebelling against the duly appointed leadership is not just an administrative dispute, but a disruption of the divine order and a submission to the chaotic influence of the demonic realm. His writings affirm that the Church's structure is a reflection of the ordered, faithful Divine Council.
