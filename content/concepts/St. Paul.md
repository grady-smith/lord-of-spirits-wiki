---
title: "St. Paul"
tags: [concept]
---

St. Paul the Apostle, originally Saul of Tarsus, is a central figure in the New Testament and a key architect of early Christian theology. A former Pharisee deeply versed in Second Temple Jewish traditions, Paul experienced a dramatic conversion on the road to Damascus when he encountered the risen Christ. His epistles form the theological backbone of the Church, addressing issues of justification, the inclusion of the Gentiles, the nature of the Church as the body of Christ, and the ultimate consummation of all things.

In the *Lord of Spirits* paradigm, St. Paul is essential for his robust articulation of the divine council worldview and spiritual warfare. Paul frequently refers to the "principalities and powers" (Ephesians 6:12) and the "rulers of this present darkness," drawing directly from the Second Temple Jewish understanding of rebellious divine beings who subjugate the nations. Paul’s theology is fundamentally triumphant: he views the crucifixion and resurrection of Christ as the definitive defeat of these dark powers, initiating a process of reclaiming the cosmos for the Most High God.
