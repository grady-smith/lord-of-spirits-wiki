---
title: "Heresy"
tags: [concept]
---
In its original Greek context, *hairesis* simply meant a "choice" or a "faction," often referring to different philosophical schools or sects within Judaism, such as the Pharisees or Sadducees. However, within the Christian Church, the term evolved to designate a deliberate choice to deviate from the established, orthodox faith handed down from the Apostles.

In Orthodox theology, heresy is not merely an intellectual error or a harmless difference of opinion; it is viewed as a spiritual sickness that distorts the reality of who God is and, consequently, how humanity is saved. Because theology directly impacts soteriology (the doctrine of salvation), heretical teachings—such as Arianism or Gnosticism—are dangerous because they offer a false Christ who cannot heal or unite humanity to God.

The *Lord of Spirits* often highlights how heresies frequently re-package ancient pagan concepts or reintroduce the worship of lesser spiritual beings. The Ecumenical Councils were convened not to invent new doctrines, but to clearly define the boundaries of the apostolic tradition against these distortions, protecting the Church's therapeutic mission and ensuring the faithful transmission of the truth of the Incarnation and the Holy Trinity.
