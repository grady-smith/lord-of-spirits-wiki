---
title: "Azazel"
tags: [concept]
---

Azazel is a complex and highly significant figure in the religious traditions of Second Temple Judaism and the Ancient Near East. In the biblical text of Leviticus 16, detailing the Day of Atonement (Yom Kippur), two goats are selected: one is sacrificed to Yahweh to purify the sanctuary, while the other—the scapegoat—has the sins of the people laid upon it and is sent out into the wilderness "to Azazel." In ancient Israelite understanding, the wilderness was the domain of chaos, death, and demonic forces, standing in opposition to the ordered, sacred space of the Temple.

In later Second Temple texts like 1 Enoch, Azazel is explicitly identified as one of the chief fallen angels (Watchers) who rebelled against God, corrupted humanity by teaching forbidden arts (like warfare and sorcery), and brought defilement upon the earth. From the perspective of Orthodox theology and the *Lord of Spirits* podcast, sending the goat to Azazel is not an offering or sacrifice to a demon, but a symbolic return of sin and corruption to its demonic source. It represents an act of spiritual warfare, banishing the defilement of sin back to the realm of the fallen powers, prefiguring Christ's ultimate defeat of death and the devil.
