---
title: "Icon"
tags: [concept]
---
In Orthodox theology, an icon (from the Greek *eikon*, meaning "image") is a sacred, liturgical image of Christ, the Theotokos, angels, or saints, typically painted on wood. However, an icon is far more than religious art or a decorative illustration; it is a theological statement in color and line, serving as a "window into heaven" that facilitates a real, spiritual communion between the believer and the person depicted.

The foundational basis for icons in Christianity is the Incarnation of the Word. In the Old Testament, God was invisible and uncircumscribable, making images forbidden (idolatry). But as St. John of Damascus argued, because the invisible God became visible, material, and circumscribable in the person of Jesus Christ, making an image of Christ is a necessary affirmation that the Incarnation was real. To reject the icon is to implicitly deny that God truly became flesh.

In the context of the *Lord of Spirits*, an icon is understood through the ancient concept of participation. The honor and veneration given to an icon passes to its prototype. An icon is not an idol (which is an empty shell meant to house a demonic spirit), but a Spirit-filled witness to the resurrected reality of the saints, demonstrating that matter itself has been redeemed and can mediate divine grace.
