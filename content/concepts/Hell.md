---
title: "Hell"
tags: [concept]
---
In ancient Near Eastern and early biblical thought, the concept of the afterlife was often localized in "Sheol" or "Hades," a shadowy underworld of the dead encompassing all departed souls regardless of their moral standing. It was viewed as a place of distance from the presence of God, where the dead existed in a diminished state. Over time, particularly in Second Temple Judaism, distinctions within the underworld emerged, conceptualizing compartments for the righteous (Paradise/Abraham's Bosom) and the wicked (Tartarus/Gehenna).

From an Orthodox perspective, Hell is not primarily a spatial location created by God as a torture chamber, but rather a relational state regarding one's experience of God's uncreated energies. As articulated by Church Fathers like St. Isaac the Syrian, the "fire" of Hell and the "light" of Heaven are the same: the infinite love and presence of God. To those who love God, His presence is illuminating and joyful; to those who hate Him or are attached to sin, that exact same presence is experienced as a burning torment.

The *Lord of Spirits* podcast often emphasizes the defeat of Hell (Hades) through Christ's descent into the underworld (the Harrowing of Hell). By entering the realm of the dead, Christ shattered its gates and emptied its power, offering liberation to the captives. Thus, eternal condemnation is understood as a human soul's final, obstinate refusal of God's universal, healing love, essentially choosing the isolation of Hell over communion with the Creator.
