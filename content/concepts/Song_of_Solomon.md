The Song of Solomon (also known as the Song of Songs) is a poetic book in the Hebrew Bible attributed to King Solomon. It is a collection of deeply passionate, erotic love poetry shared between a bridegroom and his bride. Unlike most other biblical books, it contains almost no explicit theological language, law, or prophecy, and the name of God is largely absent. This led to historical debates within both Jewish and Christian traditions regarding its inclusion in the biblical canon.

In the ancient Near Eastern context, similar love poetry existed, often associated with sacred marriage rituals or fertility cults. However, the *Lord of Spirits* podcast and the broader Orthodox tradition understand the Song of Solomon not as a pagan remnant, but as a profound allegory of divine love. The prophets (like Hosea and Ezekiel) frequently used the metaphor of marriage to describe Yahweh's covenantal relationship with Israel. The Song of Songs is read as the ultimate expression of this intense, pursuing, and jealous love that God has for His people.

For Orthodox Christianity, the Song of Solomon is the quintessential text of mystical theology. Church Fathers, notably St. Gregory of Nyssa, wrote extensive commentaries on it, interpreting the bridegroom as Christ (the Logos) and the bride as the human soul or the Church. The passionate desire expressed in the poetry describes the soul's ascent toward God (theosis), driven by *eros* (desiring love) for the divine beauty. The text beautifully illustrates that salvation is not merely a legal standing, but a deeply intimate, transformative romance with the Creator.
