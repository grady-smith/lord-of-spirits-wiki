---
title: "Feast of Tabernacles"
tags: [concept]
---

The Feast of Tabernacles (Sukkot) is one of the three major pilgrimage festivals in ancient Israel, celebrated in the autumn to commemorate the Israelites' dwelling in tents during their wilderness journey and to give thanks for the harvest. In the ancient Near East, such autumn festivals were common and often associated with the enthronement of the deity and the securing of rain and fertility for the coming year. For Israel, it was a profound reminder of God's provision and presence among them in the wilderness.

In Orthodox Christian theology and the *Lord of Spirits* framework, the Feast of Tabernacles holds significant eschatological meaning. It anticipates the ultimate ingathering of the nations and the final dwelling of God with humanity in the new creation. The Transfiguration of Christ, where Peter offers to build three "tabernacles" (tents), is understood as a fulfillment of this feast, revealing Christ's uncreated glory and prefiguring the permanent dwelling of the Divine Presence with mankind.
