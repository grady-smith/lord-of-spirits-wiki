---
title: "Greece"
tags: [concept]
---

In the context of the biblical narrative and the ancient Near East, Greece (often referred to as Javan or Hellen) represents the cultural and political powerhouse of the Mediterranean world, particularly following the conquests of Alexander the Great. The resulting spread of Hellenism deeply impacted the ancient world, introducing Greek philosophy, language, and polytheistic religion into the Levant and challenging the traditional life of Second Temple Judaism.

For the early Church and Orthodox theology, the relationship with Greece is complex and foundational. While fiercely rejecting Greek paganism and idolatry, the Church heavily utilized the Greek language (the language of the Septuagint and the New Testament) and engaged deeply with Greek philosophical concepts (such as the *Logos*) to articulate Christian theology. The early Church Fathers "baptized" the Hellenistic intellectual tradition, using its vocabulary to express the incomprehensible mysteries of the Incarnation and the Holy Trinity.
