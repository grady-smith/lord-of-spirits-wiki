---
title: "Gospel"
tags: [concept]
---

The term "Gospel" originates from the Greek word *euangelion*, meaning "good news." In the Greco-Roman world, an *euangelion* was a royal proclamation announcing a significant event, such as a military victory or the ascension of a new emperor. In the Christian context, the Gospel is the ultimate royal proclamation: that Jesus of Nazareth is the long-awaited Messiah, the Son of God, who through His life, death, and resurrection has defeated sin and death, and is now installed as the rightful King of the cosmos.

In Orthodox theology, the Gospel is not merely a set of intellectual propositions but the active power of God for salvation. It is proclaimed liturgically and lived out sacramentally in the Church. The four canonical Gospels (Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John) are the foundational texts that record this narrative, each offering a unique theological perspective on the Incarnation and the inauguration of the Kingdom of God, inviting all of humanity into a restored relationship with the Creator.
