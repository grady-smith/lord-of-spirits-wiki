---
title: "Communion"
tags: [concept]
---

In Orthodox theology, Communion (or the Eucharist) is the central act of Christian worship and the ultimate mystery (sacrament) of the Church. It is the partaking of the consecrated bread and wine, which the Church believes to be the true, objective Body and Blood of Jesus Christ. Far from being a mere symbolic memorial, Communion is a physical, transfiguring participation in the life, death, and resurrection of Christ, bridging the gap between the material world and the uncreated divine nature.

Within the ancient cosmology discussed on the *Lord of Spirits*, Communion is fundamentally understood as a sacrificial meal. In the Ancient Near East, sharing a sacrificial meal was the primary way humanity established covenantal fellowship with a deity. By partaking in Communion, Christians are actively communing with the Most High God, drawing His life into themselves. Furthermore, it serves as a cosmic counter-measure to idolatry; it is the table of the Lord that stands in direct opposition to the "table of demons" (1 Cor 10:21), actively displacing the corruption of sin and death within the believer and the world.
