---
title: "Baruch"
tags: [concept]
---

Baruch ben Neriah was the faithful scribe, disciple, and close companion of the prophet Jeremiah during the tumultuous final days of the Kingdom of Judah and the subsequent Babylonian exile. According to the biblical record, Baruch was responsible for transcribing Jeremiah’s prophecies, reading them publicly in the Temple, and enduring the intense persecution that resulted from their unpopular message of impending judgment and the necessity of submission to Babylon.

In Second Temple Judaism and Orthodox tradition, Baruch is also the namesake of several pseudepigraphal and deuterocanonical works, most notably the *Book of Baruch* (included in the Orthodox Old Testament). These texts expand on the theological problem of the exile, the justice of God, and the hope for restoration. From a cosmological perspective, the writings associated with Baruch address how Israel can maintain fidelity to the Most High God while living in a land dominated by foreign, demonically-backed empires. They emphasize the necessity of repentance and the futility of idolatry, reiterating the cosmic exclusivity of Yahweh.
