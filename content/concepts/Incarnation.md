---
title: "Incarnation"
tags: [concept]
---
The Incarnation is the central dogma of the Christian faith: the event where the eternal, uncreated Word of God (the Logos, the second Person of the Trinity) took on human nature and flesh, becoming fully man in the person of Jesus of Nazareth while remaining fully God. As articulated in the Nicene Creed, He "was incarnate of the Holy Spirit and the Virgin Mary and became man."

In the context of the *Lord of Spirits* and ancient theology, the Incarnation is the ultimate invasion of the fallen world by the Creator. Humanity had been enslaved by death and the rebellious spiritual powers. By uniting human nature to His divine nature, Christ bridged the infinite gap between God and creation, bringing the uncreated life of God into the depths of human mortality to heal, sanctify, and raise it.

Orthodox theology emphasizes that the Incarnation was not merely a legal transaction to pay a debt, but a cosmic rescue mission. As St. Athanasius famously stated, "God became man so that man might become god" (*theosis*). Because human nature is now seated at the right hand of the Father in the person of Christ, the path to eternal union with God has been irrevocably opened for all of humanity.
