---
title: "Assumption of Moses"
tags: [concept]
---

The *Assumption of Moses* (often conflated or overlapping with the *Testament of Moses*) is a first-century Jewish pseudepigraphal work that details Moses' final prophecies to Joshua before his death. The text is highly significant for biblical studies because it contains apocalyptic visions and expectations regarding the end times, divine judgment, and the role of angelic beings. Most notably, early Christian writers like Origen and Clement of Alexandria indicated that this lost or fragmented text contained the story of a dispute between the Archangel Michael and the devil over the body of Moses.

This specific tradition is directly referenced in the New Testament in the Epistle of Jude (Jude 1:9). In the context of Orthodox theology and the *Lord of Spirits*, this text exemplifies the rich Second Temple Jewish understanding of spiritual warfare and the angelic hierarchy. The conflict over Moses' body underscores the reality of the unseen realm, where high-ranking angels like Michael act as defenders of God's people against the claims of rebellious spiritual powers. It also highlights how early Christians seamlessly integrated these Second Temple traditions into their understanding of the cosmos and the ongoing battle between God's faithful council and the demonic realm.
