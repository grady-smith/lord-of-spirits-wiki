St. Thomas, one of the Twelve Apostles, is famously known in Western culture as "Doubting Thomas" due to his initial skepticism regarding Christ's resurrection. However, in Eastern Orthodox tradition, this moment is celebrated not primarily as doubt, but as the "Good Confession" of Thomas, where upon touching the wounds of Christ, he declares, "My Lord and my God!" (John 20:28). This statement is one of the clearest and most definitive affirmations of Christ's full divinity in the New Testament.

Beyond the Gospels, St. Thomas is traditionally credited with carrying the Christian faith far beyond the borders of the Roman Empire, journeying as far as India to establish the Church. Within the *Lord of Spirits* paradigm, the apostolic mission of Thomas represents the aggressive expansion of the Kingdom of God into territories long held by pagan, demonic principalities. By planting the Church in distant lands, Thomas was actively participating in the reclamation of the nations, reclaiming humanity from the rebellious divine beings assigned at the Tower of Babel.
