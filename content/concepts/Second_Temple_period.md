---
title: "Second Temple period"
tags: [concept]
---

The Second Temple period is the historical era in Jewish history extending from the late sixth century BCE to 70 CE. It began with the return of Jewish exiles from Babylon and the subsequent rebuilding of the Temple in Jerusalem under Persian rule. The period is marked by successive waves of foreign empire domination: the Persians, the Hellenistic kingdoms (Ptolemies and Seleucids) following Alexander the Great, a brief period of independence under the Hasmonean dynasty, and finally, the oppressive rule of the Roman Empire.

In the context of the *Lord of Spirits*, this period is crucial for understanding the cultural and spiritual environment of the biblical world. The encounter with Hellenism, in particular, sparked intense internal conflict regarding assimilation versus faithfulness to the Torah, famously culminating in the Maccabean Revolt. The pervasive reality of foreign occupation fueled apocalyptic hopes and the expectation of a divine intervention that would overthrow the pagan powers and establish God's kingdom on earth.

For Orthodox Christians, the Second Temple period is the "fullness of time" (Galatians 4:4) into which the Son of God was born. The historical realities of Roman occupation, the varied religious movements of the time, and the rich theological texts produced during these centuries form the necessary background for understanding the Incarnation. The era abruptly concluded with the catastrophic destruction of Jerusalem and the Temple in 70 CE, an event that profoundly reshaped both Judaism and the rapidly expanding Christian Church.
