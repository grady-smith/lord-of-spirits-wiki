---
title: "Creed"
tags: [concept]
---
A creed is a formal, authoritative statement of religious belief. In Orthodox Christianity, the term primarily refers to the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed, formulated at the First (325 AD) and Second (381 AD) Ecumenical Councils. It serves as the definitive boundary marker of the Christian faith, distilling the vast narrative of Scripture and apostolic tradition into a concise confession of the Holy Trinity, the Incarnation, the Church, and the resurrection of the dead.

In the context of the *Lord of Spirits* and ancient worldviews, the Creed functions not just as a list of intellectual propositions, but as a pledge of allegiance (a sacramentum) to Yahweh and His Christ, rejecting the claims of rival, fallen spiritual powers. Reciting the Creed in the context of the Divine Liturgy is a participatory act of worship that aligns the earthly congregation with the heavenly Divine Council, asserting the eternal, uncreated nature of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit against ancient pagan cosmologies and heretical distortions.
