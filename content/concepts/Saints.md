---
title: "Saints"
tags: [concept]
---

The term "saints" (plural) in the context of the New Testament and Orthodox tradition refers to the collective body of believers who have been set apart for God. Originally, the term was used by the Apostle Paul to address all baptized Christians in a local community, recognizing their holy status derived from their union with Christ. Over time, while all Christians are called to be saints, the Church began to use the term more specifically to honor those who have demonstrated heroic virtue, profound asceticism, or martyrdom, serving as luminous examples of the transformed life.

In the *Lord of Spirits* framework, the saints are understood as the new spiritual administration of the cosmos. As Christ defeats the fallen angelic beings (the "gods" of the nations), He elevates human beings—the saints—to take their place in the Divine Council. This fulfills the original mandate given to humanity in Eden to rule and subdue the earth as God's vice-regents. The saints, both living and departed, constitute the true Israel and the eschatological family of God.

The veneration of saints in Orthodoxy is deeply tied to the incarnation and the reality of the resurrection. Because Christ has conquered death, those who have fallen asleep in Him are not truly dead but are alive in His presence. The Church honors them through feast days, hymnography, and iconography, not as independent deities, but as clear windows through which the light of Christ shines. Their communal intercession is a vital aspect of the Church's ongoing spiritual life.
