---
title: "Old Testament"
tags: [concept]
---

The Old Testament, or the Hebrew Bible, is the first division of the Christian biblical canon, recording the creation of the world, the fall, the history of the nation of Israel, and the covenants God made with His people. It contains the Law (Torah), the Prophets, and the Writings.

In the *Lord of Spirits* framework, the Old Testament is fundamentally a record of cosmic warfare. It details Yahweh's ongoing campaign against the rebellious "sons of God"—the fallen angels who usurped the nations—and the demonic spirits (Nephilim) that plague the earth. Rather than a mere history of a Middle Eastern tribe, the Old Testament provides the essential mythological and theological vocabulary needed to understand the spiritual state of the universe.

Orthodox Christianity insists that the God of the Old Testament is identical to the God of the New Testament, firmly rejecting heresies like Marcionism. The Angel of the Lord who appears repeatedly in the Old Testament—wrestling with Jacob, appearing to Moses, and leading the armies of Israel—is the pre-incarnate Jesus Christ. Therefore, the Old Testament is fully Christian scripture, establishing the patterns, prophecies, and liturgical structures that are ultimately fulfilled in the Incarnation, crucifixion, and resurrection of Christ.
