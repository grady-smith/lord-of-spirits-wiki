---
title: "Holy Week"
tags: [concept]
---
Holy Week is the most sacred period in the Orthodox liturgical calendar, beginning with Lazarus Saturday and Palm Sunday and culminating in the celebration of Pascha (Easter). It is an intense, immersive liturgical journey that traces the final days of Jesus Christ's earthly ministry, His Passion, His crucifixion, His descent into Hades, and His glorious Resurrection.

From the perspective of ancient temple theology, Holy Week represents the ultimate fulfillment of the Israelite sacrificial system and the Day of Atonement. Christ enters Jerusalem as the true King and the spotless Passover Lamb. His crucifixion is not viewed merely as a legal penalty being paid, but as a cosmic battle—the definitive defeat of death, sin, and the demonic powers that held humanity captive. 

The services of Holy Week, particularly Holy Friday and Holy Saturday, powerfully express the Harrowing of Hell. As Christ's body rests in the tomb, His soul descends into the underworld to liberate the dead. Thus, Holy Week is not just a historical commemoration, but a real, participatory entry into the victory of the Lord of Spirits over the principalities of darkness, restoring creation to communion with God.
