---
title: "Dead Sea Scrolls"
tags: [concept]
---
The Dead Sea Scrolls are a vast collection of ancient Jewish manuscripts discovered between 1947 and 1956 in the Qumran Caves near the Dead Sea. Dating from the 3rd century BCE to the 1st century CE, these texts include the oldest surviving copies of the Hebrew Bible, as well as an array of sectarian documents, apocalyptic literature, and extra-biblical texts (such as 1 Enoch and Jubilees) that were highly influential in Second Temple Judaism.

In the *Lord of Spirits* and Orthodox context, the Dead Sea Scrolls are immensely significant because they preserve the theological worldview that the Apostles and the early Church inherited. Rather than a modern, demythologized faith, the Qumran community’s writings reveal a deeply enchanted cosmos engaged in active spiritual warfare. Their texts frequently discuss the Divine Council, the Watchers, the Nephilim, and the ongoing battle between the sons of light and the demonic forces of darkness, corroborating the ancient, supernatural context essential to understanding the New Testament.
