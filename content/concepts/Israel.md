---
title: "Israel"
tags: [concept]
---
In the biblical narrative, "Israel" originates as the new name given to the patriarch Jacob after he wrestled with the Angel of the Lord (the pre-incarnate Christ) at Peniel. It means "he who struggles with God." The term subsequently became the name for the twelve tribes descended from Jacob, the nation chosen by God to be a "kingdom of priests and a holy nation" among the pagan powers of the ancient Near East.

In the cosmic worldview of the Old Testament, the establishment of Israel was God's beachhead in a world subjugated by fallen spiritual beings. Following the division of the nations at the Tower of Babel, where God allotted the nations to lesser divine beings who eventually became corrupt, Yahweh called out Abraham and created Israel as His own unique inheritance, tasked with bearing His name and blessing all nations.

In Orthodox theology, the concept of Israel is intimately tied to the Church. The New Testament and the Church Fathers do not teach that the Church "replaced" Israel (Replacement Theology), but rather that the Church *is* the continuation and fulfillment of true Israel. Jesus Christ is the ultimate, faithful Israelite, and all who are united to Him—whether Jew or Gentile—are grafted into the singular, historic people of God, inheriting the promises and the covenant.
