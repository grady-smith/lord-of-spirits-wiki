St. Titus was an early Christian missionary and church leader, a close companion of St. Paul alongside Timothy. A Gentile convert, Titus served as Paul's emissary to the church in Corinth and was later tasked with organizing the church on the island of Crete. The Epistle to Titus provides crucial pastoral guidance on appointing qualified elders (presbyters), maintaining sound doctrine, and instructing the faithful in virtuous living amidst a profoundly corrupt pagan culture.

Within the context of the *Lord of Spirits*, the mission of Titus on Crete is emblematic of the early Church's strategy of spiritual warfare. Crete was a center of pagan mythology—traditionally the birthplace of Zeus—and its culture was notoriously deeply embedded in idolatry and moral decay. By establishing a structured, sacramental Christian community there, Titus was effectively planting a beachhead of the Kingdom of God in heavily contested, demonically controlled territory, aggressively displacing the local rebellious powers with the light of the Gospel.
