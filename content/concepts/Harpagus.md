---
title: "Harpagus"
tags: [concept]
---

Harpagus was a Median general from the 6th century BC, whose life and actions are famously recorded in the histories of Herodotus. According to the legend, the Median king Astyages ordered Harpagus to kill his infant grandson, Cyrus (who would become Cyrus the Great), due to a prophetic dream. Harpagus, unwilling to commit the murder, passed the task to a shepherd, who secretly raised the child. When Astyages discovered the deception years later, he punished Harpagus by secretly serving him the flesh of his own son at a banquet.

In historical and biblical contexts, Harpagus plays a crucial role in the transition of empire in the ancient Near East. Driven by revenge against Astyages, Harpagus later defected and aided Cyrus in overthrowing the Median Empire, leading to the establishment of the Achaemenid Persian Empire. This political upheaval directly paved the way for Cyrus the Great to issue the decree allowing the exiled Jews in Babylon to return to Jerusalem and rebuild the temple, fulfilling biblical prophecy.
