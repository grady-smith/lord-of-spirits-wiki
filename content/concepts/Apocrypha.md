---
title: "Apocrypha"
tags: [concept]
---
"Apocrypha" comes from a Greek word meaning "hidden things." In modern Protestant usage, it often refers pejoratively to the books of the Old Testament that are not found in the Hebrew Masoretic Text but were present in the Greek Septuagint. However, in Orthodox theology and the *Lord of Spirits* podcast, many of these books (such as Tobit, Sirach, Wisdom, and the Maccabees) are fully embraced as Deuterocanonical, meaning they are canonical Scripture.

Beyond the Deuterocanon, the term also loosely applies to a vast body of Second Temple Jewish literature, such as 1 Enoch, Jubilees, and 2 Baruch. While not officially canonized by the Eastern Orthodox Church (with the exception of 1 Enoch in Ethiopia), the podcast stresses that these texts are absolutely essential for understanding the biblical worldview. 

The New Testament authors were steeped in the theology of these "apocryphal" works. They provide the necessary context regarding the Watchers, the origin of demons, the structure of the unseen realm, and the cosmic nature of the Messiah's mission. Dismissing these texts entirely leaves modern readers disconnected from the ancient Near Eastern context and blind to the spiritual warfare explicitly assumed by Christ and the Apostles.
