---
title: "Hebrew Bible"
tags: [concept]
---
The Hebrew Bible is the collection of ancient Israelite scriptures that form the foundation of both Jewish and Christian theological traditions. Comprising the Law (Torah), Prophets (Nevi'im), and Writings (Ketuvim), it records the history of God’s covenantal relationship with Israel, from the creation of the cosmos to the return from Babylonian exile.

In Orthodox theology, particularly emphasized in the *Lord of Spirits* context, the Hebrew Bible is not a disjointed predecessor to the New Testament, but a thoroughly Christian text. The "God of the Old Testament" who interacts with humanity—walking in Eden, speaking to Moses, and appearing to the prophets—is understood to be the pre-incarnate Word (Logos), the second person of the Trinity.

Furthermore, the Hebrew Bible provides the critical ancient Near Eastern context for understanding the spiritual worldview of the apostolic church. Its texts are steeped in the reality of the Divine Council, territorial spirits, and the ongoing cosmic war between Yahweh and rebel divine beings, a framework essential for correctly interpreting the cosmic victory of Christ in the New Testament.
