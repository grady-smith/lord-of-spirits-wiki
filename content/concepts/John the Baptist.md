---
title: "John the Baptist"
tags: [concept]
---
St. John the Baptist, or the Forerunner, was the last and greatest of the Old Testament prophets. Miraculously born to the elderly priest Zechariah and Elizabeth, he lived a strict ascetic life in the Judean wilderness. His divine mission was to prepare the way for the Messiah, preaching a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins and identifying Jesus as the "Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world."

In the context of ancient Near Eastern and biblical theology, John embodies the spirit and power of Elijah. His rugged attire and wilderness ministry functioned as a sharp prophetic indictment of the corrupt, compromised religious leadership in Jerusalem (the Herodian dynasty and the Sadducees). His baptism in the Jordan River symbolized a new Exodus—calling the people to pass through the waters to re-enter the Kingdom of God, purified from the spiritual defilements of the age.

In Orthodox theology, John the Baptist holds a position of supreme honor, second only to the Theotokos (Virgin Mary) among humanity. He represents the culmination of the Law and the Prophets. Furthermore, tradition holds that after his martyrdom by Herod Antipas, John descended into Hades to serve once more as the Forerunner, preaching the impending arrival of Christ to the souls held captive in the underworld, preparing them for the Harrowing of Hell.
