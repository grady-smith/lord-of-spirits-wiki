---
title: "Origen"
tags: [concept]
---

Origen of Alexandria (c. 185 – c. 253 AD) was one of the most prolific, brilliant, and controversial early Christian theologians and biblical scholars. He is famous for his massive textual work, the *Hexapla*, and his allegorical method of biblical interpretation, which profoundly influenced later Church Fathers, particularly the Cappadocians. 

Despite his massive contributions, Origen's legacy in Orthodox theology is highly complex. The *Lord of Spirits* podcast discusses how some of Origen's heavily Platonized theological speculations crossed into error. He famously posited the pre-existence of souls and an extreme form of *apokatastasis*—the idea that all rational creatures, including Satan and the demons, would eventually be saved and restored to union with God.

These specific teachings, known as "Origenism," were condemned as heretical at the Fifth Ecumenical Council (553 AD). Origen tended to over-spiritualize the biblical text, sometimes divorcing the physical reality of the creation from its theological meaning, a tendency that the Orthodox Church corrected by emphasizing the goodness of the material world and the reality of human free will in accepting or rejecting God's love eternally.
