title: "Byzantine"
tags: [concept]
---

"Byzantine" refers to the continuation of the Roman Empire in its eastern provinces during Late Antiquity and the Middle Ages, with its capital in Constantinople (modern-day Istanbul). While the term itself is a later historical construct (its citizens simply called themselves "Romans"), the Byzantine Empire was the crucible wherein much of Orthodox Christian theology, liturgy, and ecclesiastical art was refined and codified. It hosted the Seven Ecumenical Councils, which definitively established the orthodox dogmas of the Trinity and the Incarnation against various heresies.

In the *Lord of Spirits* framework, the Byzantine period represents the historical era in which the early Church's cosmic worldview—inherited from Second Temple Judaism and the Apostles—was systematically defended and expressed. The rich iconography, majestic hymnography, and complex liturgical rites of the Byzantine tradition are not viewed merely as cultural artifacts, but as theological statements mapping the reality of the unseen realm. The Byzantine synthesis successfully integrated the understanding of the Divine Council, the angelic hierarchy, and the ongoing spiritual war into the permanent life and worship of the Orthodox Church.
