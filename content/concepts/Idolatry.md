---
title: "Idolatry"
tags: [concept]
---
In the ancient Near Eastern context, an idol was not merely a statue; it was a physical vessel prepared through specific rituals (like the "Opening of the Mouth" ceremony) to house the spirit of a deity. Idolatry, therefore, was the practice of feeding, housing, and manipulating these spiritual entities in exchange for earthly blessings, protection, or power.

The biblical writers and Orthodox theology understand idolatry not as the foolish worship of inanimate wood and stone, but as the active worship of fallen spiritual beings—demons. When Israel engaged in idolatry, they were violating their exclusive covenant with Yahweh by participating in the dark communion of the rebellious divine council, sacrificing to principalities and powers who sought to enslave humanity.

In the modern context, the *Lord of Spirits* emphasizes that idolatry is not confined to bowing before statues. It is the fundamental reorientation of human worship away from the Creator and toward the creation. Whenever humans sacrifice their time, energy, or morality to abstract powers—such as the state, wealth, lust, or ideology—they are participating in the ancient sin of idolatry, rendering to demonic forces the devotion that belongs to God alone.
