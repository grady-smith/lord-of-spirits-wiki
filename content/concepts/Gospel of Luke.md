---
title: "Gospel of Luke"
tags: [concept]
---

The Gospel of Luke is the third of the four canonical Gospels, traditionally attributed to Luke the physician, a companion of the Apostle Paul. It is uniquely structured as a detailed, orderly historical account of the life, ministry, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ, intended to provide "certainty" regarding the teachings the early Christians received. Luke's Gospel is closely paired with the Book of Acts, forming a two-part narrative detailing the story of Jesus and the subsequent expansion of the early Church.

In Orthodox theology and biblical studies, the Gospel of Luke is noted for its profound emphasis on the compassion of Christ, particularly toward the marginalized: the poor, women, Samaritans, and sinners. It prominently features the role of the Holy Spirit, the importance of prayer, and the themes of repentance and joy. Furthermore, Luke’s narrative frequently highlights the cosmic dimensions of Christ's ministry, portraying His healings and exorcisms as direct confrontations with the kingdom of darkness.
