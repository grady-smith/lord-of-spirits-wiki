---
title: "Talmud"
tags: [concept]
---

The Talmud is the central text of Rabbinic Judaism, encompassing rabbinic discussions, debates, and interpretations of Jewish law, ethics, customs, and history. It consists of two parts: the Mishnah (the written compendium of the Oral Torah, compiled around 200 AD) and the Gemara (the rabbinic commentary on the Mishnah, compiled around 500 AD). It represents the massive shift in Judaism following the destruction of the Second Temple in 70 AD, moving the religion from a temple-centric, sacrificial system to one focused on Torah study, legal interpretation, and localized practice.

In the *Lord of Spirits* framework, the Talmud is viewed as a post-Christian development that often explicitly or implicitly reacts against the traditions preserved by the early Christian Church. While the Talmud preserves ancient Jewish traditions, it also reflects a conscious distancing from the Second Temple apocalyptic and angelological traditions (such as the "Two Powers in Heaven" or the Enochic literature) that the early Christians used to understand Jesus as the incarnate Yahweh. Consequently, Orthodox theology relies far more on Second Temple literature than on the Talmud to understand the cultural and theological matrix of the New Testament.
