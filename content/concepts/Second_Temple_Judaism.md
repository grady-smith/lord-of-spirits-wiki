---
title: "Second Temple Judaism"
tags: [concept]
---

Second Temple Judaism refers to the religion, culture, and theology of the Jewish people from the construction of the Second Temple (c. 516 BCE) to its destruction by the Romans in 70 CE. This era was characterized by profound historical upheaval, including Persian, Greek, and Roman domination, which led to significant theological developments. It was during this period that the Hebrew Bible began to take its final form, and various sects emerged, such as the Pharisees, Sadducees, Essenes, and Zealots, each with distinct interpretations of Torah and tradition.

The *Lord of Spirits* podcast frequently relies on the literature and worldview of Second Temple Judaism to illuminate the New Testament. This period saw the flourishing of apocalyptic literature (like 1 Enoch), a heightened focus on the unseen realm, angelology, and demonology, and intense messianic expectations. The concept of the Divine Council, the rebellion of the Watchers, and the dualistic struggle between the forces of light and darkness were central themes that directly shaped the context in which Jesus and the Apostles taught.

Understanding Second Temple Judaism is vital for Orthodox theology because early Christianity did not emerge in a vacuum; it was a movement born within this specific Jewish matrix. The theological vocabulary, ritual concepts (like sacrifice and priesthood), and eschatological hopes of the early Church are deeply rooted in the Second Temple worldview. Recognizing this continuity corrects modern misunderstandings and demonstrates how Christ is the fulfillment, rather than the abrogation, of the ancient Jewish faith.
