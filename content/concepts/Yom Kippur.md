Yom Kippur, or the Day of Atonement, was the most solemn and holy day in the ancient Israelite calendar. Once a year, the High Priest entered the Holy of Holies to offer sacrifices to purify the sanctuary, the priesthood, and the people from the accumulated pollution of sin. A central and highly unusual feature of the ritual involved two goats: one was sacrificed to Yahweh to purify the temple, while the other—the "scapegoat"—had the sins of the nation placed upon it and was driven into the wilderness to Azazel, a prominent fallen Watcher or demon.

The *Lord of Spirits* podcast uses Yom Kippur to dramatically illustrate the mechanics of spiritual warfare and atonement. Atonement, in this context, is not about appeasing an angry God, but about cleansing the sacred space so God's life-giving presence can remain. Sending the goat to Azazel was a mockery and a rejection of the demonic realm, returning the pollution of sin back to its chaotic source in the wilderness. Christ ultimately fulfills Yom Kippur by perfectly purifying the cosmos with His blood and decisively conquering the wilderness of death.
