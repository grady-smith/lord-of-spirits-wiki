---
title: "Father"
tags: [concept]
---

In Orthodox Trinitarian theology, the Father is the first Person of the Holy Trinity, the unoriginate source (fount or principle) of the Godhead. The Son is eternally begotten of the Father, and the Holy Spirit eternally proceeds from the Father. This relational dynamic ensures both the absolute unity of God and the distinct personhood within the Trinity. The Father is not "more God" than the Son or the Spirit, but is the eternal cause of their existence within the eternal communion of the divine life.

In ancient Near Eastern contexts, the title of "Father" for a deity was common, denoting authority, creation, and covenantal relationship. However, the Christian revelation of God as Father is uniquely intimate, rooted in Jesus Christ's own relationship with Him ("Abba"). Through adoption in Christ, believers are brought into this divine family, allowing them to address the Creator of the cosmos as their Father, signifying a profound reconciliation and participation in the divine nature.
