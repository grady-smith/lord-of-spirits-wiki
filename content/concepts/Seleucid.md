---
title: "Seleucid"
tags: [concept]
---

The Seleucid Empire was a major Hellenistic state that emerged following the death of Alexander the Great in 323 BCE. Founded by Seleucus I Nicator, one of Alexander's generals (Diadochi), the empire at its height stretched from Anatolia and Syria in the west to Mesopotamia and the borders of India in the east. The Seleucids were key players in the complex geopolitical struggles of the ancient Near East, frequently warring with the rival Ptolemaic Empire of Egypt over control of the Levant, including Judea.

In the context of the *Lord of Spirits* and biblical history, the Seleucid Empire is most significant for its severe persecution of the Jewish people under King Antiochus IV Epiphanes in the 2nd century BCE. Antiochus sought to forcefully Hellenize Judea, outlawing Jewish religious practices, desecrating the Second Temple by sacrificing a pig on the altar, and erecting a statue of Zeus. This unprecedented religious oppression sparked the Maccabean Revolt, leading to the establishment of the independent Hasmonean dynasty and the festival of Hanukkah.

From an Orthodox theological perspective, the Seleucid persecution represents the perennial conflict between the kingdom of God and the oppressive, idolatrous empires of the world. The events of this period, recorded in the Books of the Maccabees and prophesied in the Book of Daniel, deeply shaped the apocalyptic and messianic expectations of Second Temple Judaism. The Jewish martyrs who died resisting Seleucid Hellenization are venerated by the Orthodox Church as early witnesses to the faith, prefiguring the Christian martyrs of the Roman era.
