---
title: "Council of Nicaea"
tags: [concept]
---
The First Council of Nicaea, convened in 325 AD by Emperor Constantine, was the first ecumenical council of the Christian Church. It was called primarily to address the Arian controversy, which posited that Jesus Christ was a created being and not co-eternal with God the Father. The Council firmly rejected this, articulating the orthodox theological position that the Son is *homoousios* (of the exact same essence or substance) with the Father, thus preserving the uncompromised divinity of Christ.

In Orthodox theology, Nicaea is not viewed as an invention of new doctrine, but as the formal, conciliar defense of the apostolic deposit of faith—the reality that the God of Israel had become incarnate. In the context of ancient cosmology discussed in *The Lord of Spirits*, this Council reinforced the absolute distinction between the uncreated Creator and all created beings (including the highest angels or divine council members). To suggest Christ was created, as Arius did, would reduce the Word of God to the status of a mere heavenly being or lesser god, fatally undermining the Christian understanding of the Trinity and the mechanics of human salvation and theosis.
