---
title: "Jonah"
tags: [concept]
---
Jonah was an 8th-century BC Hebrew prophet whose story is unique among the prophetic books; rather than consisting of oracles, it is a narrative about the prophet's own profound rebellion against God. Commanded to preach repentance to Nineveh—the brutal capital of the hostile Assyrian Empire—Jonah instead flees in the opposite direction, leading to his famous engulfment by a "great fish."

In the ancient Near Eastern context, the sea (and the sea monster) represented the chaotic, destructive forces of the abyss, closely associated with the realm of the dead (Sheol). Jonah's descent into the belly of the fish is explicitly described in his prayer as a descent into the underworld. His subsequent regurgitation onto dry land is a miraculous deliverance from the powers of death.

For Orthodox theology and the New Testament, Jonah is the ultimate "sign" of Christ’s resurrection. Jesus Himself declared that the only sign given to a wicked generation would be the "Sign of Jonah"—just as Jonah was three days and three nights in the belly of the great fish, so the Son of Man would be three days and nights in the heart of the earth. Furthermore, God's mercy on the repentant, pagan Ninevites dramatically highlights the universal scope of God's salvation, subverting ethnic exclusivity.
