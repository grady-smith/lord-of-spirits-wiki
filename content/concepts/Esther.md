The Book of Esther is a narrative set in the Persian Empire during the reign of Ahasuerus (Xerxes I), recounting how a young Jewish woman named Esther becomes queen and, along with her cousin Mordecai, thwarts a genocidal plot against the Jewish people orchestrated by the king's official, Haman. Uniquely, it is the only book in the Hebrew Bible that never explicitly mentions the name of God, focusing instead on themes of providence, hidden divine action, and the reversal of fortunes.

In the ancient Near Eastern context discussed in *The Lord of Spirits*, Esther illustrates the survival of God’s covenant people while living in exile under the jurisdiction of foreign nations—nations ruled, according to Deuteronomy 32, by fallen spiritual powers. Haman is identified in the text as an Agagite, directly linking him to the Amalekites, ancient enemies of Israel associated with chaos and the remnants of the giant clans (Nephilim). Thus, the defeat of Haman is not just political intrigue, but a continuation of the cosmic war between Yahweh's purposes and the chaotic, demonic forces seeking to annihilate the lineage through which the Messiah would eventually come.
