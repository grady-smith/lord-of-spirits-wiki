---
title: "Neoplatonism"
tags: [concept]
---

Neoplatonism is a philosophical system originated in the 3rd century AD by thinkers like Plotinus, based heavily on the teachings of Plato. It posits a single, transcendent, and unknowable source called "The One," from which all existence emanates in descending stages of reality. In this system, the material world is at the lowest end of the emanations, farthest from perfection, and the goal of the human soul is to ascend away from the physical realm back to union with The One.

In the *Lord of Spirits* podcast and Orthodox theology, Neoplatonism is acknowledged for providing the early Church with useful philosophical vocabulary (such as *ousia* and *hypostasis*), but its fundamental cosmological assumptions are deeply rejected. Neoplatonism views the material world as inherently deficient or a prison for the soul, whereas Orthodox theology insists that the physical creation is fundamentally good, created purposefully by God, and intended for transfiguration, not escape.

Furthermore, the God of Orthodoxy is not an impersonal, abstract "One" that emanates reality out of necessity, but a Trinity of Persons who created the world freely out of love. The Incarnation of Christ—God taking on physical flesh—stands as the ultimate refutation of Neoplatonic disdain for matter, affirming that salvation involves the redemption of the entire physical cosmos.
