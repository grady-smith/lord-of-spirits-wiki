---
title: "Patriarch"
tags: [concept]
---

A Patriarch literally means the "father or head of a ruling family or tribe." In the biblical narrative, it refers specifically to the foundational figures of the Israelite nation, primarily Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

In the *Lord of Spirits* and ancient Near Eastern context, the patriarch is the mediator of the covenant between his extended household and God. Before the establishment of the Levitical priesthood, the patriarch functioned as the priest of his family, building altars and offering sacrifices (as seen frequently in the lives of Abraham and Job) to maintain the sacred relationship with Yahweh and protect his household from hostile spiritual forces.

In the organizational structure of the Orthodox Church, the title "Patriarch" is given to the highest-ranking bishops who serve as the primates of certain ancient or major autocephalous churches (e.g., the Patriarch of Constantinople, the Patriarch of Antioch). Like their Old Testament namesakes, they serve as spiritual fathers, tasked with guarding the correct worship and unity of the Church, and serving as the chief liturgical representatives of Christ for their respective jurisdictions.
