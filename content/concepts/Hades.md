---
title: "Hades"
tags: [concept]
---

Hades is the Greek term used in the New Testament and the Septuagint to translate the Hebrew word "Sheol," referring to the realm of the dead or the underworld. In the ancient Near Eastern worldview, it was a shadowy, dismal place where all departed souls went, regardless of their moral standing in life. It was considered a place of captivity, ruled over by the power of death and hostile spiritual forces.

In Orthodox Christian theology, Hades is profoundly significant because it is the realm that Jesus Christ invaded and conquered following His crucifixion. The "Harrowing of Hell" (or the Descent into Hades) is the central theme of Orthodox Paschal (Easter) theology. Christ descended into Hades not as a captive, but as a conqueror, shattering its gates, binding the strong man (Death/Satan), and liberating the righteous dead. Thus, for the Christian, Hades has been emptied of its ultimate power.
