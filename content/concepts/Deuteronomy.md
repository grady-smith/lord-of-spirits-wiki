The Book of Deuteronomy is the fifth book of the Torah (Pentateuch), consisting of Moses' final addresses to the Israelites before they enter the Promised Land. It recounts the Law given at Sinai, establishes the parameters of Israel's covenant with Yahweh, and provides profound theological commentary on the nature of Israel’s election and the severe consequences of idolatry.

Deuteronomy is absolutely foundational to the worldview presented in *The Lord of Spirits*, specifically due to the "Deuteronomy 32 worldview" (based on Deut 32:8-9 in the Septuagint and Dead Sea Scrolls). This passage describes how God divided the nations according to the number of the "sons of God" (the angelic Divine Council) at the Tower of Babel, effectively disinheriting the nations and assigning them to lesser spiritual beings, while choosing Israel as His own direct portion. Because these lesser gods fell and demanded worship (becoming the pagan gods of the nations), Deuteronomy establishes the cosmic rationale for Israel’s strict monolatry and the ultimate necessity of Christ reclaiming the nations.
