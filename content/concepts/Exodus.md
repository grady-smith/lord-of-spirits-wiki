---
title: "Exodus"
tags: [concept]
---

The Exodus is the foundational narrative of the Old Testament, describing the liberation of the Israelites from slavery in Egypt under the leadership of Moses. In the ancient Near Eastern context, the Exodus is not merely a political liberation but a profound theological event—a divine victory over the gods of Egypt. The plagues are understood as judgments against the Egyptian pantheon, culminating in the Passover and the crossing of the Red Sea, which echoes the ancient Chaoskampf motif where God defeats the chaotic waters to create a new people.

In Orthodox Christian thought, the Exodus is a central type or prefiguration of salvation in Christ. The crossing of the Red Sea prefigures Christian baptism, the manna in the wilderness prefigures the Eucharist, and the journey to the Promised Land symbolizes the Christian journey toward the Kingdom of God. Christ is seen as the new Moses who leads humanity out of bondage to sin, death, and the demonic powers (the spiritual "Egypt") into the freedom of eternal life.
