title: "Angels"
tags: [concept]
---
In the ancient Near Eastern context and Orthodox theology, angels are not the winged infants or passive beings of popular modern imagination. They are powerful, bodiless spiritual beings who constitute the Divine Council—the heavenly host that surrounds the throne of the Most High God. The word "angel" (angelos in Greek, malakh in Hebrew) is essentially a job description meaning "messenger" or "envoy," describing their function rather than their ontological nature.

The *Lord of Spirits* podcast emphasizes that angels are active participants in the administration of the cosmos. They govern the natural elements, deliver divine messages, and act as warriors in the unseen realm. They were created to participate in God's governance of the universe, operating as the heavenly counterpart to humanity's earthly vocation. The celestial hierarchy includes various ranks such as Seraphim, Cherubim, Thrones, Dominions, Principalities, and Powers, each with specific cosmic roles.

Crucially, angels possess free will. While the faithful angels—led by figures like the Archangel Michael—serve God continually, a significant portion of the heavenly host rebelled. These fallen angels include the Watchers who corrupted humanity before the flood and the territorial spirits who accepted the worship of the gentile nations as pagan gods. The biblical narrative is largely the story of the cosmic war between God with His faithful angels against these rebellious powers.
