---
title: "Bishop"
tags: [concept]
---

In Orthodox theology, a Bishop (from the Greek *episkopos*, meaning "overseer") is the highest order of the ordained priesthood and the successor to the Apostles. The bishop is the visible center of unity for the local church and the primary liturgical celebrant, holding the fullness of the priesthood. Historically and theologically, the bishop acts as the living icon of Christ the High Priest, gathering the Eucharistic assembly and ensuring the transmission of the apostolic faith, holy tradition, and sacramental grace.

Within the cosmological framework of the *Lord of Spirits*, the role of the bishop transcends mere administrative leadership. Bishops function as the earthly counterparts to the angelic beings in the Divine Council. Just as the Archangels and faithful spiritual powers govern and protect the cosmos in obedience to God, bishops are entrusted with the spiritual governance and protection of the Church on earth. They are engaged in active spiritual warfare, tasked with guarding their flock against the influence of the demonic realm and leading the congregation in the ongoing, sacramental life of the Kingdom of God.
