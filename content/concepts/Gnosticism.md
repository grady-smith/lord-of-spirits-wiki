---
title: "Gnosticism"
tags: [concept]
---

Gnosticism refers to a diverse set of ancient religious movements that emerged in the early centuries of the Christian era. Though highly varied, Gnostic systems generally posited a dualistic worldview: the material world is inherently flawed or evil, having been created by a lesser, ignorant, or malevolent deity (the Demiurge), while the spiritual realm is pure. Salvation in Gnosticism is achieved through *gnosis*—secret, esoteric knowledge—which awakens the divine spark trapped within human bodies, allowing it to return to the ultimate, transcendent spiritual realm.

In Orthodox theology, Gnosticism is regarded as a profound heresy that fundamentally undermines the Christian message. The early Church Fathers, such as Irenaeus, vehemently fought against Gnostic teachings, defending the inherent goodness of physical creation, the reality of the Incarnation (that God truly took on human flesh), and the physical resurrection of the dead. In the *Lord of Spirits* context, Gnostic ideas are seen as recurrent distortions that seek to detach spirituality from the physical, sacramental reality of the cosmos.
