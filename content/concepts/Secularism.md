---
title: "Secularism"
tags: [concept]
---

Secularism, in modern philosophical and sociological terms, is the principle of separating government, institutions, and public life from religious authority or influence. It asserts that human affairs should be based on rational, observable evidence and natural laws rather than religious dogma or revelation. Historically, secularism emerged as a dominant force during the Enlightenment, driven by figures like Rousseau and Voltaire, fundamentally reshaping Western civilization's understanding of the world.

The *Lord of Spirits* podcast frequently critiques secularism as a profound departure from the ancient and biblical worldview. In the ancient Near East and traditional Orthodox theology, the world is fundamentally enchanted; there is no strict division between the "natural" and the "supernatural." Spiritual realities, angels, demons, and the active presence of God are understood as integral, active components of everyday life and history. Secularism, by contrast, operates on a "closed universe" model, denying or marginalizing the unseen realm.

From an Orthodox perspective, secularism is not merely a neutral political stance, but a competing, reductionist theology. It attempts to strip the cosmos of its sacramental character, reducing creation to mere dead matter to be manipulated for human ends. This disenchantment leads to a profound spiritual alienation and the illusion of absolute human autonomy. The Orthodox Church maintains that true reality is inherently spiritual and communal, calling believers to resist the secular mindset and participate in the ongoing transfiguration of the world.
