---
title: "Annihilationism"
tags: [concept]
---
Annihilationism is the theological belief that the ultimate fate of the wicked is complete destruction or cessation of existence, rather than eternal conscious torment. In the discussions of the *Lord of Spirits* podcast, this concept is frequently addressed and firmly rejected based on the traditional Orthodox understanding of human nature and the character of God.

Orthodox theology maintains the inherent immortality of the human soul. Because humanity is made in the image of God, and God's life is eternal, the soul does not cease to exist upon death or at the Last Judgment. The ancient Christian view, articulated by fathers like St. Isaac the Syrian, is that God is a "consuming fire" of infinite love. In the eschaton, God will be "all in all," and everyone will experience His unmediated presence.

Therefore, heaven and hell are not so much different spatial locations as they are different experiences of the exact same reality: the overwhelming presence of God's love. For those who have oriented their lives toward God through repentance and love (theosis), His presence is experienced as the joyous light of Paradise. For those who have hated God, aligned themselves with demonic rebellion, and clung to their sins, that exact same presence of divine love is experienced as the burning torment of Gehenna. Annihilationism is rejected because God does not un-create what He has made; existence itself is inherently good, even if the creature experiences it as suffering due to their own posture.
