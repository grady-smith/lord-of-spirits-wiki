---
title: "Socrates"
tags: [concept]
---

Socrates (c. 470–399 BCE) was a foundational figure in classical Greek philosophy, famously known for his dialectical method of inquiry and his insistence that "the unexamined life is not worth living." He wrote nothing himself; his life and ideas are known primarily through the dialogues of his student Plato and the writings of Xenophon. Socrates shifted the focus of Greek philosophy from cosmological speculation to ethics, virtue, and the nature of the human soul. He was ultimately condemned to death by the Athenian state for "corrupting the youth" and introducing new gods.

In the context of the *Lord of Spirits* and early Christian apologetics, Socrates is a complex figure. The early Church Fathers, particularly those writing in a Hellenistic context like Justin Martyr, often viewed Socrates favorably. They argued that insofar as Socrates sought truth and lived virtuously, he was participating in the *Logos spermatikos* (the "seed of the Word") that was ultimately fully incarnate in Jesus Christ. Justin even boldly claimed that Socrates and those like him were "Christians before Christ" because they lived according to reason (Logos).

However, Orthodox theology also maintains a clear distinction between the philosophical contemplation of the divine (which can arrive at certain truths via natural revelation) and the direct, personal communion with the Holy Trinity offered through revelation and the Church. While Greek philosophy provided valuable vocabulary and conceptual frameworks for articulating Christian dogma (especially in the Ecumenical Councils), the ultimate truth is not an abstract concept or a philosophical conclusion, but a Person: Jesus Christ, who defeated death—something philosophy alone could never accomplish.
