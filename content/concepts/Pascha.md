---
title: "Pascha"
tags: [concept]
---

Pascha is the Orthodox Christian term for Easter, derived directly from the Aramaic and Hebrew words for Passover (*Pesach*). It is the "Feast of Feasts," celebrating the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead.

In the *Lord of Spirits* theology, Pascha is the ultimate and decisive event of cosmic warfare. It is the fulfillment of what the Old Testament Passover foreshadowed. While the original Passover defeated the gods of Egypt and liberated the Israelites from physical slavery, Christ's Pascha is the Harrowing of Hades—the invasion of the underworld, the binding of Satan, the destruction of Death by death, and the liberation of all humanity from demonic captivity.

The Orthodox celebration of Pascha is profoundly triumphant. It is the moment when the gates of Hades are shattered, and Christ, the divine King, leads the captives into Paradise. The entire liturgical life of the Church flows from and points toward this reality, affirming that the ultimate hostile power—death itself—has been completely subverted and transformed into the pathway to eternal life.
