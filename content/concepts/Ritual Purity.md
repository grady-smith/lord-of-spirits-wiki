---
title: "Ritual Purity"
tags: [concept]
---

Ritual purity in the ancient Near East and biblical Israel was a system of categorizing the physical state of a person, object, or space to determine whether it was fit to encounter the holy presence of God. Being "unclean" (such as from touching a corpse, bodily discharges, or certain diseases) was typically not a moral sin, but rather a state of association with mortality, death, and chaos that required washing and time to resolve before approaching the sanctuary.

Within the theology of the *Lord of Spirits*, the ritual purity laws of the Torah are understood as a necessary insulation system. Because God is the source of all life and is completely antithetical to death, bringing the pollution of death into His presence results in destruction. Christ's earthly ministry inverted this dynamic: instead of becoming unclean by touching a leper, a bleeding woman, or a corpse, Christ's inherent, uncreated life and purity flowed outward, cleansing and healing the defilement. The Orthodox sacraments continue this purifying work in the world today.
