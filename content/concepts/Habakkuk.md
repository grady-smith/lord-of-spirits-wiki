---
title: "Habakkuk"
tags: [concept]
---

Habakkuk is one of the minor prophets of the Old Testament, writing during the final decades of the southern kingdom of Judah, shortly before the Babylonian conquest. Unlike most prophetic books that deliver God's message to the people, the Book of Habakkuk is unique in that it consists primarily of a dialogue—or rather, a series of complaints—from the prophet to God. Habakkuk questions why God allows injustice to flourish in Judah and then questions why God would use a nation even more wicked (the Chaldeans/Babylonians) to judge them.

In the context of Orthodox theology, Habakkuk's profound theological struggle leads to the pivotal revelation that "the righteous shall live by his faith[fulness]" (Habakkuk 2:4). This concept becomes central to the Apostle Paul's understanding of justification in the New Testament. The book also contains a majestic theophany in its final chapter, describing God as a divine warrior marching from the south to crush the forces of chaos, imagery deeply resonant with the cosmic warfare themes discussed in the *Lord of Spirits*.
