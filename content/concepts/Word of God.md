The "Word of God" (Greek: *Logos*, Hebrew: *Davar Yahweh*) is a deeply multi-faceted concept in Biblical theology. While modern Christianity often reduces this term strictly to the written text of the Bible, in the ancient Near East and Second Temple Judaism, the "Word of the Lord" was an actual, visible, acting hypostasis of God. The Word appeared to the prophets, delivered messages, and executed judgments. St. John's Gospel masterfully identifies this pre-existent, divine Logos directly with Jesus Christ.

In the *Lord of Spirits* podcast, the "Word of God" is constantly emphasized as the second person of the Trinity acting in the Old Testament. When the text says "the Word of the Lord came to Abraham in a vision," it indicates a visible Christophany, not merely a disembodied auditory experience. The Scriptures, therefore, are the "word of God" only derivatively; they are the inspired, written testimony about the true, living Word of God, Jesus Christ, who actively sustains the cosmos and leads the Divine Council.
