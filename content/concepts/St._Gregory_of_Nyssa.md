Saint Gregory of Nyssa (c. 335–395) was a bishop and theologian, the younger brother of St. Basil the Great, and one of the three Cappadocian Fathers. He is widely considered one of the most profound and original philosophical minds of the early Church. While his brother Basil provided the structural framework for Trinitarian theology, Gregory provided its deep mystical and philosophical justification, playing a pivotal role at the Second Ecumenical Council.

In the *Lord of Spirits* podcast and Orthodox theology, St. Gregory of Nyssa is paramount for his articulation of mystical theology and *theosis*. He is famous for the concept of *epektasis*, the perpetual, infinite progress of the soul into the divine life. Because God is infinite, the soul's journey toward God does not end in a static state of rest, but is a dynamic, eternal growth in love and participation in God's uncreated energies, constantly moving "from glory to glory."

Gregory is also known for his profound allegorical reading of Scripture, particularly the *Life of Moses*, where he uses the ascent of Mount Sinai as a map for the soul's journey from the darkness of sin into the "luminous darkness" of encountering the incomprehensible God. Additionally, he was a rare early Christian voice to explicitly condemn the institution of slavery, arguing that no human being, made in the image of God, could be owned by another.
