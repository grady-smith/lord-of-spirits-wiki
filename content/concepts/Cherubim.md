---
title: "Cherubim"
tags: [concept]
---

Cherubim are highly exalted angelic beings within the biblical hierarchy, most frequently associated with the direct presence, throne, and glory of God. In Ancient Near Eastern iconography, cherubim-like figures (often depicted as composite creatures, such as winged sphinxes or bulls with human heads) served as throne guardians for royalty or deities. In the Bible, they are first mentioned guarding the entrance to the Garden of Eden with a flaming sword, and their images were intricately woven into the veil of the Tabernacle and sculpted atop the Ark of the Covenant, forming the "mercy seat" where God's presence dwelt.

In the cosmology of the *Lord of Spirits* and Orthodox theology, the Cherubim are not the chubby infant angels (putti) of Renaissance art, but terrifying, powerful spiritual entities that carry the divine throne. They represent the boundary between the uncreated God and the created cosmos. The prophet Ezekiel describes them as complex, multi-faced beings surrounded by wheels within wheels covered in eyes, emphasizing their role as the living, dynamic chariot of the Most High. They continuously praise God and are intimately involved in mediating His unapproachable light to the rest of creation.
