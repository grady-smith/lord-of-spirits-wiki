---
title: "Altar"
tags: [concept]
---
In the ancient Near East and Orthodox theology, an altar is vastly more than a simple table or functional piece of furniture; it is the focal point where heaven and earth intersect. It is the designated place of communion between God and humanity. When an altar is consecrated, it establishes a sacred space, serving as the earthly counterpart to the heavenly throne and the heavenly altar where the angels offer unceasing worship.

The primary function of an altar is sacrifice, which in the ancient worldview was not fundamentally about penal appeasement, but about sharing a meal and establishing fellowship with the divine. In the pagan context, altars were where humans communed with the fallen gods of the nations (demons). Therefore, when Patriarchs like Abraham built altars across Canaan, they were executing spiritual warfare, claiming territory for the Most High God and driving back the demonic presence.

In the Orthodox Christian tradition, the altar (the Holy Table) is the center of the Church, representing the tomb from which Christ resurrected and the throne of God. It is where the ultimate sacrifice—the Eucharist—is offered. By partaking of the Eucharist at the altar, the faithful are drawn up into the heavenly liturgy, participating bodily and spiritually in the life of the Holy Trinity.
