---
title: "Son of God"
tags: [concept]
---

In the ancient Near East and the Hebrew Bible, the title "Son of God" (or "sons of God," *bene elohim*) carried multiple, deeply significant meanings. It was used to refer to angelic beings or members of the Divine Council who were created by God directly and shared in His governance of the cosmos. It was also used for the nation of Israel as God's firstborn among the nations, and specifically for the Davidic King, who functioned as God's earthly representative and adopted son. The title denoted authority, representation, and a direct relational origin from Yahweh.

The *Lord of Spirits* podcast frequently explores how this title was understood in Second Temple Judaism and how it is applied to Jesus Christ. When Jesus is declared the "Son of God," it is not merely a metaphor for a righteous human being. Drawing on the complex matrix of Jewish expectations, it signifies that He is the ultimate Davidic King, the true Israel, but far more profoundly, that He shares the exact nature and essence of God the Father. He is the *monogenes*, the unique, uncreated, only-begotten Son.

In Orthodox theology, finalized at the Council of Nicaea, the title "Son of God" affirms that Jesus Christ is "light of light, true God of true God, begotten, not made, of one essence with the Father." The Incarnation means that the eternal Son of God became a son of man, so that human beings might, by grace and adoption, become sons of God. This is the core of the doctrine of *theosis*—participation in the divine sonship of Christ.
