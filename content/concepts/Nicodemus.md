---
title: "Nicodemus"
tags: [concept]
---

Nicodemus was a prominent Pharisee and a member of the Sanhedrin who, according to the Gospel of John, visited Jesus by night. He appears three times in the Gospel: inquiring about the Kingdom of God and being born again (or "born from above"), defending Jesus' right to a fair hearing before the Jewish authorities, and finally, assisting Joseph of Arimathea in providing the massive amount of spices for the burial of Christ.

In the theological context of the *Lord of Spirits*, Nicodemus represents the earnest, knowledgeable segment of Second Temple Judaism encountering the profound reality of the Incarnation. During his nighttime dialogue, Jesus challenges Nicodemus' understanding of salvation, expanding it beyond ethnic boundaries to the cosmic necessity of being born of water and the Spirit. This points to baptism as the means of exiting the domain of the fallen powers and entering the Kingdom of God.

Nicodemus' final act of providing an extravagant burial for Jesus—fit for a king—demonstrates his eventual recognition of Christ's messianic identity. He transitions from a ruler of the old, ethnic Israel in the darkness of night, to a participant in the new Israel, boldly honoring the slain King of Glory.
