---
title: "Daniel"
tags: [concept]
---
Daniel is a major prophet in the Hebrew Bible, known for his exemplary faithfulness while living in exile in Babylon and Persia. Serving in the royal courts of foreign empires, Daniel navigated the perilous realities of ancient Near Eastern statecraft, which were inextricably linked to the worship of pagan gods. His narrative involves interpreting dreams, surviving the lions' den, and receiving apocalyptic visions concerning the future of empires and God's ultimate kingdom.

In the *Lord of Spirits* and Orthodox context, the Book of Daniel is critical for its vivid depictions of the Unseen Realm. It introduces the concept of territorial spiritual beings (such as the "Prince of Persia" and the "Prince of Greece"), illustrating the Deuteronomy 32 worldview where fallen angels rule over the nations. Furthermore, Daniel 7 contains the foundational vision of the "Son of Man" coming before the "Ancient of Days," a text profoundly important for Orthodox Christology as it portrays two distinct hypostases (persons) sharing the divine glory—a prophetic anticipation of the Holy Trinity.
