---
title: "Augustus"
tags: [concept]
---

Caesar Augustus (born Octavian) was the first Roman Emperor, whose reign marked the transition from the Roman Republic to the Roman Empire and the beginning of the Pax Romana. In the biblical narrative, Augustus is the emperor who decreed the census that brought Joseph and Mary to Bethlehem, fulfilling the prophecy of the Messiah's birthplace (Luke 2:1). More significantly, Augustus fundamentally transformed the Roman imperial cult, promoting himself as the "son of a god" (divi filius) after the deification of his adoptive father, Julius Caesar.

In the context of early Christianity and the *Lord of Spirits*, the titles and claims of Augustus serve as a stark, intentional contrast to the Gospel of Jesus Christ. When early Christians proclaimed Jesus as the "Son of God," "Lord," and "Savior" who brings true peace, they were directly subverting the imperial propaganda of Augustus and his successors. The Roman imperial cult was viewed by early Christians not merely as a political allegiance, but as the worship of fallen spiritual powers (demons) operating behind the facade of the state. Christ's kingdom, therefore, stands in cosmic opposition to the empire of Augustus, offering true salvation and defeating the demonic forces animating human tyranny.
