---
title: "Tree of Life"
tags: [concept]
---

The Tree of Life is planted in the center of the Garden of Eden, representing immortality, divine wisdom, and direct participation in the life of God. Unlike the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil, which brought death when partaken of prematurely, the Tree of Life was the intended source of sustenance for humanity, designed to nourish them as they grew in holiness and communion with their Creator. Following the Fall, Adam and Eve were exiled from Eden specifically to prevent them from eating from the Tree of Life in a state of sin, which would have immortalized their fallen condition.

In the Orthodox theology presented by the *Lord of Spirits*, the Tree of Life is not merely an ancient historical artifact but a profound typological reality fulfilled in the Cross of Christ. The Cross is frequently hailed in Orthodox hymnography as the "new Tree of Life," upon which the ultimate fruit—the body of Christ—was hung. Through the sacrament of the Eucharist, believers are once again granted access to the fruit of this tree, partaking of the eternal life that was lost in Eden, reversing the curse of death.
