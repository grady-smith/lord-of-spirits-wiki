The Books of Chronicles (1 and 2 Chronicles) are historical texts in the Hebrew Bible that retell the narrative of the Kingdom of Judah, from the genealogy of Adam down to the decree of Cyrus the Great ending the Babylonian Exile. Unlike the Books of Samuel and Kings, which often focus on the failures and idolatry of the monarchs (both northern and southern), Chronicles was written post-exile and zeroes in almost exclusively on the Davidic line, the city of Jerusalem, and the operations of the Temple.

In the *Lord of Spirits* context, Chronicles is deeply significant for its liturgical and cosmic focus. The text goes to great lengths to describe the organization of the Levites, the choirs, and the Temple worship, presenting the Temple not just as a building, but as the microcosm of the universe and the earthly counterpart to the heavenly Divine Council. The Chronicler emphasizes that Israel's true vitality is not found in military might, but in correct, faithful participation in the worship of the Most High God. When the king and the people maintain this liturgical fidelity, they experience victory over their enemies and the chaotic forces surrounding them.
