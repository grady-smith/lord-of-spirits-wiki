St. Peter, the chief among the Apostles, holds a foundational place in Orthodox tradition as a key witness to the life, transfiguration, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. As a fisherman from Galilee, he was among the first called by Christ and is often portrayed in the Gospels as the passionate, albeit sometimes flawed, spokesman for the disciples. His confession of Jesus as "the Christ, the Son of the living God" near Mount Hermon—a site heavily associated with rebellious Watchers in Second Temple Judaism—was a profound declaration of Christ's cosmic authority.

The epistles of St. Peter are frequently highlighted in the *Lord of Spirits* podcast for their explicit connections to the Enochic worldview. In his writings, St. Peter refers to the spirits in prison and the angels who sinned during the days of Noah, making direct use of traditions found in the Book of Enoch. This demonstrates that the early Apostolic Church did not view the spiritual conflict of Genesis 6 as a peripheral myth, but as an essential component of the gospel narrative, emphasizing Christ’s descent into Hades and His victory over rebellious cosmic forces.
