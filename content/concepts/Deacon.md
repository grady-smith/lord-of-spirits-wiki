---
title: "Deacon"
tags: [concept]
---
A deacon is an ordained minister of an order ranking below that of a priest within the hierarchy of the Apostolic churches. The term comes from the Greek word *diakonos*, meaning "servant" or "minister." In the early Church, the diaconate was established by the Apostles (as seen in Acts 6) specifically to handle the distribution of alms and the practical care of the community, allowing the Apostles to focus on prayer and the ministry of the Word.

In the liturgical and theological framework of the Orthodox Church, the deacon plays a vital role as the conductor of the Divine Liturgy. The deacon leads the congregation in prayer (the litanies), reads the Gospel, and acts as the bridge between the sanctuary (the altar) and the nave (the people). Drawing on the imagery of the heavenly Divine Council frequently discussed in *The Lord of Spirits*, the deacon conceptually represents the angels, functioning as a ministering spirit who facilitates the worship of God and serves the immediate needs of the faithful.
