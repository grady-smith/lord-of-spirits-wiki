title: "Messiah"
tags: [concept]
---

The term "Messiah" (Hebrew: *Mashiach*, Greek: *Christos*) literally translates to "Anointed One." In the ancient Near East and early Israel, anointing with oil was the physical ritual used to set apart kings, priests, and occasionally prophets, granting them the Holy Spirit for their appointed office. Rather than a modern political leader, the ancient concept of the Messiah represented the ultimate convergence of the King and the High Priest, the one who would bridge heaven and earth.

In Orthodox theology and the *Lord of Spirits* podcast, the Messiah is understood primarily as the pre-incarnate Son of God, the Angel of the Lord, who takes on human flesh. He is not a newly created hero but the eternal Second Person of the Trinity who comes to reclaim the nations that were divided under the rebellious sons of God (Deuteronomy 32:8-9). His messianic mission is intrinsically tied to spiritual warfare—defeating the powers of death, sin, and the demonic entities that hold humanity captive.

The Messiah’s anointing is absolute and cosmic. Through His incarnation, crucifixion, descent into Hades, and resurrection, Christ the Messiah fulfills the roles of the Davidic king and the Melchizedekian priest, ascending to the throne of the Divine Council and making His followers partakers in His messianic kingdom.
