---
title: "Saint"
tags: [concept]
---

In Orthodox theology and the biblical tradition, a "saint" (from the Greek *hagios*, meaning "holy one") is not merely a morally exceptional person, but someone who has been profoundly united with God. Holiness, in the ancient Near Eastern context, originally belonged exclusively to God; it denoted His complete otherness and purity. A person or object becomes holy—a saint—only by participation in God's holiness. Saints are therefore those in whom the Holy Spirit dwells fully, transforming them into living icons of Christ.

The *Lord of Spirits* podcast frequently discusses saints as participants in the Divine Council. In the eschatological vision of the New Testament, saints are those who overcome the world and are given authority to rule and judge alongside Christ, replacing the fallen spiritual powers (the Watchers or territorial angels) that previously governed the nations. This understanding shifts the concept of a saint from a mere ethical exemplar to a cosmic participant in God's governance of creation.

Orthodox Christians venerate saints, asking for their intercessions, because they are alive in Christ. The Church views the boundary between the living and the dead as porous, united in the one body of Christ. The saints, having attained *theosis* (deification), continue to pray for and assist the Church on earth, functioning as elder brothers and sisters in the faith who guide believers toward the same ultimate goal of union with God.
