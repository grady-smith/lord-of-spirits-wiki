Jean-Jacques Rousseau was an 18th-century Genevan philosopher whose ideas significantly influenced the Enlightenment and subsequent Western thought. In the context of the *Lord of Spirits* podcast, Rousseau is often referenced as a key figure in the development of modern secularism, individualism, and the concept of the "noble savage." His philosophies represent a stark departure from traditional Christian anthropology, which views humanity as fallen and in need of divine redemption and communion.

Orthodox theology critiques Rousseau's assumption that human beings are naturally good and that it is society or institutions that corrupt them. From an Orthodox perspective, this framework ignores the reality of ancestral sin and the ongoing spiritual warfare against cosmic powers of darkness. Rousseau's focus on the individual's inner, subjective feeling as the ultimate arbiter of truth stands in contrast to the Orthodox emphasis on truth as objectively incarnate in Jesus Christ and experienced communally within the Church.

Furthermore, Rousseau's ideas laid the groundwork for the modern, disenchanted view of the world. By redefining human freedom as autonomy from external authority, his philosophy helped sever the modern mind from the classical and biblical understanding of freedom, which is found in willing obedience to God and participation in the divine life (theosis).
