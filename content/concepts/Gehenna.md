---
title: "Gehenna"
tags: [concept]
---

Gehenna is a term originating from the Valley of Hinnom (Gai Ben-Hinnom), a ravine outside Jerusalem. In ancient times, this valley was infamously associated with idolatrous practices, including the child sacrifices to the god Molech by some of the kings of Judah. Because of these horrific acts, the prophets Jeremiah and Isaiah declared it a place of divine judgment, and it eventually became a burning refuse dump.

By the Second Temple period, Gehenna had evolved into a theological metaphor for the final place of punishment for the wicked and rebellious spiritual entities. In the New Testament, Jesus frequently uses the term Gehenna (often translated as "hell") to describe the eternal destruction awaiting the unrepentant. In Orthodox theology, Gehenna is not typically viewed as a physical torture chamber created by God, but rather as the experience of God's all-consuming, holy love by those who vehemently reject Him, a state of eternal self-exclusion from His life.
