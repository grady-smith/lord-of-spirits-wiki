---
title: "Codex Sinaiticus"
tags: [concept]
---

Codex Sinaiticus is one of the most important and ancient manuscript witnesses to the Greek Bible, dating to the mid-4th century. Discovered at St. Catherine's Monastery at the foot of Mount Sinai, it contains the oldest complete copy of the New Testament, as well as portions of the Greek Old Testament (the Septuagint) and early Christian texts like the *Epistle of Barnabas* and the *Shepherd of Hermas*. Its discovery was revolutionary for biblical textual criticism, providing scholars with a text centuries older than the medieval manuscripts previously relied upon.

For Orthodox theology and discussions on the *Lord of Spirits*, Codex Sinaiticus highlights the fluidity and breadth of the early Christian biblical canon. The inclusion of texts like the *Shepherd of Hermas* alongside canonical books demonstrates that the early Church possessed a vast library of spiritually significant literature that informed their worldview, even if not all of it ultimately made the strict, later canonical cut. Furthermore, it affirms the reliability of the textual transmission of the scriptures, anchoring the Church's cosmic and theological claims in verifiable antiquity.
