---
title: "Nag Hammadi library"
tags: [concept]
---

The Nag Hammadi library is a collection of early Christian and Gnostic texts discovered near the Egyptian town of Nag Hammadi in 1945. Written in Coptic, these texts date primarily to the 3rd and 4th centuries AD and include famous apocryphal works like the Gospel of Thomas, the Secret Book of John, and the Gospel of Truth.

In the *Lord of Spirits* podcast, the Nag Hammadi texts are often discussed as prime examples of Gnostic theology, which early Church Fathers like Irenaeus fiercely opposed. Gnosticism proposed a radical dualism where the physical, material world was created by an evil or ignorant lesser deity (the Demiurge), while the true, ultimate God was purely spiritual and entirely removed from physical creation. Salvation, in this view, was achieved by acquiring secret, esoteric knowledge (gnosis) to escape the physical body.

Orthodox theology, in direct opposition to the concepts found in the Nag Hammadi library, asserts that the material creation is fundamentally good because it was created by the Holy Trinity. The incarnation, bodily resurrection, and the sacramental use of physical matter in the Church completely reject the Gnostic disparagement of the physical world. The Nag Hammadi texts are valuable for understanding what the early Church explicitly rejected in establishing the boundaries of Orthodoxy.
