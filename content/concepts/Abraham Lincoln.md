title: "Abraham Lincoln"
tags: [concept]
---
While an American historical figure rather than a biblical one, Abraham Lincoln occasionally appears in the discussions of the *Lord of Spirits* podcast as a primary architect of the American "civil religion." In the context of political theology, the podcast critiques the modern tendency of the secular nation-state to assume a quasi-divine status, demanding the kind of ultimate loyalty, sacrifice, and ideological devotion that the ancient world reserved solely for gods.

Lincoln's rhetoric, particularly in the Gettysburg Address and his Second Inaugural, successfully synthesized biblical language with American political aims, framing the Civil War as a massive, bloody sacrifice necessary for the atonement and rebirth of the nation. From the perspective of ancient Christian theology, this represents a subtle form of idolatry, where the state positions itself as a salvific institution and its leaders are venerated as civic saints or martyrs.

Orthodox theology, rooted in the worldview of the early Church which lived under the hostile Roman Empire, maintains a sharp distinction between the Kingdom of God and the kingdoms of men. Mentions of figures like Lincoln serve to remind listeners that regardless of a nation's virtues, placing ultimate messianic hope in political systems or state power ultimately aligns one with the "princes of this world" rather than the Kingdom of Christ.
