---
title: "Underworld"
tags: [concept]
---

In the ancient Near East, the Underworld (known as Sheol in Hebrew and Hades in Greek) was universally understood as the grim, shadowy abode of the dead, lying beneath the earth. It was not originally synonymous with the modern concept of "Hell" (Gehenna) as a place of fiery torment, but rather a place of lethargy, darkness, and separation from the land of the living and the active presence of God. All who died, whether righteous or wicked, descended into the Underworld.

The *Lord of Spirits* emphasizes the Underworld as a realm ruled by death, heavily associated with chaos, the abyss, and the demonic powers that hold humanity captive. The pivotal climax of Orthodox theology is Christ's descent into the Underworld following His crucifixion. By entering Hades as the Lord of Life, Christ shattered its gates, bound the strong man (Death/the Devil), and led the captive souls to Paradise. In the Christian paradigm, the Underworld has been decisively conquered and emptied of its ultimate power over humanity.
