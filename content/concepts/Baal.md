---
title: "Baal"
tags: [concept]
---

Baal, meaning "lord" or "master," was the prominent storm and fertility god of the Canaanite pantheon in the Ancient Near East. He is a classic example of a storm deity—often depicted wielding a thunderbolt and riding the clouds—who defeats the sea/chaos monster (Yamm or Lotan/Leviathan) to establish order, and then builds a temple to rule over the earth. Throughout the Old Testament, Baal represents the primary spiritual rival to Yahweh, and the Israelites frequently succumbed to the syncretistic worship of him, leading to severe condemnation from prophets like Elijah and Hosea.

In the framework of biblical cosmology and Orthodox theology, Baal is not merely a mythological fiction, but a real, rebellious spiritual entity—a fallen member of the Divine Council who usurped worship meant for the Most High God. The biblical writers frequently co-opted Baal's imagery and applied it exclusively to Yahweh to assert God's true supremacy. For example, Psalm 68 describes Yahweh as the "rider of the clouds," explicitly stripping Baal of his title and asserting that it is the God of Israel alone who rules the cosmos, controls the storms, and defeats the powers of chaos.
