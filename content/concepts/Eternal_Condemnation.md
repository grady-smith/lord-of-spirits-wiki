---
title: "Eternal Condemnation"
tags: [concept]
---
Eternal condemnation refers to the final, irrevocable state of separation from God, often associated with Hell or the Lake of Fire in Christian eschatology. In the New Testament, it is the ultimate consequence for those who persistently align themselves with the kingdom of darkness, refusing the grace and life offered by Christ.

In Orthodox theology and the framework of *The Lord of Spirits*, eternal condemnation is not generally understood as God actively torturing sinners in a physical fire. Rather, it is the natural consequence of encountering the uncreated, burning love and presence of God in an unpurified, rebellious state. For the righteous who have been healed and united to Christ, God's presence is light and warmth; for those who have clung to sin, hatred, and the demonic powers, that exact same presence is experienced as a consuming, agonizing fire. Thus, condemnation is fundamentally self-inflicted—a refusal to participate in the life of the Creator, resulting in an eternal, spiritual death.
