Titus Flavius Josephus (c. 37–100 AD) was a first-century Romano-Jewish historian and military leader. Initially a commander of Jewish forces in Galilee during the First Jewish-Roman War, he surrendered to the Romans and eventually became a client and historian for the Flavian dynasty. His major works, *The Jewish War* and *Antiquities of the Jews*, are unparalleled historical sources for understanding the Greco-Roman world and the political, cultural, and religious landscape of 1st-century Judea.

In the context of the *Lord of Spirits* and biblical studies, Josephus is indispensable for illuminating Second Temple Judaism. He provides detailed descriptions of the major Jewish sects—the Pharisees, Sadducees, and Essenes—and their varying beliefs regarding fate, free will, the resurrection of the dead, and the spirit world. He also documents the rampant messianic expectations and the brutal socio-political environment created by the Herodian dynasty and Roman occupation.

While not a Christian or a theologian, Josephus's historical accounts profoundly corroborate and contextualize the New Testament narrative. His writings provide the historical backdrop for the destruction of the Second Temple in 70 AD—an event prophesied by Christ that radically altered the trajectory of both Rabbinic Judaism and early Christianity, forcing both to adapt to a world without a physical, localized sacrificial center.
