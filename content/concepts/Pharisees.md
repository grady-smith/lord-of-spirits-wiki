---
title: "Pharisees"
tags: [concept]
---

The Pharisees were a prominent Jewish sect that emerged during the Second Temple period, characterized by their strict adherence to both the written Torah and the oral traditions of the elders. Unlike the aristocratic Sadducees, the Pharisees enjoyed broad support among the common people and focused on the democratization of holiness, seeking to apply priestly purity laws to everyday life. They firmly believed in the resurrection of the dead, angels, spirits, and the coming of a Messiah.

In the context of the *Lord of Spirits* and ancient worldviews, the Pharisees represent a theological continuity with ancient Israelite religion regarding the unseen realm, a continuity that early Christianity maintained. While they frequently clashed with Jesus over the interpretation of the law and the nature of the Sabbath, their fundamental cosmology—recognizing a populated spiritual realm and the ultimate resurrection—aligned much more closely with Christ's teachings than the materialistic skepticism of the Sadducees. Post-destruction Rabbinic Judaism traces its lineage directly back to the Pharisaic movement.
