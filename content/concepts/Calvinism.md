---
title: "Calvinism"
tags: [concept]
---

Calvinism is a major branch of Protestant theology originating in the 16th-century Reformation, primarily associated with the teachings of John Calvin. It is characterized by its strong emphasis on the absolute sovereignty of God, the total depravity of humanity, and the doctrine of predestination—the belief that God has unconditionally elected certain individuals for salvation and reprobated others to damnation. Calvinism also rigorously adheres to a penal substitutionary model of the atonement, where Christ bears the exact retributive wrath of God the Father intended for the elect.

From the perspective of Orthodox theology, as often discussed on the *Lord of Spirits*, Calvinism represents a significant departure from the early Christian and Ancient Near Eastern worldview. Orthodoxy critiques Calvinism's rigid legalism and determinism as philosophically indebted to later medieval scholasticism and nominalism, rather than the patristic tradition. By emphasizing penal substitution over *Christus Victor* and minimizing the participatory reality of theosis (becoming by grace what God is by nature), Calvinism is often viewed as stripping away the dynamic, cosmic reality of spiritual warfare and human cooperation (synergy) with divine grace.
