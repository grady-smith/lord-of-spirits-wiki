---

Passover (Hebrew: *Pesach*) is the foundational Jewish festival commemorating the liberation of the Israelites from slavery in Egypt. It centers on the night the Angel of the Lord struck down the firstborn of Egypt, "passing over" the Israelite homes that were marked with the blood of the sacrificial lamb.

Within the *Lord of Spirits* paradigm, the Exodus event is fundamentally a narrative of cosmic spiritual warfare. The text explicitly states that God was executing judgments against "all the gods of Egypt" (Exodus 12:12). The Passover was not just a geopolitical liberation; it was the decisive defeat of the demonic entities that animated the Egyptian empire. The lamb's blood served as a ritual marker of allegiance to Yahweh, creating a boundary that the forces of death could not cross.

For Orthodox Christians, the Old Testament Passover is the direct typological precursor to the crucifixion and resurrection of Christ (Pascha). Jesus is the ultimate Passover Lamb whose blood protects the faithful from eternal death and liberates them from slavery to sin and the devil. The Eucharist is the fulfillment of the Passover meal, a sacrificial banquet that binds believers to the New Covenant.
