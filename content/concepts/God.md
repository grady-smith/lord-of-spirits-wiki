---
title: "God"
tags: [concept]
---

In Orthodox Christian theology, God is the uncreated, eternal, and infinite source of all existence. He is a Trinity of three distinct Persons (Hypostases)—the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit—sharing one indivisible divine essence (Ousia). God is fundamentally incomprehensible in His essence, yet perfectly reveals Himself and interacts with creation through His uncreated energies. He is the ultimate reality, entirely distinct from the created cosmos, yet intimately present and sustaining it at every moment.

In the ancient Near Eastern context and the worldview explored in the *Lord of Spirits*, "God" (Yahweh/Elohim) is the Most High God, the creator and sovereign ruler of the Divine Council. Unlike the gods of pagan pantheons who are part of the cosmos and subject to its forces, the God of the Bible is utterly transcendent. The biblical narrative is the story of this Creator God reclaiming His rebellious creation, defeating hostile spiritual powers, and uniting heaven and earth through the Incarnation of Jesus Christ.
