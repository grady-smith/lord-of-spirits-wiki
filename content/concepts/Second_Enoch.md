Second Enoch, also known as the Slavonic Apocalypse of Enoch, is an apocalyptic text from the late Second Temple period or early first century CE. It survives in its entirety only in Old Church Slavonic manuscripts, though it is believed to have been originally composed in Greek or Hebrew/Aramaic. The text expands upon the traditions surrounding the biblical figure of Enoch, detailing his ascent through multiple heavens, his transformation into an angelic being, and his receipt of divine revelations concerning creation, history, and the end of the world.

In the context of the *Lord of Spirits* podcast, Second Enoch is crucial for understanding the cosmological and mystical worldview of Second Temple Judaism, which deeply informed early Christianity. The text provides a detailed geography of the unseen realm, describing the various hierarchies of angels, the prisons of the fallen Watchers, and the nature of Paradise and Hell. It reflects the ancient belief in a porous boundary between the earthly and heavenly realms, emphasizing the ongoing interaction between human and angelic beings.

While not considered canonical Scripture by the Orthodox Church, Second Enoch is highly valued for its historical and theological context. It illustrates the robust Jewish tradition of heavenly ascent and visionary experience that forms the background for New Testament accounts, such as the Apostle Paul's ascent to the "third heaven" (2 Corinthians 12) and the Book of Revelation. Its depiction of Enoch's transformation also foreshadows the Christian doctrine of *theosis*, the deification of humanity.
