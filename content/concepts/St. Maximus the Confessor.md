---
title: "St. Maximus the Confessor"
tags: [concept]
---

St. Maximus the Confessor (c. 580–662) is one of the most profound theological minds in Eastern Orthodoxy, best known for his defense of dyothelitism—the doctrine that Jesus Christ possessed both a human and a divine will. He argued that if Christ did not possess a complete human will, He did not fully assume human nature, and therefore humanity could not be fully saved. His cosmic vision of salvation emphasizes that humanity was created as a microcosm of the universe, intended to unite the material and spiritual realms and offer them back to God.

In discussions related to the *Lord of Spirits*, St. Maximus is often invoked for his understanding of the *logoi*—the divine intentions or words embedded in all created things. According to Maximus, every aspect of creation participates in God through its unique *logos*, which ultimately connects to the supreme Logos, Jesus Christ. This framework provides a deep metaphysical backing for how the spiritual realm interacts with the physical, maintaining that the universe is saturated with divine purpose and spiritual presence rather than being a closed, mechanistic system.
