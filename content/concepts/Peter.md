---
title: "Peter"
tags: [concept]
---

Peter, originally named Simon, was a Galilean fisherman who became the prominent leader among the twelve Apostles of Jesus Christ. Known for his impulsive faith, his confession of Jesus as the Messiah, and his subsequent denial during the Passion, Peter was later restored by the resurrected Christ and tasked with shepherding the early Church. He is traditionally recognized as the first Bishop of Antioch and later Rome, where he was martyred.

In the theological landscape of the *Lord of Spirits*, Peter’s role extends beyond early church governance into cosmic, apocalyptic significance. His confession at Caesarea Philippi—situated at the foot of Mount Hermon, the traditional site of the Watchers' descent—serves as the definitive declaration of Christ's divine identity and the declaration of war against the gates of Hades. The authority granted to Peter and the Apostles to "bind and loose" is understood within the context of the divine council, delegating to the Apostles the administrative authority once held by the fallen angelic sons of God.
