title: "Church Fathers"

---

The "Church Fathers" (or Patristics) are the influential theologians, bishops, and apologists of the early Christian Church (roughly spanning the 1st to 8th centuries) who established the foundational doctrinal and liturgical frameworks of Christianity. Key figures include St. Ignatius of Antioch, St. Irenaeus of Lyons, St. Athanasius, the Cappadocian Fathers, and St. John of Damascus. In Orthodox theology, the consensus of the Fathers (*consensus patrum*) is not merely a historical curiosity but the living, authoritative voice of the Holy Spirit guiding the Church.

The *Lord of Spirits* frequently relies on the Church Fathers to demonstrate that the early Christian worldview was vastly different from modern, post-Enlightenment assumptions. The Fathers uniformly read the scriptures through the lens of spiritual warfare, interpreting pagan gods as real demons, understanding the atonement primarily as a victory over death (Christus Victor), and viewing the sacraments as physical encounters with divine energy. Returning to the Fathers is, therefore, a reclamation of the ancient, cosmic context of the Bible, stripping away centuries of medieval scholastic and modern rationalist overlays.
