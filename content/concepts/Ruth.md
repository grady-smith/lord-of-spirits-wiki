---
title: "Ruth"
tags: [concept]
---

The Book of Ruth is a narrative in the Hebrew Bible that tells the story of a Moabite woman who binds herself to the Israelite people and their God following the death of her husband. In the context of ancient Near Eastern culture, Ruth's decision to leave her homeland and follow her mother-in-law, Naomi, represents a radical shift in allegiance. Moabites were traditionally enemies of Israel, and Ruth's declaration, "Your people shall be my people, and your God my God," functions as a profound act of covenantal faithfulness and conversion.

In Orthodox Christian tradition, Ruth is celebrated as an archetype of the Gentile inclusion into the people of God. Her faithfulness and virtue lead to her marriage to Boaz, making her the great-grandmother of King David and an ancestor of Jesus Christ. This lineage underscores the biblical theme that God's plan of salvation was never meant to be confined to a single ethnic group, but rather to encompass all nations, fulfilling the promise made to Abraham.

The story of Ruth also emphasizes the concept of *hesed* (loving-kindness or covenantal loyalty) and the role of the kinsman-redeemer (go'el). Boaz's redemption of Ruth and Naomi's property prefigures the ultimate redemption brought by Jesus Christ, who takes on human flesh to redeem humanity from bondage to sin and death, bringing them into the household of God.
