---

The Epistle to the Romans is the Apostle Paul’s longest and most theologically dense letter, written to the Christian community in the imperial capital. It systematically addresses the universality of sin for both Jews and Gentiles, the mechanism of salvation through faithfulness in Jesus Christ, the role of the Torah, and God's sovereign plan for the nations.

In the context of the *Lord of Spirits*, Romans is often reclaimed from later forensic and legalistic interpretations (common in Protestantism) and re-read through the lens of ancient cosmic warfare. The "wrath of God" discussed by Paul is understood not as an emotional anger demanding punishment, but as God handing humanity over to the very corrupt spiritual powers they chose to worship. Justification in Romans is therefore the deliverance from these tyrannical powers, the destruction of the dominion of Death, and the integration of believers into the family of God through the faithfulness of the Messiah.
