---
title: "Shepherd of Hermas"
tags: [concept]
---

The *Shepherd of Hermas* is an early Christian text, composed in Rome during the first or second century CE. It was highly esteemed by many early Church Fathers—including Irenaeus and Origen—and was often read alongside canonical Scripture in early Christian communities, being included in some early biblical manuscripts like the Codex Sinaiticus. The text is structured around a series of visions, mandates (commandments), and similitudes (parables) given to a former slave named Hermas by an angelic figure appearing as a shepherd.

In the context of the *Lord of Spirits* and early Christian theology, the *Shepherd of Hermas* is significant for its deep continuity with Second Temple Jewish apocalyptic literature. It vividly portrays the unseen realm, detailing the presence of angels of repentance, guardian angels, and evil spirits that seek to influence human behavior. The text heavily emphasizes moral purity, the necessity of repentance after baptism, and the communal nature of salvation, depicting the Church as a great tower currently under construction.

From an Orthodox perspective, while the text did not ultimately become part of the canonical New Testament, it remains a valuable witness to the piety and worldview of the apostolic and sub-apostolic Church. It underscores the reality of spiritual warfare and the belief that the material world and human actions are deeply intertwined with the cosmic struggle between angelic forces of light and demonic powers of darkness.
