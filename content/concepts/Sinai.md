---
title: "Sinai"
tags: [concept]
---

Mount Sinai (also known as Horeb) is the sacred mountain in the Sinai Peninsula where, according to the Book of Exodus, God revealed Himself to Moses and established His covenant with the Israelites following their deliverance from Egypt. The events at Sinai represent one of the most critical theophanies (manifestations of God) in the Hebrew Bible. It was here that God delivered the Torah, including the Ten Commandments, amidst terrifying cosmic phenomena: fire, smoke, earthquakes, and the sound of a trumpet.

In the ancient Near Eastern context and the *Lord of Spirits* framework, Sinai functions as the cosmic mountain, the intersection of heaven and earth where the Divine Council meets. The giving of the Law was not merely the delivery of a legal code, but the establishment of Israel as God's vassal kingdom. God was making His dwelling among them, and the Torah provided the necessary instructions for maintaining ritual purity so that a holy God could dwell amidst a sinful people without destroying them. 

For Orthodox theology, Sinai is deeply typological. The ascent of Moses into the dark cloud to commune with God is a primary image of mystical theology and *apophatic* (negative) theology, heavily utilized by Church Fathers like St. Gregory of Nyssa. Furthermore, Sinai is understood as the birthplace of the Old Covenant Church, prefiguring Pentecost, where the fire of the Holy Spirit descended on Mount Zion to establish the New Covenant Church, writing the law not on stone tablets, but on the human heart.
