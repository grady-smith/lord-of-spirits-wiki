---
title: "Jehovah"
tags: [concept]
---
"Jehovah" is a relatively modern, anglicized vocalization of the Tetragrammaton (YHWH), the sacred, personal name of God revealed to Moses at the burning bush. The word is actually a linguistic hybrid: it takes the consonants of YHWH and combines them with the vowels of the Hebrew word *Adonai* (Lord). 

In Second Temple Judaism, out of profound reverence and a desire not to take God's name in vain, the Jewish people ceased pronouncing the Divine Name aloud. When reading the scriptures, they would substitute *Adonai* wherever YHWH appeared. Medieval Christian scholars, not realizing this scribal practice, combined the consonants of the Name with the vowels of the substitute, resulting in the artificial pronunciation "Jehovah," which gained popularity in English translations like the King James Version.

In Orthodox theology and the *Lord of Spirits* podcast, the accurate name of Israel's God is understood to be Yahweh. However, the Orthodox Church continues the ancient tradition of reverence, generally translating the Name in scriptures and liturgy as "The Lord" (Greek: *Kyrios*). The ultimate revelation of the Divine Name, however, is found in the person of Jesus Christ, who stated, "I and the Father are one," and applied the divine "I AM" to Himself.
