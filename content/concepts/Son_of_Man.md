---
title: "Son of Man"
tags: [concept]
---

"Son of Man" was the primary title Jesus used to refer to Himself in the Gospels. In Hebrew and Aramaic (*ben adam* or *bar enash*), the phrase generally means "a human being" or "a mortal." However, the title takes on a profound, apocalyptic significance due to its usage in the Book of Daniel (Daniel 7:13-14), where a divine figure "like a son of man" comes riding on the clouds of heaven to receive an eternal, universal kingdom from the "Ancient of Days."

In the context of the *Lord of Spirits* and Second Temple Judaism, the "Son of Man" was understood as a pre-existent, divine, and messianic figure. Texts like the Book of Parables (1 Enoch) expand on the Danielic vision, describing the Son of Man as the one who was hidden with God before creation and who will judge both humans and rebellious angels at the end of the age. When Jesus uses this title, He is making an explicit, unmistakable claim to divine authority, cosmic judgment, and pre-existence, which is precisely why it provoked accusations of blasphemy from the religious leaders.

For Orthodox theology, the title perfectly encapsulates the hypostatic union—the two natures of Christ. He is truly a human being (a son of man), representing humanity and entering into death on our behalf. Simultaneously, He is the divine, eschatological judge riding on the clouds, sharing the throne of the Father. His use of the title constantly points to the mystery of the Incarnation: that the God who rules the cosmos has chosen to do so as a human being.
