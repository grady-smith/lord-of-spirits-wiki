---
title: "Iconography"
tags: [concept]
---
Iconography is the established tradition, discipline, and theological language of writing (painting) sacred images in the Orthodox Church. It is guided by strict stylistic and canonical rules that prioritize spiritual reality over naturalistic realism. Iconography uses reverse perspective, stylized proportions, and specific color symbolism to convey theological truths rather than earthly emotions or physical exactness.

In the ancient Near Eastern and biblical worldview, visual representation was never merely decorative; it was functional and representative of presence. Orthodox iconography continues this by ensuring that images align perfectly with the Church's dogma and scriptural witness. The iconographer does not paint from their own imagination but acts as a transmitter of Holy Tradition, similar to how a priest proclaims the Gospel.

The *Lord of Spirits* often highlights how iconography functions as an anti-idolatrous mechanism. By establishing exactly how Christ, the angels, and the saints are to be depicted—bathed in the uncreated light of God and devoid of fleshly passions—iconography guards the Church against the infiltration of pagan or demonic visual concepts, ensuring that the physical environment of worship correctly shapes the spiritual perception of the faithful.
