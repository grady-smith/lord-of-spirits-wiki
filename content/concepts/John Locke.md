title: "John Locke"

John Locke (1632–1704) was a profoundly influential English philosopher and physician, widely regarded as one of the primary architects of the Enlightenment and classical liberalism. His theories regarding the "blank slate" (tabula rasa) of the human mind, empiricism, religious tolerance, and the social contract—specifically the idea that government derives its legitimacy from the consent of the governed—deeply shaped Western political thought, including the American Revolution.

In the context of the *Lord of Spirits* and Orthodox critique of modernity, Locke represents a pivotal shift toward a secular, mechanistic worldview. Locke's philosophy functionally removed God from the active governance of the cosmos, reducing religion to a private, intellectual belief system rather than a communal, sacramental reality. By emphasizing empirical observation as the sole basis of knowledge, his framework paved the way for the rejection of the unseen realm, angels, demons, and the spiritual dimensions of creation.

Orthodoxy views this Enlightenment trajectory as a form of spiritual blindness. While Locke's political ideas provided certain earthly freedoms, his philosophical assumptions severed the material world from the divine. The traditional Christian worldview, in contrast, sees the cosmos as deeply enchanted and sacramental, where spiritual forces are constantly active, and where human rights and dignity are rooted not in abstract natural law, but in humanity being created in the Image of God.
