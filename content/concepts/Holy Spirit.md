---
title: "Holy Spirit"
tags: [concept]
---
The Holy Spirit is the third Person of the Holy Trinity, co-equal and co-eternal with the Father and the Son. In Orthodox theology, the Spirit is the "Lord, the Giver of Life," who proceeds from the Father, as articulated in the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed. The Spirit is the active, animating presence of God in creation, described in Genesis as "hovering over the face of the waters" to bring order out of chaos.

In the context of the *Lord of Spirits* and ancient Israelite theology, the Holy Spirit is the divine presence that inspired the prophets, empowered the judges and kings, and filled the Temple. The Spirit is not an impersonal force but a divine person who shares intimately in the Divine Council. The descent of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost marks the reversal of the Tower of Babel; instead of confusing languages to divide rebellious humanity, the Spirit unites humanity across languages into the one Body of Christ.

Within the life of the Orthodox Church, the Holy Spirit is the agent of sanctification and the means by which humanity experiences *theosis* (deification). Through sacraments like Chrismation, believers receive the indwelling of the Spirit, equipping them to participate in the spiritual warfare against fallen powers and to bear the fruits of divine life in the world.
