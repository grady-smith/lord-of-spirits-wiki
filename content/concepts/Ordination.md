---
title: "Ordination"
tags: [concept]
---

Ordination is the sacramental act by which a man is set apart and consecrated by the laying on of hands of a bishop to serve as a bishop, priest, or deacon in the Church. It is the mechanism of apostolic succession, ensuring that the clergy operate not on their own authority, but in continuity with the authority Christ originally granted to His Apostles.

In the *Lord of Spirits* and Orthodox theology, ordination is deeply tied to the concepts of the Old Testament priesthood and the Divine Council. The clergy are not simply administrative leaders; they are the liturgical stand-ins for Christ, leading the local assembly in joining the heavenly worship of the angels. A priest offers the Eucharistic sacrifice on earth just as Christ, the ultimate High Priest, offers Himself continually in the heavenly tabernacle.

Furthermore, ordination is an active commissioning for spiritual warfare. Just as the seventy elders in the Old Testament were granted a portion of the Spirit that was on Moses to help govern Israel, ordained clergy receive the grace of the Holy Spirit to minister the sacraments, forgive sins, and shepherd the flock, actively opposing the demonic forces that seek to scatter the Church.
