---
title: "High priest"
tags: [concept]
---
In ancient Israel, the High Priest was the chief religious official, the direct successor to Aaron, and the only individual permitted to enter the Holy of Holies. He served as the primary mediator between God and the nation, bearing the names of the tribes on his breastplate and executing the crucial rites of the Day of Atonement (Yom Kippur) to cleanse the sanctuary and the people from sin and impurity.

From an Orthodox theological perspective, particularly emphasized in the Epistle to the Hebrews, the earthly High Priest and the Levitical sacrificial system were types and shadows pointing to a greater, cosmic reality. The High Priest's entry into the earthly tabernacle prefigured the ascension of the true High Priest into the heavenly realms. 

The New Testament identifies Jesus Christ as the ultimate, eternal High Priest. Unlike the Levitical priests who had to offer sacrifices repeatedly for their own sins, Christ offered Himself once for all—both the priest and the sacrifice. In Orthodox ecclesiology, the bishop (and by extension, the presbyter/priest) does not possess an independent priesthood but participates sacramentally in the one, unique high priesthood of Christ, mediating God's grace to the Church through the Divine Liturgy.
