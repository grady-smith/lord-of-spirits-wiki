---
title: "Philo"
tags: [concept]
---

Philo of Alexandria was a prominent Hellenistic Jewish philosopher who lived during the first century AD, roughly contemporaneous with Jesus Christ and the Apostles. He is best known for his extensive efforts to synthesize Jewish scriptural traditions with Greek philosophy, particularly Platonism and Stoicism, through the heavy use of allegorical interpretation of the Hebrew Bible.

In the context of Orthodox theology and the *Lord of Spirits*, Philo is a crucial figure for understanding the theological vocabulary of Second Temple Judaism, especially the concept of the *Logos*. Long before the Gospel of John was written, Philo discussed the *Logos* as the divine Word or Reason that acts as an intermediary between the transcendent God and the material creation. While Philo's conception of the *Logos* remained somewhat abstract and philosophical, it provided the cultural and linguistic foundation for the Christian proclamation that the *Logos* is a distinct, uncreated divine person who became incarnate in Jesus Christ.
