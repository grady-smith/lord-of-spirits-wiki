---
title: "Holy Matrimony"
tags: [concept]
---
Holy Matrimony in the Orthodox Church is a sacrament (or mystery) in which a man and a woman are united by God, transforming a natural human relationship into an eternal, spiritual union. Unlike legal contracts, Orthodox marriage is crowned with martyrdom—the crowns placed on the couple's heads symbolize their calling to die to themselves, sacrificing for one another to achieve mutual salvation and sanctification.

In the broader scriptural and ancient Near Eastern context, marriage is the ultimate symbol of the covenantal relationship between God and His people. The Prophets frequently described Israel's apostasy and idolatry as "spiritual adultery," emphasizing that worship of false gods was a betrayal of the exclusive marital bond with Yahweh. 

In the New Testament, this imagery is fulfilled in Christ and the Church. The Apostle Paul in Ephesians 5 explicitly links the mystery of human marriage to the union between Christ and His Bride, the Church. Thus, Holy Matrimony is not merely a social institution but a living icon of the Kingdom of God, meant to reflect the selfless love, unity, and life-giving communion of the Holy Trinity.
