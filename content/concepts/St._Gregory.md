---
title: "St. Gregory"
tags: [concept]
---

"St. Gregory" is a name referring to several highly influential Fathers in the Orthodox Church, most notably St. Gregory of Nyssa, St. Gregory the Theologian (Nazianzen), and St. Gregory Palamas. In the context of early Church history and the Cappadocian Fathers, it typically refers to either Gregory of Nyssa or Gregory Nazianzen. Both were critical in defending Nicene Orthodoxy and articulating the theology of the Trinity during the 4th century.

When referenced generally in contexts like the *Lord of Spirits*, St. Gregory often evokes the profound mystical and apophatic theology characteristic of the Eastern tradition. These Fathers emphasized that God in His essence is utterly incomprehensible and beyond all human concepts or language. True knowledge of God is not found in intellectual mastery, but in direct, experiential encounter and purification of the heart.

Whether it is Gregory of Nyssa's writings on the soul's infinite ascent toward God (*epektasis*) or Gregory the Theologian's precise articulation of the Trinity in his *Five Theological Orations*, the "Gregories" represent the synthesis of profound intellectual rigor with deep ascetic and mystical experience. They established that true theology is inherently doxological—oriented toward worship and the transformation of the human person into the likeness of Christ.
