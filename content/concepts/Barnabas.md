---
title: "Barnabas"
tags: [concept]
---

Barnabas, originally named Joseph and distinguished as a Levite from Cyprus, was a prominent early Christian leader and apostle, best known for his close association with St. Paul. The name Barnabas, meaning "Son of Encouragement" or "Son of Consolation," was given to him by the apostles in Jerusalem. He played a crucial transitional role in the early Church, not only by generously selling his property to support the community but, more importantly, by vouching for the newly converted Paul when the other disciples were still fearful of their former persecutor.

Within the context of early Christian missions and the cosmic struggle depicted in Orthodox theology, Barnabas and Paul’s missionary journeys represented a direct assault on the territories governed by fallen spiritual powers. As they traveled through Hellenistic cities, they were reclaiming the nations for the Most High God, fulfilling the Deuteronomy 32 worldview where the displaced nations are brought back into the fold of the true Creator. Barnabas’s legacy is that of a key unifier and bridge-builder, instrumental in the Church's expansion beyond its Jewish roots into the Gentile world.
