---
title: "Herod"
tags: [concept]
---
The Herodian dynasty, beginning with Herod the Great, ruled Judea as client kings for the Roman Empire during the 1st century BC and 1st century AD. Herod the Great was an Idumean (Edomite) whose claim to the throne was backed by Rome rather than Davidic lineage, making his rule a source of deep resentment among the Jewish populace.

In the context of Second Temple Judaism, Herod was a deeply controversial figure. He was a master builder, responsible for the massive expansion of the Second Temple in Jerusalem, an effort to win favor with his Jewish subjects. However, he was also notoriously paranoid and brutal, engaging in heavy taxation, assassinating political rivals and family members, and blending Jewish religion with Roman paganism and emperor worship.

In the New Testament and Orthodox theology, the Herods represent the ultimate corruption of earthly power allied with demonic, worldly systems. Herod the Great’s slaughter of the innocents in Bethlehem was an attempt to destroy the true Davidic King, echoing Pharaoh's actions in Egypt. Similarly, his descendants, such as Herod Antipas (who executed John the Baptist), consistently acted as agents of the world's rebellion against God's Kingdom, standing in stark contrast to the self-emptying, sacrificial kingship of Jesus Christ.
