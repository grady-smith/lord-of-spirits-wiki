---
title: "Second Temple"
tags: [concept]
---

The Second Temple refers to the Jewish holy temple that stood on the Temple Mount in Jerusalem between c. 516 BCE and 70 CE. It replaced the First Temple (Solomon's Temple), which was destroyed by the Babylonians in 586 BCE. The construction of the Second Temple was initiated by Jewish exiles returning from Babylon under the Persian king Cyrus the Great. It was later massively expanded and renovated by King Herod the Great in the first century BCE, transforming it into one of the most magnificent structures of the ancient world.

In the ancient Near Eastern worldview, a temple was not merely a place of worship; it was the literal dwelling place of the deity's presence on earth, the intersection between heaven and earth. The *Lord of Spirits* podcast emphasizes that the Second Temple was the focal point of Jewish identity, sacrifice, and communion with God. However, Jewish tradition also noted that certain elements of the First Temple, such as the Ark of the Covenant and the visible glory of God (Shekinah), were absent from the Second Temple, creating a persistent eschatological expectation for God's dramatic return.

For Orthodox Christianity, the Second Temple is the crucial historical and theological setting of the New Testament. Jesus Christ’s life, ministry, and passion are intimately connected to the Temple. Christ ultimately identifies Himself as the true Temple (John 2:21)—the ultimate locus of God's presence and the place of final sacrifice. The destruction of the Second Temple by the Romans in 70 CE marked a decisive shift, fulfilling Christ's prophecies and definitively transitioning the locus of worship to the Christian Eucharist and the Church as the Body of Christ.
