---
title: "St. John"
tags: [concept]
---

Saint John the Apostle, also known as John the Evangelist or John the Theologian, was one of the Twelve Apostles of Jesus, traditionally identified as the "disciple whom Jesus loved." He is credited with authoring the Gospel of John, three epistles (1, 2, and 3 John), and the Book of Revelation. Within the Orthodox Church, he is one of only three saints to hold the specific title of "Theologian," recognizing the profound spiritual depth and exalted Christology of his writings.

In the context of the *Lord of Spirits*, St. John's writings are foundational for bridging the Jewish Second Temple worldview with Christian dogma. His Gospel famously begins by identifying Jesus as the pre-existent *Logos* (Word), a concept deeply rooted in both Jewish Wisdom literature and Greek philosophy, who became flesh. John's theology emphasizes the cosmic battle between light and darkness, the necessity of the sacramental life (particularly baptism and the Eucharist), and the reality of *theosis* (becoming children of God).

Furthermore, St. John's Book of Revelation provides the ultimate apocalyptic vision of the ancient Near Eastern divine council motif. It depicts Christ, the slain Lamb, ascending to the throne of God to execute judgment upon the fallen spiritual powers (dragons and beasts) and to establish the New Jerusalem. John's vision confirms the ultimate victory of God over death and chaos, fulfilling the ancient promises made to Israel and the patriarchs.
