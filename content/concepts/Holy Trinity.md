---
title: "Holy Trinity"
tags: [concept]
---
The Holy Trinity is the central mystery of the Christian faith: the revelation that God is one in essence (ousia) and three in distinct persons (hypostases)—the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. In Orthodox theology, the Trinity is a communion of perfect, self-giving love. The Father is the unoriginate source, the Son is eternally begotten of the Father, and the Spirit eternally proceeds from the Father.

The *Lord of Spirits* podcast heavily emphasizes that the Trinity is not a late philosophical invention, but is rooted deeply in the Hebrew Bible. The ancient Israelites understood a multiplicity within Yahweh—evidenced by the "Two Powers in Heaven" theology of Second Temple Judaism, where the invisible Yahweh and the visible Angel of Yahweh (the pre-incarnate Christ) were both worshipped as God. The New Testament writers simply identified these existing divine persons as the Father, Jesus Christ, and the Holy Spirit.

Understanding God as Trinity is essential because it means that God is fundamentally relational. Because God is love in Himself, creation was not an act of necessity or loneliness, but an overflow of that divine communion. Human salvation (*theosis*) is ultimately an invitation to be drawn into the eternal, loving life of the Holy Trinity.
