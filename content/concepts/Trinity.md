The Holy Trinity is the central, foundational mystery of the Christian faith: that God is one in essence (ousia) but three in distinct persons (hypostases)—Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. This doctrine was fiercely defended and articulated during the early Ecumenical Councils, protecting the truth that the Son and the Spirit are co-eternal and co-equal with the Father. The Trinity reveals that God is not a solitary monad but an eternal communion of self-emptying love.

In the *Lord of Spirits* podcast, the Trinity is often discussed in its ancient Near Eastern and Second Temple context. Rather than being a late Hellenistic philosophical invention, the podcast demonstrates how the concept of the Trinity is deeply rooted in the Old Testament Scriptures, specifically through the "Two Powers in Heaven" tradition—where Yahweh is seen both as the transcendent, invisible God and the visible, interacting "Word" or "Angel of the Lord." Furthermore, the Divine Council of the Old Testament is seen as a created reflection of the uncreated, eternal communion of the Holy Trinity.
