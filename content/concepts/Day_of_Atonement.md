The Day of Atonement (Yom Kippur) was the holiest and most solemn day in the ancient Israelite liturgical calendar, detailed primarily in Leviticus 16. It was the one day of the year when the High Priest would enter the Holy of Holies in the Tabernacle (or Temple) to offer blood sacrifices to cleanse the sanctuary, the priesthood, and the entire congregation from the accumulated pollution of sin and ritual impurity. 

In the context of the *Lord of Spirits* podcast and Orthodox theology, the ritual of the two goats on this day is deeply significant. One goat was sacrificed to Yahweh to purify the sacred space, while the other—the "scapegoat"—had the sins of the people laid upon it and was driven into the wilderness to the demon Azazel. This vividly demonstrates the ancient Near Eastern understanding of sin as a demonic force that must be expelled back to the realm of chaos and death. Christ’s crucifixion fulfills both elements: He is the perfect sacrifice whose blood purifies the cosmos, and He is the one who journeys into the wilderness of death (Hades) to conquer the powers of darkness.
