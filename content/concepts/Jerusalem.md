---
title: "Jerusalem"
tags: [concept]
---
Jerusalem is the central geographic and theological focal point of the biblical narrative. Established as the capital of a united Israel by King David, and made the center of worship by King Solomon's construction of the Temple, Jerusalem (Zion) became the localized throne of Yahweh on earth, the place where the heavenly and earthly realms intersected.

In the ancient Near Eastern worldview, cities were intrinsically linked to their patron deities. Jerusalem was uniquely the "City of the Great King," the cosmic mountain from which God ruled. Its destruction by the Babylonians (and later the Romans) was a catastrophic theological crisis, seemingly indicating the victory of chaotic, demonic powers over God's sanctuary, though the prophets interpreted it as God's necessary judgment on idolatry.

In Orthodox theology, earthly Jerusalem is a type and shadow of the "New Jerusalem" described in Revelation. While the physical city holds immense historical and spiritual significance—being the site of Christ's crucifixion, resurrection, and the outpouring of the Holy Spirit—the ultimate reality is the heavenly city. The Church itself is understood as the New Jerusalem, the bride of Christ, where the presence of God dwells fully among His people through the sacraments.
