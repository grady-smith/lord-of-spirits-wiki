---
title: "DEVIL"
tags: [concept]
---
The word "Devil" comes from the Greek *diabolos*, meaning "slanderer" or "accuser," and is used in the New Testament to translate the Hebrew *satan* (the adversary). In Orthodox theology, the Devil is not an evil equal-and-opposite deity to God (which would be dualism), but rather a high-ranking angelic being who rebelled against God out of pride and a desire to usurp the divine throne. 

Within the ancient worldview presented by *The Lord of Spirits*, the Devil is often identified with the serpentine figure in Eden who introduced sin and death to humanity. He is viewed as the "prince of the power of the air" and the chief of the fallen spiritual entities who demand worship through idolatry and seek the destruction of mankind. The entirety of Christ's incarnational work—His life, death, descent into Hades, and resurrection—is understood primarily as a cosmic rescue mission to defeat the Devil, destroy his kingdom of death, and liberate humanity from his tyranny.
