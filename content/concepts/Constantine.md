Constantine the Great was the first Roman Emperor to convert to Christianity, fundamentally shifting the trajectory of the Church in late antiquity. Within the framework of Orthodox theology, he is venerated as a saint and "Equal-to-the-Apostles," not because of personal sinlessness, but because his reign marked the decisive victory of Christ over the pagan pantheon and the demonic powers that animated the Roman imperial cult. His victory at the Milvian Bridge is seen as the establishment of a Christian Roman Empire (Byzantium) where the state ceased to persecute the Church and instead facilitated its universal mission.

Constantine’s convocation of the First Council of Nicaea in 325 AD was instrumental in preserving orthodox Christology against Arianism. By summoning the bishops from across the empire, he provided the structural means for the Church to unify its conciliar voice and articulate the dogma that Christ is "of one essence" (*homoousios*) with the Father.
