---
title: "Rabbi Akiva"
tags: [concept]
---

Rabbi Akiva ben Joseph was a prominent Jewish sage of the late first and early second centuries AD, considered one of the primary contributors to the Mishnah and a foundational figure in the development of Rabbinic Judaism. He was famously involved in the Bar Kokhba revolt against the Roman Empire (132–136 AD), famously declaring Simon bar Kokhba to be the long-awaited Jewish Messiah, which ended in catastrophic defeat.

In the historical and theological context of the *Lord of Spirits*, Rabbi Akiva marks a critical divergence point between early Christianity and the emerging Rabbinic tradition. In response to the growing Christian movement—which utilized the Greek Septuagint and its complex multi-personal understanding of God—Akiva and his contemporaries championed a more strict, singular monotheism and commissioned new Greek translations of the Hebrew scriptures that minimized the ancient "Two Powers in Heaven" theology. This conscious theological pivot helped solidify the boundaries between the synagogue and the Church.
