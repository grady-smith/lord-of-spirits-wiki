---
title: "Demons"
tags: [concept]
---
In the theological framework of the ancient Near East and Second Temple Judaism—a perspective robustly defended in Orthodox theology—demons are not merely psychological projections or abstract concepts of evil, but real, personal, and rebellious spiritual entities. Unlike the higher-tier fallen angels (such as the rebellious Divine Council members who rule the nations), demons are frequently understood in ancient literature, such as 1 Enoch, as the disembodied spirits of the dead Nephilim (the giant offspring of the Watchers and human women).

In *The Lord of Spirits* podcast, demons are characterized by their insatiable craving for bodies and worship, which they seek to obtain through idolatry, blood sacrifice, and the possession of human beings. Their ultimate goal is the degradation and destruction of humanity, the image-bearers of God. Christ’s earthly ministry, characterized by widespread exorcisms, was a direct assault on the kingdom of demons, demonstrating Yahweh's reclamation of the earth and the subjugation of the chaotic spiritual forces that had enslaved mankind.
