---
title: "Death"
tags: [concept]
---
In modern secular thought, death is often viewed simply as the biological cessation of life. However, in ancient Near Eastern and Orthodox theology, Death (often personified as Mot in Canaanite religion, or Thanatos in Greek) is understood as a hostile, cosmic power, a spiritual force of corruption and entropy that invades God's good creation. It is the ultimate consequence of humanity's sin and alienation from God, the Source of Life.

According to the *Lord of Spirits* paradigm, Death is not something God created; it is an intruder introduced through the deceit of the Devil. Death is intrinsically linked to Hades (the Underworld), serving as a prison where humanity was held captive by fallen spiritual powers. The central triumph of the Christian Gospel is that Jesus Christ, being the Author of Life, willingly submitted to Death on the Cross in order to enter its domain. By doing so, He destroyed Death from the inside out, shattering the gates of Hades, and liberating humanity, making resurrection and eternal life possible.
