---
title: "Hasmoneans"
tags: [concept]
---
The Hasmoneans were a ruling dynasty of Judea and surrounding regions during classical antiquity, coming to power following the successful Maccabean Revolt against the Seleucid Empire in the 2nd century BC. They are significant in Second Temple Judaism for establishing an independent Jewish state and reclaiming the Temple in Jerusalem, an event commemorated by Hanukkah.

However, from an Orthodox and scriptural perspective, the Hasmonean dynasty was deeply problematic. They illegitimately combined the offices of king and high priest, a usurpation of both the Davidic royal line and the Zadokite priestly line. This sparked profound opposition from various Jewish groups, likely contributing to the formation of the Essene community at Qumran, who rejected the Hasmonean priesthood as corrupt and apostate.

Their reign, characterized by Hellenistic assimilation, political intrigue, and eventual civil war, created the fractured socio-religious environment of the 1st century AD. This context is crucial for understanding the expectations of the Messiah—whom the New Testament identifies as Jesus Christ—as the true, eternal King and High Priest who resolves the failures of the Hasmonean era.
