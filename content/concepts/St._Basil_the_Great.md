---
title: "St. Basil the Great"
tags: [concept]
---

Saint Basil the Great (330–379) is the formal title for St. Basil, bishop of Caesarea, one of the most influential theologians and leaders in the early Church. He is honored as a "Great" Hierarch due to his multifaceted contributions to Christian dogma, monasticism, liturgy, and social justice. As a leading Cappadocian Father, he played a crucial role in the triumph of Nicene Orthodoxy over the Arian heresy in the 4th century.

A central aspect of St. Basil's legacy, frequently relevant to the theology discussed on the *Lord of Spirits*, is his articulation of the distinction between the essence (*ousia*) and the persons (*hypostases*) of the Trinity. He provided the necessary philosophical vocabulary to express how God is simultaneously one in nature and three distinct persons. Furthermore, his defense of the divinity of the Holy Spirit in his treatise *On the Holy Spirit* was definitive for the Church's pneumatology, securing the belief that the Spirit is uncreated and fully God.

St. Basil the Great is also renowned for his liturgical and pastoral contributions. The *Divine Liturgy of St. Basil the Great*, an extended and profoundly theological Eucharistic service, is celebrated in the Orthodox Church ten times a year. His homilies, particularly the *Hexaemeron* (on the six days of creation), profoundly shaped the Christian understanding of cosmology, asserting God's absolute sovereignty over creation and refuting pagan materialism and dualism.
