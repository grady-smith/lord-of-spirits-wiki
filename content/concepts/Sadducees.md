---
title: "Sadducees"
tags: [concept]
---

The Sadducees were a prominent Jewish sect during the Second Temple period, primarily composed of the priestly aristocracy and the wealthy elite in Jerusalem. They held significant power, often controlling the high priesthood and dominating the Sanhedrin. Unlike the Pharisees, the Sadducees accepted only the written Torah (the first five books of Moses) as authoritative, rejecting the oral traditions and the later prophetic and wisdom literature that the Pharisees embraced.

In the context of the *Lord of Spirits* and ancient Judaism, the Sadducees' theological positions were distinct. They famously denied the existence of the resurrection of the dead, angels, and spirits (as noted in Acts 23:8). Their worldview was heavily focused on the present life, the maintenance of the Temple cult, and navigating the political realities of Roman occupation. They were often seen as accommodationists, willing to compromise with Hellenistic culture and Roman authority to preserve their status and the Temple's operation.

From an Orthodox perspective, the Sadducees represent a reductionist view of the biblical faith. By rejecting the broader spiritual reality, the afterlife, and the unseen realm, their theology was essentially a materialistic ancient religion. Christ's interactions with the Sadducees often highlighted their profound misunderstanding of the Scriptures and the power of God, particularly regarding the resurrection, which is central to the Christian hope and the ultimate defeat of death.
